# EP-102

## Problem Statement
Let $c>0$ and $h_c(n)$ be such that for any $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ such that there are $\geq cn^2$ lines each containing more than three points, there must be some line containing $h_c(n)$ many points. Estimate $h_c(n)$. Is it true that, for fixed $c>0$, we have $h_c(n)\to \infty$?

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Purdy. It is not even known if $h_c(n)\geq 5$ (see [101]).

It is easy to see that $h_c(n) \ll_c n^{1/2}$, and Erd\H{o}s at one point \cite{Er95} suggested that perhaps a similar lower bound $h_c(n)\gg_c n^{1/2}$ holds. Zach Hunter has pointed out that this is false, even replacing $>3$ points on each line with $>k$ points: consider the set of points in $\{1,\ldots,m\}^d$ where $n\approx m^d$. These intersect any line in $\ll_d n^{1/d}$ points, and have $\gg_d n^2$ many pairs of points each of which determine a line with at least $k$ points. This is a construction in $\mathbb{R}^d$, but a random projection into $\mathbb{R}^2$ preserves the relevant properties.

This construction shows that $h_c(n) \ll n^{1/\log(1/c)}$.

## Local Proof Attempts
Route:
Rechecked incidence-style forcing from many rich lines and compared with high-dimensional grid projection constructions.

Hard point:
Having cn^2 many lines with >3 points does not force a strong per-line concentration under current methods.

Target:
Under the hypothesis of \(c n^2\) lines containing \(>3\) points, prove the
maximum collinearity parameter \(h_c(n)\to\infty\) (even slowly).
Step A: Rich-line multiplicity profile
Quantify distribution of line occupancies and show low \(h_c(n)\) forces very
high overlap between rich lines.
Step B: Incidence rigidity
Prove such overlap implies near-grid/projective-product structure.
Step C: Obstruction analysis
Either derive contradiction with \(c n^2\) rich-line count, or classify true
obstructions and bound their achievable \(c\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a sharp inverse theorem for rich-line incidence matrices in the regime where
many lines have occupancy \(4\) but maximal occupancy is bounded.

Proposition AA1 (Pair counting through rich lines):
If \(L_t\) is number of lines containing exactly \(t\) points of \(P\), then
\[
\sum_{t\ge 4} \binom{t}{2} L_t
\le \binom{n}{2}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Each unordered point pair determines at most one line.
\(\square\)
Proposition AA2 (Immediate occupancy consequence):
If \(h_c(n)\le H\), then
\[
\sum_{t=4}^{H} L_t \le \frac{\binom{n}{2}}{\binom{4}{2}}= \frac{n(n-1)}{12}.
\]
So achieving \(c n^2\) rich lines requires \(c\le 1/12+o(1)\), and any
improvement beyond this threshold must exploit higher occupancies or structure.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
From AA1 and \(\binom{t}{2}\ge 6\) for \(t\ge 4\):
\[
6\sum_{t=4}^{H}L_t\le \binom n2.
\]
\(\square\)
