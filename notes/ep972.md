# EP-972

## Problem Statement
Let $\alpha>1$ be irrational. Are there infinitely many primes $p$ such that $\lfloor p\alpha\rfloor$ is also prime?

## Site Remarks
Vinogradov \cite{Vi48} proved that the sequence $\{p\alpha\}$ is uniformly distributed for every irrational $\alpha$, and hence there are infinitely many primes $p$ of the shape $p=\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor$ for every irrational $\alpha>1$. Indeed, this occurs if and only if\[\frac{p}{\alpha}\leq n<\frac{p+1}{\alpha},\]which is true if and only if $\{p\alpha^{-1}\}>1-\alpha^{-1}$, which happens infinitely often by the uniform distribution of $\{p\alpha^{-1}\}$.

## Site References
- [Vi48] Vinogradov, I. M., On an estimate of trigonometric sums with prime numbers. Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Ser. Mat. (1948), 225--248.

## Local Computation
- deep multi-$\alpha$ finite scan up to $X=60{,}000{,}000$.
- For each selected irrational $\alpha$, computed
$$
N_\alpha(x)=\#\{p\le x:\ p,\lfloor\alpha p\rfloor\ \text{both prime}\}
$$
at large checkpoints.
- Tested 12 irrational choices (including $\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\pi,e,\varphi,\sqrt5$ and others).

- Counts grow substantially for all tested $\alpha$ up to $x=6\times 10^7$.
- Ratios $N_\alpha(x)/\pi(x)$ in this range are stable near about $0.056$-$0.060$ across tested $\alpha$.
- Heuristic normalization against $x/\log^2 x$ is around constant scale in this finite regime, consistent with expected sparse prime-pair behavior.

## Local Proof Attempts
All tested irrational parameters show substantial growth of \(N_\alpha(x)\), which matches the expected sparse prime-pair heuristic. But the open problem is genuinely infinitary: one needs a theorem producing infinitely many primes \(p\) with \(\lfloor\alpha p\rfloor\) also prime for each irrational \(\alpha>1\).
