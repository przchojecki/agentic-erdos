# EP-917

## Problem Statement
Let $k\geq 4$ and $f_k(n)$ be the largest number of edges in a graph on $n$ vertices which has chromatic number $k$ and is critical (i.e. deleting any edge reduces the chromatic number).

Is it true that\[f_k(n) \gg_k n^2?\]Is it true that\[f_6(n)\sim n^2/4?\]More generally, is it true that, for $k\geq 6$,\[f_k(n) \sim \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lfloor k/3\rfloor}\right)n^2?\]

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er93} wrote 'I learned of this definition from Dirac in 1949 and immediately asked whether $f_k(n)=o(n^2)$. To my great surprise Dirac constructed a $6$ critical graph on $n$ vertices with more than $\frac{n^2}{4}$ edges.' In fact Dirac \cite{Di52} proved\[f_6(4n+2) \geq 4n^2+8n+3,\]as witnessed by taking two disjoint copies of $C_{2n+1}$ and adding all edges between them.

Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er69b} observed that Dirac's construction generalises to show that, if $3\mid k$, there are infinitely many values of $n$ (those of the shape $mk/3$ where $m$ is odd) such that\[f_k(n) \geq \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{k/3}\right)n^2 + n.\]Toft \cite{To70} proved that $f_k(n)\gg_k n^2$ for $k\geq 4$.

Constructions of Stiebitz \cite{St87} show that, for $k\geq 6$, there exist infinitely many values of $n$ such that\[f_k(n) \geq \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\lfloor k/3\rfloor+\delta_k}\right)n^2\]where $\delta_k=0$ if $k\equiv 0\pmod{3}$, $\delta_k=1/7$ if $k\equiv 1\pmod{3}$, and $\delta_k\equiv 24/69$ if $k\equiv 2\pmod{3}$, which disproves Erd\H{o}s' conjectured asympotic for $k\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}$.

Stiebitz also proved the general upper bound\[f_k(n) < \mathrm{ex}(n;K_{k-1})\sim \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{k-2}\right)n^2\]for large $n$. Luo, Ma, and Yang \cite{LMY23} have improved this upper bound to\[f_k(n) \leq \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{k-2}-\frac{1}{36(k-1)^2}+o(1)\right)n^2\]See also [944] and [1032].

## Local Computation
- Built standalone exact colorability checker and applied it to Dirac-family graphs
$$
G_t=C_{2t+1}+C_{2t+1}
$$
for $t=2,\dots,12$.
- For each $t$, verified:
 - $G_t$ is not 5-colorable but is 6-colorable,
 - after deleting any single edge, the graph becomes 5-colorable (full edge-by-edge check).

- All tested instances are fully 6-critical in exact checks.
- Edge counts exactly match
$$
|E(G_t)|=4t^2+8t+3,
$$
with density decreasing toward $1/4$ as $t$ grows.
- At $t=12$ ($n=50$), we get $|E|=675$ and edge density $0.27$, consistent with approach to the $n^2/4$ scale from above.

## Local Proof Attempts
The exact verification of the Dirac family confirms that this classical construction really is 6-critical on a substantial tested range and that its density approaches the conjectural \(n^2/4\) scale from above. This gives clean structural evidence for the \(k=6\) case, but it does not settle the general asymptotic problem.
