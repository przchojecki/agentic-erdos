# EP-783

## Problem Statement
Fix some constant $C>0$ and let $N$ be large. Let $A\subseteq \{2,\ldots,N\}$ be such that $(a,b)=1$ for all $a\neq b\in A$ and $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n}\leq C$.

What choice of such an $A$ minimises the number of integers $m\leq N$ not divisible by any $a\in A$?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er73} suggests that, if $p_i$ is the $i$th prime, then choosing $A=\{p_r<\cdots<p_t\}$, where $p_t$ is the largest prime $\leq N$ and $r$ is minimal such that $\sum_{p\in A}\frac{1}{p}\leq C$ 'either gives the extremal sequence (or at least nearly gives the minimum)'.

Chojeckl has proved (https://www.ulam.ai/research/erdos783.pdf) this is the extremal sequence when $C\leq \log 2$.

Hunter in the comments notes that there are cases where this is not the literal extremal sequence, since small improving perturbations are possible. Tao suggests the problem (which is likely what Erd\H{o}s meant) of whether the minimum number of integers in $[1,N]$ not divisible by any $a\in A$ is\[(\rho(e^C)+o(1))N,\]which the construction above shows is achievable.

Hildebrand \cite{Hi87b} has proved that this weak form of the conjecture is true if $A$ is a set of primes (answering a question of Erd\H{o}s and Ruzsa \cite{ErRu80}).

Tao has resolved this (https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/erdos783-3.pdf) question (asymptotically at least), showing that the number of integers not divisible by any $a\in A$ is at least\[(\rho(e^C)+o(1))N,\]where the $o(1)$ term $\to 0$ as $N\to \infty$ with fixed $C$.

See also [784] and [1200].

Here's a GPT-5.2 argument where to put it to upgrade the write-up by Tao (https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/erdos783-3.pdf):
  
  For any \(N\ge 2\) and any cutoff \(y\in[2,N]\), let \(\mathbb P (y;N):=\{p\le N:\ p\ \text{prime},\ p>y\}\). Then an integer \(1\le n\le N\) is not divisible by any element of \(\mathbb{P}(y;N)\) if and only if \(P^{+}(n)\le y\). Hence the prime-tail sieve has the exact finite-\(N\) identity \(\sigma_{N}(\mathbb{P}(y;N))=\Psi(N,y)/N\), where \(\Psi(N,y):=\#\{n\le N:\ P^{+}(n)\le y\}\).
  
  Given a harmonic budget \(C>0\), choose \(y=y(N,C)\) so that \(\sum_{y<p\le N}\frac{1}{p}\le C\) and \(y\) is maximal (or equivalently \(\mathbb{P}(y;N)\) is maximal under the constraint). Then \(\mathbb{P}(y;N)\) is admissible, so the finite-\(N\) minimax value \(\Sigma(N,C):=\inf\{\sigma_{N}(\mathbb{A}):\ \mathbb{A}\subseteq\{2,\dots,N\}\ \text{pairwise coprime},\ \sum_{a\in\mathbb{A}}\frac{1}{a}\le C\}\) satisfies \(\Sigma(N,C)\le \Psi(N,y(N,C))/N\) exactly. Tao’s argument inserts the Dickman approximation \(\Psi(N,N^{1/\mathrm{e}^{C}})\sim N\rho(\mathrm{e}^{C})\) to obtain \(\Sigma(N,C)=\rho(\mathrm{e}^{C})+o(1)\), but this uses only a first-order asymptotic for \(\Psi\).
  
  Saias’ theorem upgrades the evaluation of \(\Psi(N,y)\) to a genuinely finite-\(N\) statement: for fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and in the range \(1\le u:=\frac{\log N}{\log y}\le \exp\{(\log y)^{3/5-\varepsilon}\}\), one has \(\Psi(N,y)=\Lambda(N,y)\bigl(1+O(\exp\{-(\log y)^{3/5-\varepsilon}\})\bigr)\), where \(\Lambda(N,y):=N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}(\lfloor y^{v}\rfloor y^{-v})\). In the Erdős regime \(y=N^{1/w}\exp(\theta/\log N)\) with fixed \(w>1\) and \(|\theta|=O(1)\), one has \(\log y\asymp\log N\) and \(u=w+O(1/\log^{2}N)\), so Saias applies uniformly and yields \(\Psi(N,y)=\Lambda(N,y)+O(N\exp\{-(\log N)^{3/5-\varepsilon}\})\). Therefore \(\sigma_{N}(\mathbb{P}(y;N))=\Lambda(N,y)/N+O(\exp\{-(\log N)^{3/5-\varepsilon}\})\), an error far smaller than any power of \(1/\log N\), and all boundary-layer sensitivity at scale \(N/(\log N)^{2}\) is captured by \(\Lambda\).
  
  This improves Tao’s result on the “upper bound side” from an asymptotic evaluation of the prime-tail competitor to an essentially exact finite-\(N\) evaluation: \(\Sigma(N,C)\le \Lambda(N,y(N,C))/N+O(\exp\{-(\log N)^{3/5-\varepsilon}\})\), and one can optimize \(y\) within the boundary layer \(y=N^{1/w}\exp(\theta/\log N)\) by analyzing \(\Lambda(N,y)\) without worrying about analytic remainder terms. What remains open is the matching “lower bound side” at comparable precision: one would need a finite-\(N\) inequality showing \(\sigma_{N}(\mathbb{A})\) is bounded below by the same \(\Lambda\)-based expression (up to an error smaller than the boundary-layer scale), first for prime moduli and then uniformly through Tao’s reduction from coprime sets to primes.

- 04:50 on 20 Feb 2026 - TerenceTao:
  It turns out that one can use the existing results of Hildebrand (or Granville-Soundararajan) in the prime case to generalize to the arbitrary pairwise coprime case, as suggested in Section 6.1 of Chojecki's GPT 5.2 text; writeup here (https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/erdos783-1.pdf). The simplest case is when all the elements of $A$ are large. Then one can basically just move each element of $A$ (with a small number of exceptions) to the nearest available prime, and an analysis of the inclusion-exclusion / Bonferroni inequalities shows that this doesn't affect the size of the sieved set much (nor does it affect the sum of reciprocals by much either). (There is a simple argument (mapping each element of $A$ to its smallest prime factor) showing that $A$ can't be that much more dense than the primes, so it is relatively easy to move (most of) $A$ to be prime without incurring a large transport cost, thanks to the prime number theorem.)
  
  One then has to deal with small and medium elements of $A$. By a standard pigeonholing argument (the "dyadic pigeonholing" trick of Bourgain (https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06736)) one can assume that the medium elements have negligible size. For small elements one can use a sieve: the traditional thing to do here is invoke the fundamental lemma of sieve theory, but for this problem just the pure Brun sieve suffices actually (because we are happy with a qualitative error term $o(1)$). One can then replace the small elements of $A$ with an equivalent set of small primes that has almost the same Euler product.
  
  The upshot is that the minimum size of the sifted set under the condition $\sum_{n \in A} 1/n \leq C$ and pairwise coprimality is indeed $(\rho(e^C)+o(1)) N$, without requiring the elements of $A$ to be prime, so that the Erdos construction is optimal up to $o(N)$ errors.

  Log-concavity of $\rho$ is established in Hildebrand's "On the Number of Positive Integers <x and Free of Prime Factors >y" (JNT, 1986). The proof of Lemma 1 explicitly establishes that $f(u):=-\rho'(u)/\rho(u) = -(\log \rho(u))'$ is strictly increasing in $(1,\infty)$. I've seen Lemma 1 being referenced to as a source for this fact. ## Local Computation
- Ran standalone finite optimization under pairwise-coprime + harmonic-budget constraints:
 - baseline: prime-tail construction,
 - competitor: local-search over prime-only pairwise-coprime families (multi-start),
 - objective: exact uncovered count on \([1,N]\).

- Tested \(N\in\{5000,10000,20000\}\), \(C\in\{0.6,1.0,1.4\}\).
- In most tested pairs, prime-tail had lower uncovered count than the local-search competitor; only small improvements over prime-tail appeared in 2 low-\(C\) cases.
- This supports prime-tail competitiveness in finite windows, while not proving optimality.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite comparison treats the prime-tail family as the benchmark candidate and searches for pairwise-coprime alternatives under the same harmonic budget. On the tested windows the prime tail is usually at least as good as the heuristic competitors, which supports Erd\H{o}s's proposed extremizer, but it does not prove exact finite optimality.
