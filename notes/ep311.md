# EP-311

## Problem Statement
Let $\delta(N)$ be the minimal non-zero value of $\lvert 1-\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n}\rvert$ as $A$ ranges over all subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$. Is it true that\[\delta(N)=e^{-(c+o(1))N}\]for some constant $c\in (0,1)$?

## Site Remarks
It is trivial that\[\delta(N)\geq \frac{1}{[1,\ldots,N]}=e^{-(1+o(1))N},\]where $[1,\ldots,N]$ is the least common multiple of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$.

The formulation in \cite{ErGr80} has the additional condition that $A$ contain no $S$ such that $\sum_{n\in S}\frac{1}{n}=1$, but Kovac in the comments has shown that the simpler formulation above is equivalent.

Tang has shown (https://github.com/QuanyuTang/erdos-problem-311-note/blob/main/On_Erd%C5%91s_Problem_311_v2.pdf) that\[\delta(N) \leq \exp\left(-c\frac{N}{(\log N\log\log N)^3}\right)\]for some constant $c>0$.

  This formulation, which appeared in the Erdős-Graham problem book, is slightly awkward, because the asked minimal quantity, call it $\delta(N)$, is easily seen to be the same as\[ \delta’(N) := \min \Big( \Big\{ \Big|1-\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n}\Big| : A\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,N\} \Big\} \cap (0,\infty) \Big). \]Namely, if $A$ contains a strict subset $S$ with reciprocals of its elements summing to $1$, then\[ \sum_{n\in A} \frac{1}{n} - 1 \geq \frac{1}{N} + \sum_{n\in S} \frac{1}{n} - 1 = \frac{1}{N}, \]which is “quite large”. It certainly does not make the asymptotics of $\delta(N)$ and $\delta’(N)$ different, but with a bit of brute force verification for small $N$, one can show that, in fact, $\delta(N)= \delta’(N)$ for every $N\geq1$.
  The first $25$ values of $\delta(N)$ can be computed as:
  \begin{align*}
  & 1,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{12},\frac{1}{30},\\
  & \frac{1}{30},\frac{1}{105},\frac{1}{120},\frac{1}{252},\frac{1}{360},\\

  & \frac{11}{1244880},\frac{23}{17907120},\frac{1}{1105104},\frac{1}{1105104},\frac{1}{1593900}.

Also note that\[ \frac{1}{[1,\ldots,N]} = e^{-(1+o(1))N}, \]so the conjecture claims that $\delta(N)$ is exponentially small, but still much larger than the trivial bound.

## Local Computation
I computed exact delta(N) for small N via meet-in-the-middle on scaled
integer subset sums (lcm(1..N) denominator).

For N=10..30, exact values were obtained; empirical
-log(delta(N))/N stays around 0.54 to 0.65 in this range.
This supports exponential decay on finite scales.

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Small-N exact values do not determine existence/uniqueness of an asymptotic
constant c.

Target:
Prove
\[
\delta(N)=\exp(-(c+o(1))N)
\]
for some constant \(c\in(0,1)\), or characterize limsup/liminf constants.
Step A: Lattice subset-sum geometry
Embed scaled reciprocal sums into subset sums on integer lattice
\(\{0,1\}^N\) with common denominator \(L_N=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\dots,N)\).
Step B: Exponential spacing bounds
Prove matching upper/lower bounds for minimum nonzero distance of subset sums to
\(L_N\).
Step C: Constant extraction
Show normalized \(-\log\delta(N)/N\) has a limit.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a sharp anti-concentration bound for near-cancellations of reciprocal subset
sums at scale \(\exp(-\Theta(N))\).

Lemma 1 (Existence of positive minimum):
For fixed \(N\), set
\[
X_N=\left\{\left|1-\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n\right|:A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\},\
\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n\ne1\right\}.
\]
Then \(\delta(N)=\min X_N>0\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
\(X_N\) is finite because there are finitely many subsets. All elements are
strictly positive by definition, so the minimum is positive.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Scaled integer formulation):
Let \(L_N=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\dots,N)\). Then
\[
\delta(N)=\frac{1}{L_N}\min_{A}\left|L_N-\sum_{n\in A}\frac{L_N}{n}\right|,
\]
where minimum is over subsets not giving exact equality.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Multiply each absolute difference by \(L_N\). Since \(L_N/n\in\mathbb Z\), inner
quantity is integer, and scaling is exact.
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Submultiplicative envelope reduction):
If one proves for some constants \(c_1,c_2>0\):
\[
e^{-c_2N}\le \delta(N)\le e^{-c_1N}
\]
and a quasi-submultiplicative relation
\[
\delta(N+M)\le \delta(N)\delta(M)e^{o(N+M)},
\]
then \(-\log\delta(N)/N\) has limsup/liminf trapped in \([c_1,c_2]\), and limit
exists if matching quasi-supermultiplicativity also holds.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Take logs and apply generalized Fekete arguments to \(\log(1/\delta(N))\) with
additive \(o(N)\) errors.
\(\square\)
