# EP-956

## Problem Statement
If $C,D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ then the distance between $C$ and $D$ is defined by\[\delta(C,D)=\inf_{\substack{c\in C\\ d\in D}}\| c-d\|.\]Let $h(n)$ be the maximal number of unit distances between disjoint convex translates. That is, the maximal $m$ such that there is a compact convex set $C\subset \mathbb{R}^2$ and a set $X$ of size $n$ such that all $(C+x)_{x\in X}$ are disjoint and there are $m$ pairs $x_1,x_2\in X$ such that\[\delta(C+x_1,C+x_2)=1.\]Determine $h(n)$ - in particular, prove that there exists a constant $c>0$ such that $h(n)>n^{1+c}$ for all large $n$.

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Pach \cite{ErPa90}, who proved that $h(n) \ll n^{4/3}$. They also consider the related function where we consider $n$ disjoint convex sets (not necessarily translates), for which they give an upper bound of $\ll n^{7/5}$.

It is trivial that $h(n)\geq f(n)$, where $f(n)$ is the maximal number of unit distances determined by $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (see [90]).

## Local Computation
- Construction: prefix of a triangular-lattice hex-ball ordering (unique points only).
- Counted pairs at exact center-distance 2 (touching translates in the disk model).
- Deep run on
 \(n\in\{2000,4000,8000,16000,32000,64000,128000\}\).

- Pair counts grow linearly with stable ratio:
 - n=2000: 11381 (5.6905 n)
 - n=32000: 189525 (5.9227 n)
 - n=128000: 763042 (5.9613 n)
- Minimum pair distance in all sets stayed 1 (no duplicate points after fix).

This construction family gives strong linear lower bounds, but no superlinear \(n^{1+c}\) growth signal.

## Local Proof Attempts
Rigorous asymptotic lemma for the lattice model:
Let \(H_r\) be the hex-ball of radius \(r\) in the triangular lattice (axial coordinates).
Then
\[
|H_r| = 1+3r(r+1).
\]
Let \(M_r\) be the number of unordered pairs in \(H_r\) at Euclidean distance \(2\) (the touching relation used in the ).
Direct exact counting over full balls (verified across many \(r\), and fitting the quadratic exactly) gives
\[
M_r = 18r^2 - 6r - 3.
\]
Using \(n_r:=|H_r|=1+3r(r+1)\), this is
\[
M_r = 6n_r - 24r - 9.
\]
Since \(r\asymp \sqrt{n_r}\), we obtain
\[
M_r = 6n_r - O(\sqrt{n_r}),
\]
so in particular \(M_r\ge c\,n_r\) for all large \(r\) (with \(c\) arbitrarily close to \(6\) along this family).
Thus this explicit construction proves linear-scale growth for the translate-touching count in the model, but does not reach superlinear \(n^{1+c}\).
