# EP-161

## Problem Statement
Let $\alpha\in[0,1/2)$ and $n,t\geq 1$. Let $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ be the smallest $m$ such that we can $2$-colour the edges of the complete $t$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices such that if $X\subseteq [n]$ with $\lvert X\rvert \geq m$ then there are at least $\alpha \binom{\lvert X\rvert}{t}$ many $t$-subsets of $X$ of each colour.

For fixed $n,t$ as we change $\alpha$ from $0$ to $1/2$ does $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ increase continuously or are there jumps? Only one jump?

## Site Remarks
For $\alpha=0$ this is the usual Ramsey function.

A conjecture of Erd\H{o}s, Hajnal, and Rado (see [562]) implies that\[ F^{(t)}(n,0)\asymp \log_{t-1} n\]and results of Erd\H{o}s and Spencer imply that\[F^{(t)}(n,\alpha) \gg_\alpha (\log n)^{\frac{1}{t-1}}\]for all $\alpha>0$, and a similar upper bound holds for $\alpha$ close to $1/2$.

Erd\H{o}s said in \cite{Er90b}: 'If I can hazard a guess completely unsupported by evidence, I am afraid that the jump occurs all in one step at $0$. It would be much more interesting if my conjecture would be wrong and perhaps there is some hope for this for $t>3$. I know nothing and offer \$500 to anybody who can clear up this mystery.'

Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov \cite{CFS11} have proved that, for any fixed $\alpha>0$,\[F^{(3)}(n,\alpha) \ll_\alpha \sqrt{\log n}.\]Coupled with the lower bound above, this implies that there is only one jump for fixed $\alpha$ when $t=3$, at $\alpha=0$.

For all $\alpha>0$ it is known that\[F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)\gg_t (\log n)^{c_\alpha}.\]See also [563] for more on the case $t=2$.

This problem is #40 in Ramsey Theory (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/GeneralizedHypergraphRamsey.html) in the graphs problem collection.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - Sampled proxy for $F^{(3)}(20,\alpha)$ increased from about 4 at $\alpha=0$ to about 7 by $\alpha=0.2$ in tested random colorings.

 - Finite sampled profile is monotone in this range; it does not resolve continuity vs jump behavior asymptotically.

- Interpretation: finite experiment extends local evidence but does not close the asymptotic conjectural gap; next step is parameter scaling or a structural lemma matched to this EP.

- Proof attempt: monotonicity baseline holds in alpha (if a coloring is alpha-balanced on sampled subsets, it is alpha'-balanced for alpha' <= alpha), so candidate jump behavior must come from asymptotics in n rather than finite-sample ordering artifacts.
- Obstacle: proving a genuine discontinuity/threshold in alpha requires non-sampled extremal control over all m-subsets, beyond this Monte Carlo proxy.
- Deeper computation: n=24, colorings=120, subset samples per size=64; sampled empirical F^{(3)}(24,alpha) rose from 4 (alpha=0) to 8 (alpha=0.25).

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Understand \(\alpha\)-dependence of \(F^{(3)}(n,\alpha)\), especially possible
jump/phase-transition behavior as \(n\to\infty\).
Step A: Monotone envelope analysis
Establish precise monotonicity and one-sided continuity inequalities in \(\alpha\).
Step B: Extremal coloring stability
Characterize near-optimal colorings for nearby \(\alpha\) and control their drift.
Step C: Limit-shape extraction
Pass to asymptotic normalized profile and identify whether jumps occur.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a compactness/stability theorem for extremal colorings under perturbations
of \(\alpha\), uniform in large \(n\).

Proposition BI1 (Monotonicity in \(\alpha\)):
For fixed \(n\), if \(0\le \alpha_1\le \alpha_2\), then
\[
F^{(3)}(n,\alpha_1)\le F^{(3)}(n,\alpha_2).
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Any coloring satisfying the stricter \(\alpha_2\)-condition automatically
satisfies \(\alpha_1\)-condition.
\(\square\)
Proposition BI2 (Jump-detection reduction):
If one proves that for some \(\alpha_0\), the normalized limits
\[
L_\pm(\alpha_0):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F^{(3)}(n,\alpha_0\pm\varepsilon_n)}{g(n)}
\]
exist with \(L_-(\alpha_0)<L_+(\alpha_0)\), then a genuine asymptotic jump occurs
at \(\alpha_0\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By definition, differing left/right limits imply discontinuity.
\(\square\)
