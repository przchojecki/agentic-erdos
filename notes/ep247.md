# EP-247

## Problem Statement
Let $1\leq a_1<a_2<\cdots$ be a sequence of integers such that\[\limsup \frac{a_n}{n}=\infty.\]Is\[\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{a_n}}\]transcendental?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er75c} proved the answer is yes under the stronger condition that $\limsup n_k/k^t=\infty$ for all $t\geq 1$.

Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er88c} says 'many of these problems seem hopeless at present, but perhaps one can prove that if $a_n>cn^2$ then $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^{a_n}}$ is not the root of any quadratic polynomial'.

## Local Computation
Finite tests were carried out against eventual periodicity in the binary tail, a necessary signature of rationality, with periods checked up to \(600\).

All tested sparse-growth models showed persistent mismatch rates above zero, with no strong periodic-tail fit.

This remains heuristic evidence only: failure of finite periodicity tests does not prove transcendence, which requires genuinely arithmetic input.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background proves transcendence under a much stronger sparsity condition, namely \(\limsup n_k/k^t=\infty\) for every \(t\ge 1\).

The condition asked here, \(\limsup a_n/n=\infty\), is much weaker and allows denser subsequences than the known transcendence criteria currently handle.

Target:
Under the weak condition \(\limsup a_n/n=\infty\), prove transcendence, or at least irrationality, of the associated sparse binary series.
Step A: Growth-window extraction
From the limsup condition, extract infinitely many long gaps or rapid-growth windows.
Step B: Rational-approximation obstruction
Use those windows to rule out eventual periodicity and derive strong irrationality bounds.
Step C: Transcendence upgrade
Apply Mahler-type or Subspace Theorem machinery to pass from approximation bounds to transcendence.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a theorem translating sparse but irregular exponent growth into non-automaticity or non-periodicity strong enough for transcendence criteria.

Proposition CU1 (Periodic-tail criterion):
If a binary expansion is eventually periodic, the represented real number is rational.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Eventually periodic base-\(2\) expansions correspond to rational geometric tail sums.
\(\square\)
Proposition CU2 (Reduction to approximation bounds):
If one proves that for infinitely many \(m\), every rational \(p/q\) with \(q\le Q_m\) has
\[
\left|x-\frac pq\right|\ge Q_m^{-2-\eta},
\]
and this contradicts any algebraic-degree-\(D\) approximation bound pattern, then transcendence follows.
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Combine the lower approximation barrier with Roth or Subspace-type upper restrictions for algebraic numbers.
\(\square\)
