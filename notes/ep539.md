# EP-539

## Problem Statement
Let $h(n)$ be such that, for any set $A\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ of size $n$, the set\[\left\{ \frac{a}{(a,b)}: a,b\in A\right\}\]has size at least $h(n)$. Estimate $h(n)$.

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s and Szemer\'{e}di proved that\[n^{1/2} \ll h(n) \ll n^{1-c}\]for some constant $c>0$. The upper bound has been improved to $h(n)\ll n^{2/3}$ by Freiman and Lev. A proof of these bounds can be found in a paper of Granville and Roesler \cite{GrRo99}.

Granville and Roesler also reformulate this problem as one in combinatorial geometry: given $A\subseteq \mathbb{Z}^d$ of size $n$ what is the minimum size possible of\[\{ \delta(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) : \mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in A\}\]where $\delta(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})$ has $i$th coordinate $\max(0,a_i-b_i)$. Using this formulation that obtain improved lower bounds in fixed small dimension $d$.

## Site References

  The problem has been studied in the meantime and the best known bounds are\[ n^{1/2} \ll h(n) \ll n^{2/3}. \]Both are easy and are elaborated in this paper by Granville and Roesler from 1999. The lower bound is, as stated, probably an observation of Erdős and Szemerédi, while for the upper bound the authors credit Freiman and Lev.
  Granville and Roesler nicely reformulate the problem as a problem in combinatorial geometry, which is also the viewpoint of Holzman, Lev, and Pinchasi (https://holzman.technion.ac.il/files/2012/09/project.pdf). The two papers also showed (in number theoretic language) that:
  If $A$ consists of numbers with prime divisors $2,3$, then $h_2(n)\gg n^{2/3}$.
  If $A$ consists of numbers with prime divisors $2,3,5$, then $h_3(n)\gg n^{3/5}$.
  If $A$ consists of numbers with prime divisors $2,3,5,7$, then $h_4(n)\gg n^{6/11-\epsilon}$.
