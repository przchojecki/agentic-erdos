# EP-101

## Problem Statement
Given $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, no five of which are on a line, the number of lines containing four points is $o(n^2)$.

## Site Remarks
There are examples of sets of $n$ points with $\sim n^2/6$ many collinear triples and no four points on a line. Such constructions are given by Burr, Gr\"{u}nbaum, and Sloane \cite{BGS74} and F\"{u}redi and Pal\'{a}sti \cite{FuPa84}.

Gr\"{u}nbaum \cite{Gr76} constructed an example with $\gg n^{3/2}$ such lines. Erd\H{o}s speculated this may be the correct order of magnitude. This is false: Solymosi and Stojakovi\'{c} \cite{SoSt13} have constructed a set with no five on a line and at least\[n^{2-O(1/\sqrt{\log n})}\]many lines containing exactly four points.

See also [102] and [669]. A generalisation of this problem is asked in [588].

This problem is Problem 71 on Green's open problems list (https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf).

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine the extremal growth of the number of 4-point lines in planar point sets
with no 5 collinear points.
Step A: Hypergraph model
Encode 4-point lines as 4-uniform edges with pairwise geometric consistency
constraints from planar realizability.
Step B: Structural supersaturation
Show near-quadratic many 4-lines force rigid algebraic/incidence patterns.
Step C: Exact asymptotic envelope
Characterize feasible rigid patterns to determine correct constant/exponent.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a realizability theorem: abstract 4-uniform incidence patterns satisfying
local constraints must embed into few geometric archetypes.

Proposition Z1 (Double-counting identity):
Let \(\mathcal L_4\) be set of 4-point lines in \(P\), \(|P|=n\). Then
\[
\sum_{p\in P} \deg_{\mathcal L_4}(p)=4|\mathcal L_4|.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Count incidences \((p,\ell)\) with \(p\in\ell\), \(\ell\in\mathcal L_4\), by
points and by lines.
\(\square\)
Proposition Z2 (Reduction to point-degree bound):
If one proves \(\deg_{\mathcal L_4}(p)\le g(n)\) for all \(p\), then
\[
|\mathcal L_4|\le \frac{n\,g(n)}4.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply Z1 and bound each summand.
\(\square\)
