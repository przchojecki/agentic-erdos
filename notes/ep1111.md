# EP-1111

## Problem Statement
If $G$ is a finite graph and $A,B$ are disjoint sets of vertices then we call $A,B$ anticomplete if there are no edges between $A$ and $B$.

If $t,c\geq 1$ then there exists $d\geq 1$ such that if $\chi(G)\geq d$ and $\omega(G)<t$ then there are anticomplete sets $A,B$ with $\chi(A)\geq \chi(B)\geq c$.

## Site Remarks
A problem of El Zahar and Erd\H{o}s \cite{ElEr85}, who show that it suffices to consider the case $t\leq c$. Let $d(t,c)$ be the minimal such $d$.

El Zahar and Erd\H{o}s note that a result of Wagon \cite{Wa80b} implies $d(t,2)\leq \binom{t}{2}+1$ (and in fact $d(t+1,2)\leq d(t,2)+t$). We also have $t(2,2)=2$ and $t(3,2)=4$ and $t(4,2)=5$.

El Zahar and Erd\H{o}s proved $d(3,3)\leq 8$ and\[d(t,3) \leq 2\binom{t-1}{3}+7\binom{t-1}{2}+t\]for $t>3$.

Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour \cite{NSS24} prove that if $t,c\geq 1$ then there exists $d\geq 1$ such that if $\chi(G)\geq d$ and $\omega(G)<t$ then there are anticomplete sets $A,B$ with $\chi(B)\geq c$ and such that the minimum degree of the induced graph on $A$ is at least $c$.

  From "Bounding the Chromatic Number of Dense Digraphs by Arc Neighborhoods(2024)" (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-024-00098-z) by F. Klingelhoefer and A. Newman, the problem is equivalent to asking:
  
  For all $c ≥ 0$, there exists $d ≥ 0$ such that if $T$ is a tournament with $\vec{χ}(T) ≥ d$, there are two sets $A, B ⊆ V (T)$ such that $\vec{χ}(T[A]), \vec{χ}(T[B]) ≥ c$ and all arcs between $A$ and $B$ go from vertices of $A$ to vertices of $B$.
  
  The above is essentially a conjecture from "Some results and problems on tournament structure(2023)" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02364) by T. Nguyen, A. Scott and P. Seymour.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Quantify how large \(\min(\chi(A),\chi(B))\) must be for anticomplete pairs \((A,B)\) inside high-chromatic triangle-free graphs.
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a local expansion lemma: in every triangle-free graph with \(\chi(G)\ge c\), either there is an anticomplete pair with
\[
\min(\chi(A),\chi(B))\ge \phi(c),
\]
or \(G\) contains a bounded-size forbidden configuration violating known strengthening hypotheses.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (anticomplete pair coloring inequality):
If \(A,B\) are anticomplete, then
\[
\chi(G[A\cup B])=\max(\chi(A),\chi(B)).
\]
Proof.
No edges between \(A,B\), so a coloring of \(A\cup B\) is obtained by independently coloring each side with the same palette size equal to the larger chromatic number; lower bound is obvious from induced subgraphs. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (critical subgraph reduction):
To prove lower bounds depending on \(\chi(G)\), it suffices to consider \(\chi\)-critical triangle-free \(G\).
Proof.
Every graph contains an induced \(\chi(G)\)-critical subgraph; any anticomplete-pair guarantee there lifts to the original graph. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies asymptotic growth):
If Lemma A yields \(\phi(c)\to\infty\), then the desired function \(f(c)\) exists with \(f(c)\to\infty\).
Proof.
Set \(f=\phi\). The lemma provides the property for each \(c\). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The hard part is establishing quantitative local expansion/structure in triangle-free \(\chi\)-critical graphs strong enough to force large chromaticity on both sides of an anticomplete split.
