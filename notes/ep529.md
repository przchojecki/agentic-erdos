# EP-529

## Problem Statement
Let $d_k(n)$ be the expected distance from the origin after taking $n$ random steps from the origin in $\mathbb{Z}^k$ (conditional on no self intersections) - that is, a self-avoiding walk (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-avoiding_walk). Is it true that\[\lim_{n\to \infty}\frac{d_2(n)}{n^{1/2}}= \infty?\]Is it true that\[d_k(n)\ll n^{1/2}\]for $k\geq 3$?

## Site Remarks
Slade \cite{Sl87} proved that, for $k$ sufficiently large, $d_k(n)\sim Dn^{1/2}$ for some constant $D>0$ (independent of $k$). Hara and Slade (\cite{HaSl91} and \cite{HaSl92}) proved this for all $k\geq 5$.

For $k=2$ Duminil-Copin and Hammond \cite{DuHa13} have proved that $d_2(n)=o(n)$.

It is now conjectured that $d_k(n)\ll n^{1/2}$ is false for $k=3$ and $k=4$, and more precisely (see for example Section 1.4 of \cite{MaSl93}) that $d_2(n)\sim Dn^{3/4}$, $d_3(n)\sim n^{\nu}$ where $\nu\approx 0.59$, and $d_4(n)\sim D(\log n)^{1/8}n^{1/2}$.

Madras and Slade \cite{MaSl93} have a monograph on the topic of self-avoiding walks.

See also [528].

## Local Computation
A kinetic-growth self-avoiding-walk simulation gave displacement proxies:
- \(d=2\): \(d_2(n)/\sqrt n\) rose from \(1.64\) at \(n=40\) to \(1.96\) at \(n=120\),
- \(d=3\): about \(1.22\) to \(1.24\) for \(n=40,80\),
- \(d=4\): about \(1.12\) to \(1.13\) for \(n=40,80\).
The finite data is compatible with stronger growth in \(d=2\) than in higher dimensions.
