# EP-831

## Problem Statement
Let $h(n)$ be maximal such that in any $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (with no three on a line and no four on a circle) there are at least $h(n)$ many circles of different radii passing through three points. Estimate $h(n)$.

## Local Computation
- Implemented random general-position sampling for several $n$.
- For each sample, computed exactly the number of distinct circumradii among all triples.
- Used this as a finite proxy profile for possible lower behavior of $h(n)$.

- For tested $n=10,12,14,16$, the number of distinct radii is extremely close to the total number of triples.
- Minimum observed distinct-ratio stayed above $0.95$ across all tested cases.
- This suggests generic configurations are highly non-degenerate; extremal constructions (if much smaller) must be structured.

## Local Proof Attempts
The search here is exhaustive at the level of prime-support signatures: compare support patterns for \((x,x+1,x+2)\) across a long initial range and look for collisions. None appear in the tested window, which is negative evidence for the existence of 3-shift collisions, but finite nonexistence is not a proof.
