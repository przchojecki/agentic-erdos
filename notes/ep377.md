# EP-377

## Problem Statement
Is there some absolute constant $C>0$ such that\[\sum_{p\leq n}1_{p\nmid \binom{2n}{n}}\frac{1}{p}\leq C\]for all $n$ (where the summation is restricted to primes $p\leq n$)?

## Site Remarks
A question of Erd\H{o}s, Graham, Ruzsa, and Straus \cite{EGRS75}, who proved that if $f(n)$ is the sum in question then\[\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n) = \sum_{k=2}^\infty \frac{\log k}{2^k}=\gamma_0\]and\[\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)^2 = \gamma_0^2,\]so that for almost all integers $f(m)=\gamma_0+o(1)$. They also prove that, for all large $n$,\[f(n) \leq c\log\log n\]for some constant $c<1$. (It is trivial from Mertens estimates that $f(n)\leq (1+o(1))\log\log n$.)

A positive answer would imply that\[\sum_{p\leq n}1_{p\mid \binom{2n}{n}}\frac{1}{p}=(1-o(1))\log\log n,\]and Erd\H{o}s, Graham, Ruzsa, and Straus say there is 'no doubt' this latter claim is true.

This is mentioned in problem B33 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.

  The latter claim looks doable with current technology. It suffices to show that, for any fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and any $1 \leq k \leq \log^{1-\varepsilon} n$, that $\sum_{n^{1/(k+1)} < p \leq n^{1/k}} 1_{p\not \mid \binom{2n}{n}} \frac{1}{p} \ll \frac{\varepsilon}{k}$. A necessary condition for $p\not \mid \binom{2n}{n}$ is that $\{ \frac{n}{p^j} \} \leq \frac{1}{2}$ for all $j$. I think just taking $j = k-1, k-2, \dots, k-J$ for some $J \sim \log \frac{1}{\varepsilon}$, combined with known prime number theorems in short intervals, should be able to get the required bound after some lengthy but routine calculations.
  
  The main problem looks hard though, because of the contribution of the very small primes, where the difficulties are similar to those in #376.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
For
\[
f(n):=\sum_{\substack{p\le n\\ p\nmid \binom{2n}{n}}}\frac1p,
\]
determine whether \(f(n)\) is uniformly bounded.
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a uniform weighted carry bound:
\[
\sum_{p\le n}\frac{\mathbf 1_{p\nmid \binom{2n}{n}}}{p}\le C
\]
with absolute \(C\), by splitting small/medium/large primes and showing enough cancellation/rarity in the no-carry condition for medium and large \(p\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (Kummer reformulation):
\[
p\nmid \binom{2n}{n}\iff \text{no carry in base }p\text{ when adding }n+n.
\]
Proof.
Immediate from Kummer: \(v_p\!\binom{2n}{n}\) equals the carry count. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (large-prime sparsity):
If \(p>n/2\), then \(p\mid\binom{2n}{n}\) unless \(n<p\), and thus primes in \((n/2,n]\) contribute at most \(O(1/\log n)\) to \(f(n)\).
Proof.
For \(p\in(n/2,n]\), \(v_p((2n)!)=2\) and \(v_p(n!)=1\), so \(v_p\binom{2n}{n}=0\) only in narrow edge configurations; summing \(1/p\) over such primes is bounded by \(\sum_{p>n/2}1/p\ll 1/\log n\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (dyadic reduction):
If for every dyadic block \(P<p\le2P\le n\) one has
\[
\sum_{P<p\le2P}\frac{\mathbf 1_{p\nmid \binom{2n}{n}}}{p}\ll P^{-\delta}
\]
uniformly in \(n\), then \(f(n)\) is bounded.
Proof.
Summing the dyadic estimate over \(P=2^j\) gives a convergent geometric series \(\sum_j 2^{-j\delta}\), uniformly in \(n\). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is obtaining a uniform dyadic saving for the weighted no-carry indicator across all \(n\); existing methods provide average control but not the fully uniform bound needed for global boundedness.
