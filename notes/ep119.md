# EP-119

## Problem Statement
Let $z_i$ be an infinite sequence of complex numbers such that $\lvert z_i\rvert=1$ for all $i\geq 1$, and for $n\geq 1$ let\[p_n(z)=\prod_{i\leq n} (z-z_i).\]Let $M_n=\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\lvert p_n(z)\rvert$.

Is it true that $\limsup M_n=\infty$?

Is it true that there exists $c>0$ such that for infinitely many $n$ we have $M_n > n^c$?

Is it true that there exists $c>0$ such that, for all large $n$,\[\sum_{k\leq n}M_k > n^{1+c}?\]

## Site Remarks
This is Problem 4.1 in \cite{Ha74} where it is attributed to Erd\H{o}s.

The weaker conjecture that $\limsup M_n=\infty$ was proved by Wagner \cite{Wa80}, who show that there is some $c>0$ with $M_n>(\log n)^c$ infinitely often.

The second question was answered by Beck \cite{Be91}, who proved that there exists some $c>0$ such that\[\max_{n\leq N} M_n > N^c.\]Erd\H{o}s (e.g. see \cite{Ha74}) gave a construction of a sequence with $M_n\leq n+1$ for all $n$. Linden \cite{Li77} improved this to give a sequence with $M_n\ll n^{1-c}$ for some $c>0$.

The third question seems to remain open.

## Local Computation
Direct numerical evaluation of \(M_n=\max_{|z|=1}|\prod_{i\le n}(z-z_i)|\) was carried out on dense angle grids.

The finite profiles show strong strategy sensitivity: some constructions produce large spikes, while others keep moderate values for a substantial range before blow-up.

This numerical evidence does not settle the third EP question, namely whether \(\sum_{k\le n}M_k>n^{1+c}\) for all large \(n\), since that requires robust analytic lower bounds.

## Local Proof Attempts
Route:
Split the problem into its three subquestions and tried to push from known pointwise lower bounds on M_n toward cumulative-growth bounds for \sum_{k \le n} M_k.

Hard point:
The remaining open part asks for a persistent lower bound on partial sums (\sum_{k \le n} M_k > n^{1+c} for all large n), which is much stronger than sporadic large spikes.

Closed Subquestions:
From the cited literature:
- Wagner proves \(\limsup M_n=\infty\).
- Beck proves a polynomial spike bound
 \[
 \max_{n\le N} M_n > N^c
 \]
 for some \(c>0\).
So the first two subquestions are theorem-level resolved.
Remaining Open Subquestion:
Need eventual lower bound
\[
\sum_{k\le n} M_k > n^{1+c}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).
- Need a transfer from sparse large spikes of \(M_n\) to persistent cumulative
 growth, i.e. a density-of-large-\(M_n\) theorem rather than existence of rare
 spikes.

Target:
Prove for some \(c>0\) and all large \(n\):
\[
\sum_{k\le n} M_k > n^{1+c}.
\]
Step A: Spike Frequency, Not Just Existence
Upgrade \(\max_{k\le N}M_k>N^c\) to a counting statement:
there are at least \(N^\theta\) indices \(k\le N\) with \(M_k\ge N^\gamma\).
Step B: Scale-Consistent Lower Envelope
Show such indices occur across nested dyadic scales with controlled losses.
Step C: Summation Upgrade
Sum contributions from Step B to force superlinear power growth of partial sums.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a quantitative lower-density theorem for large \(M_n\)-events:
\[
|\{k\le N: M_k\ge N^\gamma\}|\ge N^\theta
\]
for fixed \(\gamma,\theta>0\) with \(\gamma+\theta>1\).
