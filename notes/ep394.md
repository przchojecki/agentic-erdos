# EP-394

## Problem Statement
Let $t_k(n)$ denote the least $m$ such that\[n\mid m(m+1)(m+2)\cdots (m+k-1).\]Is it true that\[\sum_{n\leq x}t_2(n)\ll \frac{x^2}{(\log x)^c}\]for some $c>0$?

Is it true that, for $k\geq 2$,\[\sum_{n\leq x}t_{k+1}(n) =o\left(\sum_{n\leq x}t_k(n)\right)?\]

## Site Remarks
In \cite{ErGr80} they mention a conjecture of Erd\H{o}s that the sum is $o(x^2)$. This was proved by Erd\H{o}s and Hall \cite{ErHa78}, who proved that in fact\[\sum_{n\leq x}t_2(n)\ll \frac{\log\log\log x}{\log\log x}x^2.\]Erd\H{o}s and Hall conjecture that the sum is $o(x^2/(\log x)^c)$ for any $c<\log 2$.

Since $t_2(p)=p-1$ for prime $p$ it is trivial that\[\sum_{n\leq x}t_2(n)\gg \frac{x^2}{\log x}.\]Erd\H{o}s and Hall \cite{ErHa78} also note that $t_{n-1}(n!)=2$ and $t_{n-2}(n!)\ll n$, which $n=2^r$ shows is the best possible. They ask about the behaviour of $t_{n-3}(n!)$ and also ask ask whether, for infinitely many $n$,\[t_k(n!)< t_{k-1}(n!)-1\]for all $1\leq k<n$. They proved (with Selfridge) that this holds for $n=10$.

## Local Computation
- a CRT-based exact algorithm for t_2(n) and
 sliding-window exact searches for t_3(n), t_4(n).
- Finite prefixes show \sum_{n\le x} t_4(n) < \sum_{n\le x} t_3(n) throughout
 the tested range; at x=10000, ratio is about 0.920.
- Prefix comparison to t_2 also trends downward in this range, but finite data
 cannot prove the asymptotic o(\cdot) statements.

## Local Proof Attempts
Background says an earlier Erdos conjecture sum_{n<=x} t_2(n)=o(x^2) was proved
by Erdos--Hall, with the explicit bound
sum_{n<=x} t_2(n) << x^2 * (log log log x)/(log log x).
Hence the coarse o(x^2) behavior is settled.

The stronger log-power improvement in subquestion (1) is not obtained by this
bound, and subquestion (2) is also not resolved by the provided background.

Target:
Strengthen from known
\[
\sum_{n\le x} t_2(n)=o(x^2)
\]
to log-power savings, and prove hierarchy
\[
\sum_{n\le x} t_{k+1}(n)=o\!\left(\sum_{n\le x} t_k(n)\right).
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Establish uniform distribution control of solution residues for
\[
n\mid m(m+1)\cdots(m+k-1),
\]
showing average minimal \(m\) contracts by a fixed factor when \(k\mapsto k+1\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (definition monotonicity in \(k\)):
\[
t_{k+1}(n)\le t_k(n).
\]
Proof.
Any \(m\) satisfying divisibility by a \(k\)-block also satisfies it for \((k+1)\)-block by multiplying one extra factor. Minimum cannot increase. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (sum comparison consequence):
From B1,
\[
\sum_{n\le x} t_{k+1}(n)\le \sum_{n\le x} t_k(n).
\]
Proof.
Summing termwise inequality over \(n\le x\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies strict asymptotic separation):
If A yields average contraction \(t_{k+1}(n)\le (1-\delta_k)t_k(n)\) on density-1 set, then
\[
\sum_{n\le x} t_{k+1}(n)=o\!\left(\sum_{n\le x} t_k(n)\right).
\]
Proof.
Split by good density-1 set and negligible exceptional set; good set gives fixed-factor gain repeatedly, forcing ratio to \(0\). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is proving quantitative average contraction beyond monotonicity, uniformly in large \(n\).
