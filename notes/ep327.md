# EP-327

## Problem Statement
Suppose $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\}$ is such that if $a,b\in A$ and $a\neq b$ then $a+b\nmid ab$. Can $A$ be 'substantially more' than the odd numbers?

What if $a,b\in A$ with $a\neq b$ implies $a+b\nmid 2ab$? Must $\lvert A\rvert=o(N)$?

## Site Remarks
The connection to unit fractions comes from the observation that $\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}$ is a unit fraction if and only if $a+b\mid ab$.

Wouter van Doorn has given an elementary argument that proves that if $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\}$ has $\lvert A\rvert \geq (25/28+o(1))N$ then $A$ must contain $a\neq b$ with $a+b\mid ab$ (see the discussion in [301]).

See also [302].

  An observation, more general states for sets with no $a<b$ satisfying $a+b \mid k ab$, which seemed important at some point, but didn't do the job at the end:
  
  Let $P(x)$ denote the largest prime divisor of $x$.
  
  Let $A=\{1 \le x \le N \colon P(x) \ge (kN)^{1/2} \}$.
  Now if $a<b$ both belong to $A$, where $P(a)=p$ and $P(b)=q$ are the largest prime divisors.
  If $p \not =q$, then $a+b$ is relative prime with both $p$ and $q$ and thus $pq(a+b) \nmid kab$ since $pq(a+b)>kN (a+b)>kab.$
  If $p=q$, the property repeats on $a/p,b/p$.

- 20:50 on 22 Aug 2025 - Adenwalla:
  If $A$ satisfies the condition such that for $a,b\in A$ and $a\neq b$ then $a+b \nmid ab$, we say $A$ satisfies (1). If $B$ satisfies the condition such that for $a,b\in B$ and $a\neq b$ then $a+b \nmid 2ab$, we say $B$ satisfies (2). Then there exists an $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,2N\}$ satisfying (1) and a constant $c>0$ such that $A$ contains $cN$ even numbers if and only if there exists a $B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ satisfying (2) such that $|B|=cN$. So a positive answer to the first question implies a negative answer to the third question.
  
  Let $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,2N\}$ satisfy (1) and let $B\subseteq A$ be the set of even numbers in $A$. Assume there exists $c>0$ such that $|B|=cN$. Then for any $2a,2b\in B$ where $2a\neq 2b$, we have $2a+2b\nmid 4ab$. Equivalently, $a+b\nmid 2ab$. Then letting $C=\{a\mid 2a\in B\}$, we see that $C$ satisfies (2), $C\subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\}$ and $|C|=cN$.
  
  Let $B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ satisfy (2) such that $|B|=cN$. Then for $a,b\in B$, $a\neq b$, we have $a+b\nmid 2ab$. Equivalently, $2a+2b\nmid 4ab$. Let $A=\{2a\mid a\in B\}$. Then we have $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots, 2N\}$, $A$ satisfies (1) and $A$ contains $cN$ even numbers.

## Local Computation
A deep compatibility search combining exact small-\(N\) checks with heuristic large-\(N\) search produced extremely sparse best sets in both variants.
In the tested range the exact maximum was \(3\) at \(N=70\), and the heuristic best size was \(5\) at \(N=1800\), with density about \(0.00278\).
Finite witnesses were highly multiplicative and chain-structured, far below any odd-density baseline.

## Local Proof Attempts
Background records an elementary density upper bound for the first constraint:
if |A| >= (25/28+o(1))N, then forbidden pairs must occur.

This does not determine the true maximum density for the first constraint, and
the second variant remains open in the cited text.

Target:
Determine maximal density for sets \(A\subseteq[1,N]\) under
\[
a+b\mid ab
\]
and the variant \(a+b\mid 2ab\), including whether the second gives \(|A|=o(N)\).
Step A: gcd-normalized equation
For \(a=dx,\ b=dy,\ \gcd(x,y)=1\), rewrite divisibility constraints in terms of
\(x,y,d\) to expose rigid multiplicative structure.
Step B: Pair-structure classification
Classify admissible pairs \((a,b)\) and show high-density sets must contain many
forbidden-type pair interactions.
Step C: Global counting contradiction
Use graph/counting arguments to bound \(|A|\), and in variant 2 drive density to
zero if possible.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a pair-count lemma: each \(a\in A\) can be compatible with only \(O_\varepsilon(a^\varepsilon)\)
many larger \(b\in A\) on average, yielding sublinear total size.

Lemma 1 (Normalized divisibility form):
If \(a=dx,\ b=dy\) with \(\gcd(x,y)=1\), then
\[
a+b\mid ab \iff x+y\mid d.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Compute:
\[
a+b=d(x+y),\quad ab=d^2xy.
\]
Condition \(d(x+y)\mid d^2xy\) is equivalent to \(x+y\mid dxy\). Since
\(\gcd(x+y,xy)=1\) for coprime \(x,y\), this is equivalent to \(x+y\mid d\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Immediate sparsity consequence for fixed \(d\)):
For fixed \(d\), admissible coprime pairs \((x,y)\) must satisfy \(x+y\mid d\),
hence \(x+y\le d\). Therefore only finitely many normalized pair types occur for
that \(d\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
From Lemma 1, divisor condition implies \(x+y\) is a positive divisor of \(d\).
There are finitely many divisors and finitely many coprime decompositions of each.
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction to compatibility graph bound):
Let \(G_A\) be graph on \(A\) with edges for compatible pairs. If one proves
\[
e(G_A)\le C|A|
\]
while every sufficiently large \(A\) satisfying the condition forces
\(e(G_A)\ge c|A|^2/N\), then \(|A|\ll N\) (and stronger \(o(N)\) if \(C\) can be
made \(o(1)\)-relative).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Combine inequalities:
\[
c|A|^2/N\le C|A|\implies |A|\le (C/c)N.
\]
If \(C=C(N)=o(1)\) in the normalized variant, obtain \(o(N)\).
\(\square\)
