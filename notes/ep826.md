# EP-826

## Problem Statement
Are there infinitely many $n$ such that, for all $k\geq 1$,\[\tau(n+k)\ll k?\]

## Local Computation
- Built a finite proxy scan with exact divisor counts up to $N_{\max}+K$.
- For each $n\le N_{\max}$, measured
 $$\max_{1\le k\le K}\frac{\tau(n+k)}{k}.$$
- Also measured a tail-only variant
 $$\max_{K_0\le k\le K}\frac{\tau(n+k)}{k},$$
 to reduce trivial small-$k$ domination.

- With $(N_{\max},K,K_0)=(40000,2000,50)$:
 - full metric best value is $2$ (dominated by $k=1$);
 - tail metric best value is about $0.1967$ at $n=11$.
- This highlights the difficulty of uniform-in-$k$ control: small shifts dominate finite constants.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite proxy measures the worst normalized divisor count along shifts \(n+k\) and separates the full metric from a tail-only version to reduce the trivial impact of very small \(k\). That illustrates the core obstruction clearly: uniform control over all \(k\) is dominated by tiny shifts, and finite scans do not yet suggest a route to infinitely many such \(n\).
