# EP-74

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n)\to \infty$ (possibly very slowly). Is there a graph of infinite chromatic number such that every finite subgraph on $n$ vertices can be made bipartite by deleting at most $f(n)$ edges?

## Site Remarks
Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s, Hajnal, and Szemer\'{e}di \cite{EHS82}.

R\"{o}dl \cite{Ro82} has proved this for hypergraphs, and also proved there is such a graph (with chromatic number $\aleph_0$) if $f(n)=\epsilon n$ for any fixed constant $\epsilon>0$.

It is open even for $f(n)=\sqrt{n}$. Erd\H{o}s offered \$500 for a proof but only \$250 for a counterexample. This fails (even with $f(n)\gg n$) if the graph has chromatic number $\aleph_1$ (see [111]).

## Local Computation
Finite computations targeted the natural analogue: among graphs with \(\chi\ge 4\), minimize the odd-cycle-transversal cost, i.e. the fewest edge deletions needed to make the graph bipartite.

Best sampled witnesses at \(n=14\) had \(\chi=4\) and required only two deletions to become bipartite for some densities, showing that finite near-bipartite high-chromatic behavior can be quite sparse.

This remains only finite evidence; the EP asks for infinite-chromatic constructions with a sublinear deletion profile for every \(n\)-vertex subgraph.

## Local Proof Attempts
Background gives a construction of Rodl for graphs of chromatic number \(\aleph_0\) when \(f(n)=\varepsilon n\) for fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), and it also gives the hypergraph analogue.

For graphs, the sublinear regime remains open; the background explicitly notes that this is open even for \(f(n)=\sqrt n\).

Target:
Construct an infinite-chromatic graph \(G\) such that every \(n\)-vertex
subgraph \(H\subseteq G\) satisfies
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{odd}}(H)\le f(n),
\]
with \(f(n)\to\infty\) sublinear (e.g. \(n^{1/2}\)).
Step A: Layered random construction
Build \(G\) as a union of sparse random layers \(G_t\) with carefully tuned
densities so odd cycles are globally spread but locally repairable.
Step B: Local repair lemma
Prove every \(n\)-vertex induced \(H\) has odd-cycle transversal
\(\tau_{\mathrm{odd}}(H)\le C n^\alpha\) for some \(\alpha<1\).
Step C: Global chromatic explosion
Show the layered design forces \(\chi(G)=\aleph_0\) (or higher) despite local
near-bipartiteness.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform concentration/containers statement controlling odd-cycle packing
in all \(n\)-vertex traces simultaneously; current probabilistic tools are not
tight enough in the sublinear regime.

Proposition H1 (Local repair + global obstruction gives solution):
Suppose an infinite graph \(G\) satisfies:
\[
\forall n,\ \forall H\subseteq G,\ |V(H)|=n:\ \tau_{\mathrm{odd}}(H)\le Cn^\alpha
\quad(\alpha<1),
\]
and also every proper coloring of \(G\) uses infinitely many colors. Then \(G\)
is a positive solution with \(f(n)=\lceil Cn^\alpha\rceil\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
The local inequality is exactly the deletion requirement. The second condition
is \(\chi(G)=\aleph_0\). Hence \(G\) meets both demanded properties.
\(\square\)
Proposition H2 (Sublinear control is the only missing quantitative step):
If Step C is achieved by construction and Step B is proved with \(\alpha<1\),
then EP-74 is resolved for that exponent profile.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate from Proposition H1.
\(\square\)
