# EP-968

## Problem Statement
Let $u_n=p_n/n$, where $p_n$ is the $n$th prime. Does the set of $n$ such that $u_n<u_{n+1}$ have positive density?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s and Prachar \cite{ErPr61} proved that\[\sum_{p_n<x} \lvert u_{n+1}-u_n\rvert \asymp (\log x)^2,\]and that the set of $n$ such that $u_n>u_{n+1}$ has positive density.

As with many of Erd\H{o}s' questions, by 'positive density' here he most likely meant positive lower density - in other words, there exists a constant $c>0$ such that for all large $x$ the number of such $n\leq x$ is at least $cx$.

Erd\H{o}s also asks whether\[u_n<u_{n+1}<u_{n+2}\]or\[u_n>u_{n+1}>u_{n+2}\]have infinitely many solutions.

- 21:57 on 10 Sep 2025 - TerenceTao:
  The answer to the problem is positive assuming both the Riemann hypothesis and a weak form of the pair correlation conjecture, specifically estimate (5) of this paper of Heath-Brown (http://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa41/aa4118.pdf). By Corollary 1 of that paper, we have for any $A$ that
  $$ \sum_{p_n \leq x: p_{n+1}-p_n \geq A \log x} (p_{n+1}-p_n) \ll \frac{1}{A} x \quad (1)$$
  for any $A > 0$. From telescoping series we have
  $$ \sum_{p_n \leq x} (p_{n+1}-p_n) = (1+o(1)) x$$
  hence by the prime number theorem
  $$ \sum_{p_n \leq x: p_{n+1}-p_n \leq \log x} (\log x - (p_{n+1}-p_n)) =
  \sum_{p_n \leq x: p_{n+1}-p_n > \log x} ((p_{n+1}-p_n) - \log x) + o(x).$$
  From known results about small gaps between primes (such as the GPY result mentioned in my previous comment) the left-hand side is $\gg x$, hence the right hand side is also. Combining this with (1) we conclude that there exist $0 < \varepsilon < A$ such that $(1+\varepsilon) \log x \leq p_{n+1}-p_n \leq A \log x$ for $\gg x/\log x$ values of $p_n$. For such values we have $u_n < u_{n+1}$, giving the claim.
  
  It is also straightforward to deduce the claim from a sufficiently uniform version of the prime tuples conjecture (which, by a well known calculation of Gallagher (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/on-the-distribution-of-primes-in-short-intervals/DCE557AC8750333E68426FCEEC11858C), implies that the normalized prime gaps $(p_{n+1}-p_n) / \log p_n$ are asymptotically exponentially distributed).
  
  This seems close to the limit of current technology, so further progress may need to await some breakthrough on the pair correlation conjecture or the prime tuples conjecture.

  The claim $u_n < u_{n+1}$ rearranges to $p_{n+1}-p_n > \frac{p_n}{n}$ so by the prime number theorem this is essentially asking for a large prime gap $p_{n+1}-p_n \gtrsim \log n$. In the other direction, one can recover Erdos's previous result from the more recent result of results of Goldston, Pintz, and Yildirim (https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.5886) that for a positive density of $n$, one has $p_{n+1}-p_n \leq \eta \log n$ for any fixed $\eta> 0$.
  
  I imagine that with the modern Maynard sieve one can similarly conclude that $u_n > u_{n+1} > u_{n+2}$ for a set of positive density by exhibiting many clusters of three nearby primes, but I haven't checked this carefully (and it may already be in the literature).
  
  The enemy preventing one from proving $u_n < u_{n+1}$ for a set of positive density is if almost all prime gaps in say $[x,2x]$ are less than $\log x$, and a small number (zero density) of prime gaps are much larger than $\log x$. Possibly one could use some of the large prime gap theory or the Maier matrix method to produce a positive density of clusters of moderately large prime gaps to finish off the problem though?

## Local Proof Attempts
Exact reformulation and proof lemmas:
Let \(p_n\) be the \(n\)-th prime, \(g_n:=p_{n+1}-p_n\), and \(u_n:=p_n/n\).
Lemma 1 (exact increment identity):
\[
u_{n+1}-u_n=\frac{n\,g_n-p_n}{n(n+1)}.
\]
Proof:
\[
\frac{p_{n+1}}{n+1}-\frac{p_n}{n}
=\frac{n(p_n+g_n)-(n+1)p_n}{n(n+1)}
=\frac{n g_n-p_n}{n(n+1)}.
\]
Corollary 1 (sign criterion):
\[
u_{n+1}>u_n \iff g_n>\frac{p_n}{n}=u_n.
\]
Similarly,
\[
u_{n+1}<u_n \iff g_n<u_n.
\]
So the EP-968 set
\[
\{n:\,u_n<u_{n+1}\}
\]
is exactly
\[
\{n:\,g_n>u_n\}.
\]
Heuristic bridge:
Since \(u_n\sim \log n+\log\log n\) and typical prime gaps are also of logarithmic scale,
the sign is governed by the fluctuation of \(g_n\) around the local mean scale.
This explains why both signs appear with substantial frequency in computations,
while density questions remain subtle.
Arithmetic progression obstruction (rational \(\alpha\)):
If \(\alpha=a/b\in\mathbb Q\), then \(\{\alpha p_n\}\) lies in the finite set
\[
\left\{0,\frac1b,\dots,\frac{b-1}{b}\right\},
\]
so it cannot be equidistributed in \([0,1)\).
Thus irrationality assumptions in equidistribution variants are logically necessary; rational parameters are rigidly periodic modulo 1.
