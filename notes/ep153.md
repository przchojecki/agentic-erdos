# EP-153

## Problem Statement
Let $A$ be a finite Sidon set and $A+A=\{s_1<\cdots<s_t\}$. Is it true that\[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{1\leq i<t}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2 \to \infty\]as $\lvert A\rvert\to \infty$?

- 16:13 on 15 Feb 2026 - Sayan Dutta:
  Not sure if this is interesting enough to comment, but this conjecture is immediate in the “very sparse relative to its span” regime, and does not use the Sidon property at all. Let $A$ be any finite set of integers, $|A|=k$, and write $A+A=\{s_1<\dots<s_t\}$ with gaps $d_i=s_{i+1}-s_i$. Let $D:=\operatorname{Diam}(A)=\max(A)-\min(A)$. Then $s_t-s_1=2D$. We have
  $$\frac1t\sum_{i=1}^{t-1} d_i^2 \ge \frac{16D^2}{k^2(k+1)^2}$$
  by Cauchy–Schwarz. In particular, if $D\gg k^2$ (e.g. for $A\subset[1,n]$ with $\operatorname{Diam}(A)\asymp n$ and $k=o(\sqrt n)$), then the mean square consecutive gap $\to\infty$.

  Interesting. I guess when you wrote $D\gg k^2$, you meant $D/k^2\to\infty$ as $k\to\infty$. Since dense Sidon sets with $k$ elements have diameter about $k^2$, one needs to exploit more about the structure of $A+A$.

## Local Computation
Exact enumeration for Sidon sets with \(\min(A)=1\) and \(N\le 60\) gives the following global minima for the average squared gap in \(A+A\):
- \(m=2\mapsto 0.666667\), \(m=3\mapsto 1.333333\), \(m=4\mapsto 1.8\), \(m=5\mapsto 2.8\), \(m=6\mapsto 3.52381\), \(m=7\mapsto 4.5\), \(m=8\mapsto 5.5\), \(m=9\mapsto 5.644444\), \(m=10\mapsto 6.872727\).
Thus the exact minimum already rises across the full computed range. The Ruzsa prime family and the Mian-Chowla family both show strong growth as well. In larger adversarial searches, the best values found are \(11.27,28.08,66.16,121.56,154.65,218.26,301.62,373.09\) for \(m=11,20,30,40,50,60,70,80\), and the extension to \(m=81,90,100\) gives \(353.57,417.53,594.15\). These data strongly support divergence, though they are still only finite evidence.

## Local Proof Attempts
Let \(m=|A|\). For Sidon sets one has \(t=|A+A|=m(m+1)/2\) and also \(a_{\max}-a_{\min}\ge \binom m2\), since all positive differences are distinct. Hence
\[
s_t-s_1=2(a_{\max}-a_{\min})\ge m(m-1).
\]
By Cauchy,
\[
\frac1t\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2\ge \frac{(s_t-s_1)^2}{t(t-1)},
\]
which yields only a constant-order lower bound, not divergence. Writing \(x_i=(s_{i+1}-s_i)-1\ge 0\), one gets
\[
\frac1t\sum (s_{i+1}-s_i)^2=rac{(t-1)+2\sum x_i+\sum x_i^2}{t}.
\]
So divergence would follow either from very large total excess gap mass or from strong variance forcing. The missing ingredient is a rigidity theorem showing that a large Sidon set cannot have both small range and uniformly small gap variance.
