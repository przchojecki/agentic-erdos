# EP-222

## Problem Statement
Let $n_1<n_2<\cdots$ be the sequence of integers which are the sum of two squares. Explore the behaviour of (i.e. find good upper and lower bounds for) the consecutive differences $n_{k+1}-n_k$.

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er51} proved that, for infinitely many $k$,\[ n_{k+1}-n_k \gg \frac{\log n_k}{\sqrt{\log\log n_k}}.\]Richards \cite{Ri82} improved this to\[\limsup_{k\to \infty} \frac{n_{k+1}-n_k}{\log n_k} \geq 1/4.\]The constant $1/4$ here has been improved, most lately to $0.868\cdots$ by Dietmann, Elsholtz, Kalmynin, Konyagin, and Maynard \cite{DEKKM22}.

The best known upper bound is due to Bambah and Chowla \cite{BaCh47}, who proved that\[n_{k+1}-n_k \ll n_k^{1/4}.\]The differences are listed at A256435 (https://oeis.org/A256435) on the OEIS.
