# EP-719

## Problem Statement
Let $\mathrm{ex}_r(n;K_{r+1}^r)$ be the maximum number of $r$-edges that can be placed on $n$ vertices without forming a $K_{r+1}^r$ (the $r$-uniform complete graph on $r+1$ vertices).

Is every $r$-hypergraph $G$ on $n$ vertices the union of at most $\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n;K_{r+1}^r)$ many copies of $K_r^r$ and $K_{r+1}^r$, no two of which share a $K_r^r$?

## Forum Comments
- 23:43 on 08 Feb 2026 - Zeraoulia Rafik:
  For $r=3$, if $m=|E(G)|$ and $\nu(G)$ is the maximum number of \emph{edge-disjoint}
  copies of $K_4^3$ in $G$, then the optimal decomposition into edge-disjoint
  $K_4^3$'s plus single edges uses exactly $m-3\nu(G)$ parts.
  
  I exhaustively checked all $2^{\binom{6}{3}}=2^{20}$ $3$-graphs on $n=6$ vertices:
  $\mathrm{ex}_3(6;K_4^3)=14$, and for every $G$ on $6$ vertices one has
  $m-3\nu(G)\le 14$ (so no counterexample exists for $(r,n)=(3,6)$). Code available
  on request.
  
  Also $(r,n)=(7,8)$ is immediate: on $8$ vertices there are exactly $8$ $7$-edges,
  so $\mathrm{ex}_7(8;K_8^7)=7$, and any $7$-graph on $8$ vertices decomposes into
  $\le 7$ single edges if $m\le 7$, or into one $K_8^7$ if $m=8$.
