# EP-1201

## Problem Statement
Is it true that for every $\epsilon,\eta>0$ there exists a $k$ such that the density of $n$ for which\[P(n(n+1)\cdots(n+k))>n^{1-\epsilon}\]is at least $1-\eta$ (where $P(m)$ is the greatest prime divisor of $m$)?

- 15:05 on 30 Apr 2026 - TerenceTao (reply depth 1):
  Using known results for pair correlations between $P(n+i)$ and $P(n+j)$, e.g., from this paper of Teravainen (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.01195), one should be able to prove a significantly stronger result, namely that for any $k$, one has $P(n \dots(n+k)) = n^{(\lambda + o_{k \to \infty}(1))k}$ on a set of logarithmic density $1 - o_{k \to \infty}(1)$, where $\lambda = 0.6243\dots$ is the Golomb-Dickman constant (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golomb%E2%80%93Dickman_constant).
  
  EDIT: Actually, this result doesn't quite strictly contain #1201 because it involves logarithmic density instead of natural density. On the other hand, it should be noted that Theorem 1.19 of Teravainen's paper already establishes $P(n (n+1)) > n^{2-\varepsilon}$ on a set of positive lower natural density.
  
  SECOND EDIT: As pointed out below, the above comments actually apply to $P(n) \dots P(n+k)$ and $P(n) P(n+1)$ instead of $P(n \dots (n+k))$ and $P(n (n+1))$ respectively.
