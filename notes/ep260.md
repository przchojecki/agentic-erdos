# EP-260

## Problem Statement
Let $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ be an increasing sequence such that $a_n/n\to \infty$. Is the sum\[\sum_n \frac{a_n}{2^{a_n}}\]irrational?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er81l} proved this is true under either of the stronger assumptions that
{UL}
{LI} $a_{n+1}-a_n\to \infty$ or {/LI}
{LI} $a_n \gg n\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}$.{/LI}
{/UL}
Erd\H{o}s and Graham speculate that the condition $\limsup a_{n+1}-a_n=\infty$ is not sufficient, but know of no example.

In \cite{Va99} this is asked with $a_n/n\to \infty$ replaced by $a_{n+1}-a_n\to \infty$, which as above was proved by Erd\H{o}s in \cite{Er81l}.

- 20:25 on 12 Aug 2025 - DesmondWeisenberg:
  If $a \in \mathbb{N}$, $b \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, and $A$ is the set of all numbers of the form $am + b$ (with $m$ a positive integer), then
  \begin{equation*}
  \sum_{n \in A} \frac{n}{2^n} = \sum_m \frac{am + b}{2^{am + b}} = \left(\frac{1}{2^b}\right)\left(\frac{a2^a}{(2^a - 1)^2}\right) + \left(\frac{b}{2^b}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2^a - 1}\right).
  \end{equation*}
  In particular, this number is rational. To see this, first observe that
  \begin{equation*}
  \sum_m \frac{am + b}{2^{am + b}} = \frac{1}{2^b}\sum_m \frac{am + b}{2^{am}}
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{equation*}
  = \frac{1}{2^b}\sum_m \frac{am}{2^{am}} + \frac{1}{2^b}\sum_m \frac{b}{2^{am}}
  \end{equation*}
  \begin{equation*}
  = \left(\frac{1}{2^b}\right)\sum_m \frac{am}{2^{am}} + \left(\frac{b}{2^b}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2^a - 1}\right).
  \end{equation*}
  Now all that remains is to prove that
  \begin{equation*}
  \sum_m \frac{am}{2^{am}} = \frac{a2^a}{(2^a - 1)^2}.
  \end{equation*}
  To see this, recall that
  \begin{equation*}
  \frac{1}{1 - x^a} = \sum_m x^{am}
  \end{equation*}
  for all $x \in (-1, 1)$. The desired result is obtained by differentiating with respect to $x$, multiplying by $x$, and then setting $x = \frac{1}{2}$.
  
  (Note that this argument for $A = \mathbb{N}$ is already well-known; see, for example, this StackExchange post (https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/1325254/what-does-sum-k-0-infty-frack2k-converge-to).)

## Local Computation
- exact dyadic partial-sum arithmetic and deep rational-approximation scans.
- The tested families did not exhibit anomalously tiny rational-approximation errors in scanned ranges.
- Obstacle: this remains computational evidence only; proving irrationality under the full a_n/n\to\infty hypothesis requires theoretical arguments beyond finite scans.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background gives two stronger hypotheses under which irrationality is already proved:
1. \(a_{n+1}-a_n\to\infty\).
2. \(a_n\gg n\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}\).

What remains open is whether the weaker condition \(a_n/n\to\infty\) alone suffices.

Target:
Under only
\[
a_n\uparrow\infty,\qquad \frac{a_n}{n}\to\infty,
\]
prove irrationality of
\[
S=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a_n}{2^{a_n}}.
\]
Step A: Binary-block rigidity reduction
Translate irrationality into non-eventual-periodicity of the binary expansion of
\(S\). Show each summand contributes a localized binary block around bit-scale
\(a_n\), with carry interaction controlled by tails.
Step B: Sparse-carry separation
Prove infinitely many indices \(n\) where tail mass
\(\sum_{m>n}a_m2^{-a_m}\) is too small to alter a prescribed finite window of bits
created by the \(n\)-th summand.
Step C: Periodicity contradiction
If the expansion were eventually periodic, hence rational, sufficiently far windows
would repeat with fixed period; Step B yields infinitely many non-repeating
witness windows, a contradiction.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform isolated-window lemma: existence of infinitely many \(n\) such
that
\[
\sum_{m>n}\frac{a_m}{2^{a_m}}<2^{-a_n-\Delta_n}
\]
with \(\Delta_n\to\infty\), and simultaneously the \(n\)-th summand produces a
nontrivial bit pattern within \([a_n,a_n+\Delta_n]\). This is automatic under
\(a_{n+1}-a_n\to\infty\), but open under only \(a_n/n\to\infty\).

Proposition DF1 (Rational implies eventual periodicity):
If \(S\in\mathbb Q\), its base-2 expansion is eventually periodic.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Standard characterization of rational numbers in integer bases.
\(\square\)
Proposition DF2 (Tail localization bound):
For \(S_n=\sum_{m\le n}a_m2^{-a_m}\),
\[
0<S-S_n\le \sum_{m>n}\frac{a_m}{2^{a_m}}.
\]
Hence if the right-hand side is \(<2^{-T}\), then the binary digits of \(S\) and \(S_n\) agree up to position \(T-1\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate from absolute convergence and binary place-value stability.
\(\square\)
Proposition DF3 (Reduction to isolated windows):
If infinitely many \(n\) satisfy the blocking lemma above, then \(S\notin\mathbb Q\).
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Using DF2, each such \(n\) certifies a bit window determined by one summand and
immune to tail carries. Eventual periodicity would force eventual repetition of
all long windows, contradicting infinitely many isolated non-repeating windows.
\(\square\)
