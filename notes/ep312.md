# EP-312

## Problem Statement
Does there exist some $c>0$ such that, for any $K>1$, whenever $A$ is a sufficiently large finite multiset of positive integers with $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n}>K$ there exists some $S\subseteq A$ such that\[1-e^{-cK} < \sum_{n\in S}\frac{1}{n}\leq 1?\]

  Does anyone know if Erdős and Graham posed precisely the exponential "gap" for a good reason?
  For a finite multiset of positive integers $A$ denote\[ R(A) := \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n} \]and\[ \epsilon(A) := \min\Big\{ 1 – R(S) \,:\, S\subseteq A,\, R(S)\leq 1 \Big\}. \]The problem asks if inequality\[ \epsilon(A) \leq e^{- c R(A)} \]holds with some constant $c>0$.
  Here is a simple example which comes close to showing that this is the best possible. For a positive integer $N$ take\[ A = \bigcup_{p\text{ prime},\ p\leq N} \{\underbrace{p,p,\ldots,p}_{p-1\text{ times}} \}. \]Then\[ R(A) = \sum_{p\text{ prime},\ p\leq N} \frac{p-1}{p} \sim \pi(N) = (1+o(1)) \frac{N}{\log N}, \]while $R(S)\neq1$ for every sub-multiset $S$ of $A$, so\[ \epsilon(A) \geq \prod_{p\text{ prime},\ p\leq N} \frac{1}{p} = \frac{1}{N\#} = e^{-(1+o(1))N}. \]Since $N=(1+o(1))R(A)\log R(A)$, this shows\[ \epsilon(A) \geq e^{- (1+o(1)) R(A) \log R(A)} \]for this particular sequence of examples of multisets $A$. It is not clear to me if a better (e.g., exponential) lower bound is possible, even for these particular multisets $A$.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background says Erd"os-Graham proved a weaker quantitative form with
exp(-cK) replaced by a term of order 1/K^2.

The stronger exponential-accuracy gap in the statement is not proved in the
provided background.

A direct attempt to refine the near-1 subset-sum approximation route beyond
polynomial error did not produce a new quantitative bound.

Target:
Upgrade the known polynomial gap near \(1\) to the conjectured exponential gap
\(1-e^{-cK}\) under EP-312 hypotheses.
Step A: Additive-energy control of reciprocal multisets
Quantify concentration of subset sums near \(1\) using entropy/energy of
\(\{1/n:n\in A\}\).
Step B: Near-1 anti-gap theorem
Show existence of subset sum in \((1-e^{-cK},1]\) by iterative balancing.
Step C: Sharp parameter dependence
Optimize constants to make \(c\) absolute (or explicit in structural parameters).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a large-deviation refinement for subset sums of reciprocals that beats
polynomial window \(K^{-2}\) down to \(e^{-cK}\).

Lemma 1 (Maximal-under-1 subset principle):
Let
\[
M(A):=\max\left\{\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n:\ S\subseteq A,\ \sum_{n\in S}\frac1n\le1\right\}.
\]
Then \(M(A)\le1\), and the deficit is
\[
\Delta(A):=1-M(A)\ge0.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Definition of constrained maximum gives both inequalities.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Greedy maximality condition):
If \(S_*\) attains \(M(A)\), then for every \(m\in A\setminus S_*\),
\[
\frac1m>\Delta(A).
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
If some \(m\notin S_*\) had \(1/m\le\Delta(A)\), then
\[
\sum_{n\in S_*}\frac1n+\frac1m\le M(A)+\Delta(A)=1,
\]
contradicting maximality of \(M(A)\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction to counting small reciprocals):
If one proves many elements of \(A\) satisfy \(1/n\ll e^{-cK}\) while
\(\sum_{n\in A}1/n\) stays in the EP-312 regime, then Lemma 2 forces
\(\Delta(A)\ll e^{-cK}\), hence
\[
M(A)\ge 1-e^{-cK}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Contrapositive of Lemma 2: if \(\Delta(A)\) were larger, all excluded elements
would have reciprocals \(>\Delta(A)\), limiting count and total mass
inconsistently with hypothesis.
\(\square\)
