# EP-1137

## Problem Statement
Let $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$, where $p_n$ denotes the $n$th prime. Is it true that\[\frac{\max_{n<x}d_{n}d_{n-1}}{(\max_{n<x}d_n)^2}\to 0\]as $x\to \infty$?

- 12:24 on 02 Feb 2026 - Przemek Chojecki:
  Heuristically this is not true by Cramer like conjecture.
  Let $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$ be prime gaps and define $M(x):=\max_{n<x} d_n$ and $P(x):=\max_{n<x} d_{n-1}d_n$. In a Cramer-Poisson heuristic one treats the normalized gaps\[
  X_n:=\frac{d_n}{\log p_n}
  \]as approximately i.i.d. exponential random variables with mean $1$ (locally, on the scale of $\log p_n$), and also approximately independent for neighboring indices.
  
  For $N$ i.i.d. $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ variables, the maximum satisfies $X_{(1)}=\max_{1\le n\le N}X_n=(1+o(1))\log N$ with high probability. Translating back, this predicts\[
  M(x)\approx (\log p_x)\,\log x,
  \]up to lower-order factors, consistent with the usual extreme-value behavior in the model.
  
  Consider adjacent pairs $(X_n,X_{n-1})$ and set a threshold $t=a\log N$. Under independence,\[
  \mathbb{E}\#\{1\le n\le N:\; X_{n-1}\ge t,\ X_n\ge t\}
  \approx N\,\mathbb{P}(X\ge t)^2
  = N e^{-2t}
  = N^{\,1-2a}.
  \]Hence for any $a<\tfrac12$ there are typically many indices with $X_{n-1},X_n\ge a\log N$, while for $a>\tfrac12$ there are typically none; this places the largest adjacent pair around $t\approx \tfrac12\log N$ in each coordinate. Consequently,\[
  \max_{n\le N} X_{n-1}X_n \approx \left(\tfrac12 \log N\right)^2
  \quad\text{while}\quad
  \left(\max_{n\le N}X_n\right)^2 \approx (\log N)^2,
  \]so the ratio $P(x)/M(x)^2$ is heuristically bounded away from $0$ (on the order of a positive constant, plausibly about $1/4$ in this simplified model), rather than tending to $0$.
