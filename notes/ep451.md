# EP-451

## Problem Statement
Estimate $n_k$, the smallest integer $>2k$ such that $\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}(n_k-i)$ has no prime factor in $(k,2k)$.

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s and Graham write 'we can prove $n_k>k^{1+c}$ but no doubt much more is true'.

In \cite{Er79d} Erd\H{o}s writes that probably $n_k<e^{o(k)}$ but $n_k>k^d$ for all constant $d$.

Adenwalla observes that an easy upper bound is $n_k\leq \prod_{k<p<2k}p=e^{O(k)}$.

  Here is a heuristic proof of this Problem.
  Let\[
  \mathcal P_k=\{p:\ p\ \text{prime},\ k<p<2k\},\qquad
  M_k=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_k}p.
  \]The condition that the whole block $[n-k,n-1]$ contains no multiple of any $p\in\mathcal P_k$ is equivalent to saying that the $k$ integers $n-1,\dots,n-k$ are all coprime to $M_k$. For a fixed $p\in\mathcal P_k$, the interval $[n-k,n-1]$ contains a multiple of $p$ iff\[
  n\equiv 1,2,\dots,k\pmod p.
  \]Thus the allowed residues mod $p$ are exactly the $p-k$ classes $\{0,k+1,\dots,p-1\}$. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the number of allowed residue classes mod $M_k$ is\[
  A_k=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_k}(p-k),
  \]so the exact density of admissible $n$ is\[
  D_k \ :=\ \frac{A_k}{M_k}
  \ =\ \prod_{\substack{k<p<2k\\ p\ \mathrm{prime}}}
  \left(1-\frac{k}{p}\right).
  \]Using summation over primes and the prime number theorem one obtains the asymptotic\[
  \log\frac{1}{D_k}=(\log 4+o(1))\,\frac{k}{\log k}.
  \]Numerically (for $1\le k\le 50$), $\log n_k$ grows on the $\frac{k}{\log k}$ scale and closely tracks $(\log 4)\frac{k}{\log k}$; in this range we in fact observe\[
  \log n_k \ \le\ (\log 4)\,\frac{k}{\log k},
  \]while we cannot replace $\log 4$ by $\log 3$.
  Heuristically one expects $n_k\asymp D_k^{-1}$; making this rigorous appears to be the remaining difficulty.
  Details of the proofs and numerical evidence can be found on my GitHub page here (https://github.com/QuanyuTang/Notes-on-Erdos-Problem-451/blob/main/Notes_on_Erdos_Problem_451_Density_and_Heuristics.pdf).

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
The search cap limits exact determination for larger k; asymptotic behavior of
n_k remains wide open.

Target:
Estimate growth of
\[
n_k:=\min\left\{n>2k:\ P\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i)\right)\notin(k,2k)\right\}.
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a prime-avoidance interval lemma: for each \(k\), among \(k\)-blocks \((n-k,\dots,n-1)\), the first block avoiding primes in \((k,2k)\) appears at \(n_k\) with asymptotic scale governed by a Poisson model of forbidden prime divisibility.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (forbidden-prime criterion):
Condition on \(n_k\) is equivalent to
\[
v_p\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n-i)\right)=0\quad\forall p\in(k,2k).
\]
Proof.
No prime factor in interval \((k,2k)\) means each such prime has valuation zero. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (block divisibility events):
For fixed \(p\in(k,2k)\), event \(p\mid \prod_{i=1}^k(n-i)\) is equivalent to \(n\equiv r\pmod p\) for one of \(k\) residues.
Proof.
Product includes \(k\) consecutive residues; divisibility occurs when one term is \(0\mod p\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies \(n_k\) asymptotic):
If A gives accurate hazard rate for simultaneous avoidance of events in B2, expected first-hit location yields asymptotic for \(n_k\).
Proof.
First-hit in Bernoulli/Poisson process has inverse hazard-scale; apply with modeled avoidance probability. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The key obstacle is controlling dependence between many prime-divisibility events across overlapping residue conditions.
