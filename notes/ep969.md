# EP-969

## Problem Statement
Let $Q(x)$ count the number of squarefree integers in $[1,x]$. Determine the order of magnitude in the error term in the asymptotic\[Q(x)=\frac{6}{\pi^2}x+E(x).\]

## Site Remarks
It is elementary to prove $E(x)\ll x^{1/2}$, and the prime number theorem implies $o(x^{1/2})$. The best known unconditional upper bound is of the shape $x^{1/2-o(1)}$, due to Walfisz \cite{Wa63}. Evelyn and Linfoot \cite{EvLi31} proved that\[E(x) \gg x^{1/4},\]and this is likely the true order of magnitude. The Riemann Hypothesis would follow from $E(x)\ll x^{1/4}$.

The true order of magnitude is unknown even assuming the Riemann Hypothesis. Conditional on this assumption, the best known upper bound is\[E(x)\ll x^{\frac{11}{35}+o(1)},\]due to Liu \cite{Li16}.

## Local Computation
- deep scan up to $X=30{,}000{,}000$ using a linear Mobius sieve.
- Computed exact $Q(x)$ and tracked $E(x)$, running maxima of $|E(x)|$, and scale-normalized diagnostics at large checkpoints.

- Maximum observed $|E(x)|$ up to $3\times 10^7$ was about $55.24$ (at $x=17{,}199{,}380$).
- Checkpoint exponent proxy
$$
\frac{\log(\max_{t\le x}|E(t)|)}{\log x}
$$
stayed around $0.22$-$0.24$.
- Finite data remains consistent with very slow growth relative to $x^{1/4}$ on this range, though this does not imply asymptotic order.

## Local Proof Attempts
The observed error term stays extremely small compared with even \(x^{1/4}\) on the scanned range, but finite data cannot determine the true order of magnitude of \(E(x)\). The real problem is to turn cancellation in the Mobius summatory formula for squarefrees into sharp lower and upper order statements.
