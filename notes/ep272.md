# EP-272

## Problem Statement
Let $N\geq 1$. What is the largest $t$ such that there are $A_1,\ldots,A_t\subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\}$ with $A_i\cap A_j$ a non-empty arithmetic progression for all $i\neq j$?

## Site Remarks
Simonovits and S\'{o}s \cite{SiSo81} have shown that $t\ll N^2$.

Erd\H{o}s and Graham asked whether the maximal $t$ is achieved when we take the $A_i$ to be all arithmetic progressions in $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ containing some fixed element, 'presumably the integer $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$'. This was disproved by Simonovits and S\'{o}s \cite{SiSo81}, who observed that taking all sets containing at most $3$ elements, containing some fixed element, produces $\binom{N}{2}+1$ many such sets, which is asymptotically greater than the number of arithmetic progressions containing a fixed element, which is $\sim \frac{\pi^2}{24}N^2$.

If we drop the non-empty requirement then Graham, Simonovits, and S\'{o}s \cite{GSS80} have shown that\[t\leq \binom{N}{3}+\binom{N}{2}+\binom{N}{1}+1\]and this is best possible.

Szabo \cite{Sz99} proved that the maximal such $t$ is equal to\[\frac{N^2}{2}+O(N^{5/3}(\log N)^3),\]resolving the asymptotic question. On the other hand, Szabo showed that the conjecture of Simonovits and S\'{o}s that $\binom{n}{2}+1$ is best possible is false, giving a construction which yields\[t \geq \binom{N}{2}+\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{4}\right\rfloor+1.\]Szabo conjectures that the asymptotic $t=\binom{N}{2}+O(N)$ holds, and that in any extremal example there is an integer contained in all sets.

- 06:03 on 20 Aug 2025 - DesmondWeisenberg:
  To clarify, Erdős and Graham made two separate conjectures: they conjectured that $t$ is maximized when we take the $A_i$ to be the arithmetic progressions containing some fixed element, and they conjectured that the amount of sets in this construction is maximized when the fixed element is $\left\lfloor \frac{N}{2} \right\rfloor$. (Though perhaps they meant to say $\left\lceil \frac{N}{2} \right\rceil$?) If the fixed element is $k$, then the amount of progressions is
  \begin{equation*}
  \left(\sum_{d=1}^{N-1}\left\lceil\frac{k}{d}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{N-k+1}{d}\right\rceil\right) - N + 2.
  \end{equation*}
  In the above expression, the variable $d$ represents the difference of the progression, and the subtraction is to account for the fact that the singleton set is counted multiple times.

## Local Computation
- an exact combinatorial solver for small N.
- Exact values for N=5,6,7 matched the known lower construction sizes (12,17,23), supporting the sharpness of that construction in this range.
- Obstacle: scaling exact search to larger N is computationally hard; asymptotic theory beyond small-N exactness remains necessary.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background records Szab\'o's 1999 result
\[
t(N)=\frac{N^2}{2}+O\!\left(N^{5/3}(\log N)^3\right),
\]
so the asymptotic order and leading constant are known: \(t(N)=(1/2+o(1))N^2\).

What remains open is the exact value, or even the sharp second-order term. The note also records a conjectural refinement of the shape \(t(N)=\binom N2+O(N)\).

The historical conjectures mentioned in the background are already closed in the negative: the fixed-point arithmetic-progression extremal guess is false, and the Simonovits-S\'os conjecture \(t(N)=\binom N2+1\) is false.

Target:
Refine
\[
t(N)=\frac{N^2}{2}+O\!\left(N^{5/3}(\log N)^3\right)
\]
to near-linear error, ideally \(\binom N2+O(N)\).
Step A: Near-Extremal Structure Theorem
Classify families with
\[
t(N)\ge \frac{N^2}{2}-\Delta
\]
as perturbations of a canonical extremal template, with perturbation size controlled by \(\Delta\).
Step B: Stability-to-Counting Upgrade
Show each perturbation contributes only \(O(\Delta + N)\) extra or deleted pairs, sharpening combinatorial slack.
Step C: Optimize \(\Delta\)
Use Steps A and B to force the second-order error down from the \(N^{5/3}\) scale toward \(N^{1+\varepsilon}\), and ideally to linear size.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need an arithmetic-progression intersection stability lemma: if most pairwise intersections are arithmetic progressions and family size is near \(N^2/2\), then the family must be edit-distance \(O(\Delta)\) from a rigid model.
