# EP-251

## Problem Statement
Is\[\sum \frac{p_n}{2^n}\]irrational? (Here $p_n$ is the $n$th prime.)

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er58b} proved that $\sum \frac{p_n^k}{n!}$ is irrational for every $k\geq 1$.

In \cite{Er88c} he further conjectures that $\sum \frac{p_n^k}{2^n}$ is irrational for every $k$, and that if $g_n\geq 2$ and $g_n=o(p_n)$ then\[\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{p_n}{g_1\cdots g_n}\]is irrational. (The example $g_n=p_n+1$ shows that some condition on the growth of the $g_n$ is necessary here.)

The decimal expansion of this sum is A098990 (https://oeis.org/A098990) on the OEIS.

  The last conjecture below the main problem statement has an (overly simple) negative answer. The idea is elementary, just make the series telescope by choosing positive integers $(c_n)$ such that\[ \frac{p_n}{g_1\cdots g_n} = \frac{c_n}{g_1\cdots g_{n-1}} - \frac{c_{n+1}}{g_1\cdots g_n}, \]i.e. $c_{n+1}=c_n g_n - p_n$. Thus, one can define $(c_n)$ recursively, by only making sure that\[ c_{n+1} \equiv -p_n \ (\text{mod} \ c_n) \]and that it is bounded from below by something that grows slowly but steadily. Afterwards one just sets\[ g_n := \frac{c_{n+1}+p_n}{c_n}. \]I got ChatGPT 5.4 Pro figure out the proof (https://chatgpt.com/share/69df7105-03cc-838a-a4a5-cbb6301e199d) and write up the details (https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~vjekovac/files/Erdos_problem_251.pdf) with only minimal orchestration from my side.

  By summation by parts, this is equivalent to the irrationality of $\sum_n \frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{2^n}$. It is possible that a sufficiently quantitative and uniform version of the prime tuples conjecture can resolve this problem, if it gives sufficient statistical control on the binary expansion of about $\log \log n$ consecutive prime gaps $p_{n+1}-p_n$ (which is usually of size $\asymp \log n$) to show that the binary expansion of $\sum_n \frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{2^n}$ cannot be periodic. The theory of Shannon entropy may be helpful in this regard.

## Local Computation
Exact dyadic partial sums were computed together with a nearest-rational approximation search.

In the scanned window, the best approximation errors stayed bounded away from trivially ultra-good fits, with \(q^2\cdot\text{error}\approx 0.36\), giving a finite irrationality-style signal.

Finite denominator scans still provide only computational evidence; they cannot prove irrationality of the full infinite sum.

## Local Proof Attempts
Route:
Related the problem to known irrationality results for prime-weighted series with factorial denominators.

Hard point:
Methods exploiting factorial denominators do not directly transfer to geometric denominators.

Target:
Prove irrationality (or stronger) of
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{p_n}{2^n},
\]
where \(p_n\) is the \(n\)-th prime.
Step A: Prime-growth decomposition
Split into smooth main approximation using \(p_n\sim n\log n\) and fluctuation
error.
Step B: Binary-expansion irregularity
Show prime fluctuations induce non-periodic binary tail behavior incompatible with
rationality.
Step C: Diophantine conclusion
Convert tail irregularity into irrationality/transcendence criterion.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a quantitative lower bound on binary-tail complexity generated by the prime
sequence that cannot occur for rational numbers.

Proposition CW1 (Tail estimate):
For \(T_N=\sum_{n>N} p_n/2^n\),
\[
T_N\ll \frac{N\log N}{2^N}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Use \(p_n\ll n\log n\) and compare to weighted geometric tail.
\(\square\)
Proposition CW2 (Rationality obstruction reduction):
If one proves infinitely many \(N\) such that the first \(m(N)\) bits after
position \(N\) of partial sums cannot match any eventually periodic pattern with
period \(<P(N)\), where \(m(N)\gg \log P(N)\), then the full sum is irrational.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Rational numbers have eventually periodic binary expansions with fixed period; a
violating infinite sequence of windows contradicts this.
\(\square\)
