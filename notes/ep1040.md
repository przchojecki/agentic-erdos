# EP-1040

## Problem Statement
Let $F\subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be a closed infinite set, and let $\mu(F)$ be the infimum of\[\lvert \{ z: \lvert f(z)\rvert < 1\}\rvert,\]as $f$ ranges over all polynomials of the shape $\prod (z-z_i)$ with $z_i\in F$.

Is $\mu(F)$ determined by the transfinite diameter of $F$? In particular, is $\mu(F)=0$ whenever the transfinite diameter of $F$ is $\geq 1$?

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s, Herzog, and Piranian \cite{EHP58}, who show that the answer is yes if $F$ is a line segment or disc, and that if the transfinite diameter is $<1$ then $\{ z: \lvert f(z)\rvert < 1\}$ always contains a disc of radius $\gg_F 1$.

Erd\H{o}s and Netanyahu \cite{ErNe73} proved that if $F$ is also bounded and connected, with transfinite diameter $0<c<1$, then $\{ z: \lvert f(z)\rvert < 1\}$ always contains a disc of radius $\gg_c 1$.

The transfinite diameter of $F$, also known as the logarithmic capacity, is defined by\[\rho(F)=\lim_{n\to \infty}\sup_{z_1,\ldots,z_n\in F}\left(\prod_{i<j}\lvert z_i-z_j\rvert\right)^{1/\binom{n}{2}}.\]Aletheia \cite{Fe26} has shown that $\mu(F)$ is not determined by the transfinite diameter of $F$, by producing two distinct closed infinite sets $F_1$ and $F_2$, both of which have transfinite diameter $0$, and yet $\mu(F_1)\geq \pi/4$ while $\mu(F_2)$ can be made arbitrarily close to $0$.

## Local Computation
I tested F on the unit circle via f_m(z)=z^m-1 (roots in F) and estimated area
|{z:|f_m(z)|<1}| by Monte Carlo.
Sample estimates:
- m=40: area ~1.324
- m=100: area ~0.818
- m=300: area ~0.344

For this canonical F (capacity/transfinite diameter 1), the tested areas decrease strongly
with m, consistent with mu(F)=0 in this special case. This does not settle the general
question for arbitrary closed infinite F.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine whether \(\mu(F)\) is governed by transfinite diameter/capacity, especially whether \(\mathrm{diam}(F)\ge1\) implies \(\mu(F)=0\).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a capacity-determined upper bound:
\[
\mu(F)\le \Phi(\mathrm{cap}(F)),
\]
with \(\Phi(t)=0\) for \(t\ge1\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (capacity-transfinite diameter identity):
For compact \(F\subset\mathbb C\), transfinite diameter equals logarithmic capacity.
Proof.
Classical Fekete-Szegő theorem. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (model case calibration):
For \(F=\partial\mathbb D\), \(\mathrm{cap}(F)=1\), and \(f_m(z)=z^m-1\) gives shrinking sublevel-area trend toward \(0\).
Proof.
Direct computation/known asymptotics of \(|z^m-1|<1\) area; numerical profile supports vanishing. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies EP claim):
If A holds with \(\Phi(t)=0\) for \(t\ge1\), then any \(F\) with transfinite diameter \(\ge1\) satisfies \(\mu(F)=0\).
Proof.
Apply B1 to replace transfinite diameter by capacity in A. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The hard step is proving a universal extremal-area inequality that depends only on capacity and not finer geometry of \(F\).
