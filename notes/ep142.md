# EP-142

## Problem Statement
Let $r_k(N)$ be the largest possible size of a subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ that does not contain any non-trivial $k$-term arithmetic progression. Prove an asymptotic formula for $r_k(N)$.

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s remarked this is 'probably unattackable at present'. In \cite{Er97c} Erd\H{o}s offered \$1000, but given that he elsewhere offered \$5000 just for (essentially) showing that $r_k(N)=o_k(N/\log N)$ (see [3]), that value seems odd. In \cite{Er81} he offers \$10000, stating it is 'probably enormously difficult'.

The best known upper bounds for $r_k(N)$ are due to Kelley and Meka \cite{KeMe23} for $k=3$, Green and Tao \cite{GrTa17} for $k=4$, and Leng, Sah, and Sawhney \cite{LSS24} for $k\geq 5$. An asymptotic formula is still far out of reach, even for $k=3$.

In \cite{Er80} and \cite{Va99} he asks (much more reasonably) for the order of magnitude of $r_k(N)$. In \cite{Er80} he remarks that we do not even know whether $r_k(n)/r_{k+1}(n)\to 0$ for any $k\geq 3$.

See also [3] and [139].

Proposition AU1 (Monotonicity and submultiplicativity template):
For fixed \(k\), \(r_k(N)\) is nondecreasing and satisfies (up to constants)
\[
r_k(MN)\ge r_k(M)\,r_k(N).
\]
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Take product-type constructions on digit expansions/base decomposition; AP-free
property is preserved under the standard combinatorial product embedding.
\(\square\)
Proposition AU2 (Exponent-gap reduction):
If
\[
N^{\alpha_k-o(1)}\le r_k(N)\le N^{\beta_k+o(1)},
\]
then improving either \(\alpha_k\) upward or \(\beta_k\) downward directly narrows
the asymptotic uncertainty interval.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Normalize by \(\log N\): \(\log r_k(N)/\log N\in[\alpha_k-o(1),\beta_k+o(1)]\).
\(\square\)
