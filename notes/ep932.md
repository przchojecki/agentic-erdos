# EP-932

## Problem Statement
Let $p_k$ denote the $k$th prime. For infinitely many $r$ there are at least two integers $p_r<n<p_{r+1}$ all of whose prime factors are $<p_{r+1}-p_r$.

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s thought this was true but that there are very few such $r$. He could show that the density of $r$ such that at least one such $n$ exist is $0$.

This problem has been formalised in Lean (https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/932.lean) as part of the Google DeepMind Formal Conjectures project (https://google-deepmind.github.io/formal-conjectures/).

- 20:18 on 13 Dec 2025 - TerenceTao:
  This looks really delicate. It is basically asking to produce a pair of nearby $y$-smooth numbers, both living in a prime gap of length $y$. Cramer's conjecture predicts that the longest prime gap is of size about $\log^2 x$, and de Bruijn type asymptotics say that the density of $\log^2 x$-smooth number is about $x^{-1/2+o(1)}$, which is only barely enough to expect a near-collision on probabilistic grounds. So the problem seems exactly on the border between being heuristically true or heuristically false, and resolving it would require going beyond Cramer's conjecture which is considered hopeless by current methods (cf. problem #4).

## Local Proof Attempts
Background records a one-sided rarity result:
Erdos proved the set of r for which at least one such n exists has density 0.

Density-0 does not preclude infinitely many exceptional r, and the "at least two"
claim remains unproved.
