# EP-976

## Problem Statement
Let $f\in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ be an irreducible polynomial of degree $d\geq 2$. Let $F_f(n)$ be maximal such that there exists $1\leq m\leq n$ with $f(m)$ is divisible by a prime $\geq F_f(n)$. Equivalently, $F_f(n)$ is the greatest prime divisor of\[\prod_{1\leq m\leq n}f(m).\]Estimate $F_f(n)$. In particular, is it true that $F_f(n)\gg n^{1+c}$ for some constant $c>0$? Or even $\gg n^d$?

## Site Remarks
Nagell \cite{Na22} and Ricci \cite{Ri34} proved that\[F_f(n) \gg n\log n,\]which Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er52c} improved to\[F_f(n) \gg n(\log n)^{\log\log\log n}.\]In \cite{Er65b} he claimed a proof of\[F_f(n) \gg n\exp((\log n)^c)\]for some constant $c>0$, but said he had never published the proof, which was 'fairly complicated'. This seems to have been flawed, since Erd\H{o}s and Schinzel \cite{ErSc90} later published a weaker bound. A proof of the stronger bound above was finally provided by Tenenbaum \cite{Te90}.

- 08:06 on 03 Feb 2026 - Przemek Chojecki:
  I did some literature search and in full generality (arbitrary irreducible $f\in\mathbb Z[x]$ of degree $\ge 2$), the strongest unconditional lower bounds known for\[
  F_f(n)=P^+\Bigl(\prod_{m\le n} f(m)\Bigr)
  \]are superlinear but subpower: they grow like $n^{1+o(1)}$, e.g.\[
  F_f(n)\ \ge\ n\exp\exp\bigl(c(\log\log n)^{1/3}\bigr)
  \](and related refinements). These come from global "Chebyshev/Erdos-Schinzel" methods controlling how often primes can divide polynomial values, but they do not reach any fixed power $n^{1+c}$.
  
  Fixed-power bounds $F_f(n)\gg n^{1+c}$ are known for certain special low-degree polynomials, because one can prove that in each dyadic interval $(X,2X]$ a positive proportion of values $f(m)$ have a prime factor $\ge X^{1+c}$; this immediately implies $F_f(n)\gg n^{1+c}$. This has been proved for example for the cubic $n^3+2$ (Irving) and for certain quartics with cyclic/dihedral Galois group (Dartyge-Maynard), via deep sieve arguments plus strong "Type I/II" distribution estimates for $f(n)$ in residue classes.
  
  For quadratics such as $n^2+1$, there are powerful results showing $P^+(n^2+1)\ge n^{1+c}$ infinitely often (with explicit $c$), but not (currently) the kind of dyadic positive-proportion statement that would force $F_f(n)\gg n^{1+c}$ uniformly for all large $n$. Broadly, pushing $n^{1+c}$ for general $f$ seems to require much stronger distribution/trace-function/automorphic input than is available in the "generic" case, while special algebraic structure can make the needed analytic estimates accessible.

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
That lower bound is stronger than n log n but does not by itself force a fixed-power gain n^{1+delta}. The asked n^{1+c} and especially n^d lower targets remain unresolved in this batch.

Rigorous reduction lemma (smooth-number obstruction):
Let
\[
F_f(X):=\max_{n\le X} P^+(|f(n)|),
\]
where \(P^+(m)\) is the largest prime factor of \(m\).
For \(Y\ge 2\), if \(F_f(X)\le Y\), then every \(|f(n)|\) (\(n\le X\)) is \(Y\)-smooth.
Hence
\[
X \le \Psi\!\big(\max_{n\le X}|f(n)|,\;Y\big),
\]
where \(\Psi(T,Y)\) counts \(Y\)-smooth integers \(\le T\).
Therefore, contrapositively:
if for some \(Y\),
\[
\Psi\!\big(\max_{n\le X}|f(n)|,\;Y\big)<X,
\]
then necessarily
\[
F_f(X)>Y.
\]
This is a fully rigorous bridge from EP-976 to smooth-number upper bounds.
Any strong estimate making \(\Psi\) small enough immediately forces large prime factors of polynomial values.
