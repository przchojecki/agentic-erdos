# EP-1133

## Problem Statement
Let $C>0$. There exists $\epsilon>0$ such that if $n$ is sufficiently large the following holds.

For any $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in [-1,1]$ there exist $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in [-1,1]$ such that, if $P$ is a polynomial of degree $m<(1+\epsilon)n$ with $P(x_i)=y_i$ for at least $(1-\epsilon)n$ many $1\leq i\leq n$, then\[\max_{x\in [-1,1]}\lvert P(x)\rvert >C.\]

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s proved that, for any $C>0$, there exists $\epsilon>0$ such that if $n$ is sufficiently large and $m=\lfloor (1+\epsilon)n\rfloor$ then for any $x_1,\ldots,x_m\in [-1,1]$ there is a polynomial $P$ of degree $n$ such that $\lvert P(x_i)\rvert\leq 1$ for $1\leq i\leq m$ and\[\max_{x\in [-1,1]}\lvert P(x)\rvert>C.\]The conjectured statement would also imply this, but Erd\H{o}s in \cite{Er67} says he could not even prove it for $m=n$.

- pick n=28, epsilon=0.25, so target fit size t=floor((1-epsilon)n)=21;
- random labels y_i in {-1,1};
- for each label vector, sample many subsets S of size t;
- interpolate exactly on S (degree t-1) and measure sup norm on a dense grid.
Main signal:
- Chebyshev nodes: many label vectors admit low sup-norm sampled fits
 (median min-sup about 2.34; about 83.6% had some sampled fit with sup norm

- Equispaced nodes: sampled near-full fits are usually much larger

Interpretation:
- behavior is strongly node-geometry dependent in finite tests;
- this supports that robust obstructions may hold in unstable-node regimes, while

## Local Proof Attempts
Attempt route:
Compared the statement to Erdos' known opposite-direction extremal interpolation
construction.

Hard point:
Turning this into the stated robust near-fit impossibility for arbitrary data remains
open; background says he could not prove even the m=n version.

Target:
Establish robust interpolation obstruction: for many prescribed \((x_i,y_i)\), any polynomial fitting almost all pairs with degree \(<(1+\varepsilon)n\) must have sup norm \(>C\).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a node-geometry dichotomy lemma:
for unstable node families (large Lebesgue amplification), every near-full fit of random \(\pm1\) labels has sup norm \(\gg 1\) with overwhelming probability.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (fit-subset representation):
If \(P\) interpolates labels on subset \(S\) of size \(t\), then
\[
P(x)=\sum_{i\in S} y_i\,\ell_i^{(S)}(x),
\]
where \(\ell_i^{(S)}\) are Lagrange basis polynomials for nodes in \(S\).
Proof.
Standard interpolation formula on the selected nodes. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (sup norm lower via coefficient signs):
For random signs \(y_i\), typical size of \(\sup_x|P(x)|\) is controlled below by the \(\ell_2\)-mass
\[
\left(\sum_{i\in S} (\ell_i^{(S)}(x))^2\right)^{1/2}
\]
at points where this mass is large.
Proof.
At fixed \(x\), \(P(x)\) is a Rademacher sum; Khintchine-type bounds give typical magnitude comparable to coefficient \(\ell_2\)-norm. Take sup over \(x\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies probabilistic obstruction):
If Lemma A gives uniform lower control of the basis-mass profile on unstable nodes, then near-full low-sup fits become exponentially unlikely.
Proof.
Combine Lemma B2 with concentration for Rademacher sums and union bound over admissible subsets \(S\). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The hardest step is making the probabilistic obstruction uniform over all near-full subsets \(S\), not just a fixed chosen subset.
