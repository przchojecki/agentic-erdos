# EP-891

## Problem Statement
Let $2=p_1<p_2<\cdots$ be the primes and $k\geq 2$. Is it true that, for all sufficiently large $n$, there must exist an integer in $[n,n+p_1\cdots p_k)$ with $>k$ many prime factors?

## Site Remarks
Schinzel deduced from P\'{o}lya's theorem \cite{Po18} (that the sequence of $k$-smooth integers has unbounded gaps) that this is true with $p_1\cdots p_k$ replaced by $p_1\cdots p_{k-1}p_{k+1}$.

This is unknown even for $k=2$ - that is, is it true that in every interval of $6$ (sufficiently large) consecutive integers there must exist one with at least $3$ prime factors?

Weisenberg has observed that Dickson's conjecture (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickson%27s_conjecture) implies the answer is no if we replace $p_1\cdots p_k$ with $p_1\cdots p_k-1$. Indeed, let $L_k$ be the lowest common multiple of all integers at most $p_1\cdots p_k$. By Dickson's conjecture there are infinitely many $n'$ such that $\frac{L_k}{m}n'+1$ is prime for all $1\leq m<p_1\cdots p_k$. It follows that, if $n=L_kn'+1$, then all integers in $[n,n+p_1\cdots p_k-1)$ have at most $k$ prime factors.

  While Desmond's remark would possibly leading to saying "no",
  I also want to add observations leading to a plausible "yes".
  
  Observation:
  If this is not the case for some large $n$, one of the elements in the interval is of the form $p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} \ldots p_k^{e_k}$.
  So it is sufficient to consider the neighbourhood of these powers and check if many of them have few prime factors.
  This seems unlikely.
  
  Quantitave for $k=2$:
  Let $n>4372$ (likely the necessary bound), where $r=6 \lfloor n/6 \rfloor =2^i*3^j$.
  Then $r-2$ and $r+2$ are both multiples of $2$, and $r-3, r+3$ are both multiples of $3$.
  If $i,j>1$, then furthermore the exponents of the small primes are one.
  So the probability that is works, is bounded by a function of the form $1/ \log(x)^2.$
  Also the other elements in the interval are likely not pseudoprimepowers, so the sum of chances to find one is small.
  
  For $r=3*2^i$, one needs that $r-3$ or $r+3$ is almost prime.
  So $i$ needs to be prime or a power of $2$ (the latter is expected to be excluded, as there is likely no Fermat number above $2^{32}+1$).
  So take $p$ such that $2^p-1$ is a Mersenne prime.
  Now $3*2^p-1=t*s$ and $3*2^p-2=2*q$ should already both have at most $2$ prime factors.
  For $r=2*3^i$, one can guess along the same lines.
  
  Nonetheless, fully making the above rigourous, may be hard.
  Anyone else ideas? Cases to exclude?

  for $k=3$, $n>8611$ may be the quantitative "large enough".
