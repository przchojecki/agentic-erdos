# EP-796

## Problem Statement
Let $k\geq 2$ and let $g_k(n)$ be the largest possible size of $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}$ such that every $m$ has $<k$ solutions to $m=a_1a_2$ with $a_1<a_2\in A$.

Is it true that\[g_3(n)=\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}n+(c+o(1))\frac{n}{\log n}\]for some constant $c$?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er64d} proved that if $2^{r-1}<k\leq 2^r$ then\[g_k(n) \sim \frac{(\log\log n)^{r-1}}{(r-1)!\log n}n\](which is the asymptotic count of those integers $\leq n$ with $r$ distinct prime factors).

In particular the asymptotics of $g_k(n)$ are known; in \cite{Er69b} Erd\H{o}s discussed the second order terms, and this question is implicit (especially when compared to the explicit question [425] he asked on several occasions).

In \cite{Er69} this question actually appears with a denominator of $(\log n)^2$ in the second term. Furthermore, for $k=3$ he claims he could prove the existence of some $0<c_1\leq c_2$ such that\[\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}n+c_1\frac{n}{(\log n)^2}\leq g_3(n)\leq \frac{\log\log n}{\log n}n+c_2\frac{n}{(\log n)^2}.\]This is strange, since in \cite{Er64d} both the upper and lower bound techniques that he used in fact prove\[\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}n+c_1\frac{n}{\log n}\leq g_3(n)\leq \frac{\log\log n}{\log n}n+c_2\frac{n}{\log n}\]for some constants $0<c_1\leq c_2$. My best guess is that the denominator of $(\log n)^2$ in \cite{Er69} is just an unfortunate repeated typo, and $\log n$ was intended in both the reported bounds and the main question.

This correction is thanks to Tang, who noted independently (see the comments) the improved lower bound given above (indeed with an improvement in the constant $c_1$).

The special case $k=2$ is the subject of [425].

- 17:47 on 09 Jan 2026 - Quanyu Tang:
  I wrote a short note (https://github.com/QuanyuTang/erdos-problem-796-note/blob/main/On_Erd%C5%91s_Problem_796.pdf) showing that the asymptotic currently stated on this page cannot be correct.
  In the note (https://github.com/QuanyuTang/erdos-problem-796-note/blob/main/On_Erd%C5%91s_Problem_796.pdf) I give an explicit construction proving\[g_3(n)\ge \frac{n\log\log n}{\log n} + (M+1+o(1))\frac{n}{\log n},\]where \(M\) is the Meissel--Mertens constant. In particular, this rules out any conjectural asymptotic of the form \(g_3(n)=\frac{n\log\log n}{\log n} + (c+o(1))\frac{n}{(\log n)^2}.\)
  
  Motivated by this, a natural revised question is whether there exists an absolute constant $c>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$,\[
  g_3(n)\le \frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}+c\frac{n}{\log n}.
  \]Finally, as discussed in Section 3 of the note (https://github.com/QuanyuTang/erdos-problem-796-note/blob/main/On_Erd%C5%91s_Problem_796.pdf), Erdős' bound in [Er64d] yields\[
  g_3(n)\le \frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}+O\!\left(\frac{n}{(\log n)^{1+\delta}}\right)
  \]for some $\delta>0$.

- 18:22 on 09 Jan 2026 - Thomas Bloom (reply depth 1):
  Aha, thanks! In fact this construction is very similar to that given by Erdős in [Er64d] when he proved the lower bound of the given asymptotic. That was just taking the set of all products of two primes $p<q$ with\[\frac{n}{\log n}<pq<n\]and\[(\log n)^2 < p < q^{1/4}.\]The number of such pairs Erdős records as\[(1+o(1))\frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}\]but, as you note, it's a classical fact that in fact it's equal to\[\frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}+(M+o(1))\frac{n}{\log n}.\]Your construction is basically this (albeit a little simpler, taking advantage of fixing $k=3$), together with the set of all primes as well. But even just taking the product of two primes already gives a second order term $\gg \frac{n}{\log n}$.
  
  This makes the denominator of $(\log n)^2$ in the problem here strange, since Erdős' construction already disproved it. Perhaps this was a typo in [Er69]? Or maybe he'd miscalculated the asymptotics.
  
  Also, there is something strange in that this two-primes construction lower bound also contradicts the\[g_3(n)\leq \frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}+O\left(\frac{n}{(\log n)^{1+\delta}}\right)\]upper bound you mention here. So is there also an error in this upper bound?
  
  EDIT: Ah, yes, Erdős' proof actually gives an upper bound of\[g_3(n) \leq \frac{n\log\log n}{\log n}+O\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right),\]so he's wrong on the final page where he says the bound you mention. In Erdős' upper bound proof he early on discards those integers which may themselves be primes (which of course may contribute $\asymp \frac{n}{\log n}$ to the count). I think that his bound of $\frac{n}{(\log n)^{1+\delta}}$ is only for the error term after some initial pruning of $A$ (e.g. discarding elements of $A$ which are prime or a prime multiplied by a small integer). Looking at Erdős' upper bound proof I do wonder if just sharpening this up a bit would already give an asymptotic for the second order term.
