# EP-288

## Problem Statement
Is it true that there are only finitely many pairs of intervals $I_1,I_2$ such that\[\sum_{n_1\in I_1}\frac{1}{n_1}+\sum_{n_2\in I_2}\frac{1}{n_2}\in \mathbb{N}?\]

  $$
  c = P_0u_0,\qquad c+1 = P_1u_1,\qquad P_1u_1-P_0u_0=1,
  $$
  where the $P_i$ contain most of the large prime factors, while the $u_i$ are small smooth cofactors.

It did note a survey of Graham from 2013 (https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet/article?mr=3203600) (likely outdated at this point) on this general subject, and a recent paper of Lim and Steinerberger (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11354) showing that a single sum $\sum_{n_1 \in I_1} \frac{1}{n_1}$ can be rather close to $1$, which points towards the problem being difficult.
  
  One small observation: if $N$ is the largest element of $I_1 \cup I_2$ then the length of either interval should be $o(N)$. Otherwise, by the prime number theorem there would be a large prime $p \asymp N$ that only divides $O(1)$ of the elements of $I_1$ and $I_2$ (with bounded cofactors) and it is then easy to see that the sum $\sum_{n_1 \in I_1} \frac{1}{n_1} + \sum_{n_2 \in I_2} \frac{1}{n_2}$ will have a negative $p$-valuation and so cannot be an integer (or a p-adic integer). Indeed the enemy seems to be a dyadic-type interval $I_1 = [\alpha M, M]$ and a very short interval $I_2 = [N-h, N]$ where $N$ is significantly larger than $M$ (perhaps even exponentially larger: many of the primes in $I_1$ will likely also have to divide elements of $I_2$.)

- 15:19 on 01 Feb 2026 - Zeraoulia Rafik:
  I wrote a short note about the “singleton” variant of Erdős Problem 288 (the case $|I_2|=1$), clarifying a useful reduction but not claiming a complete solution of #288.
  
  Main points. If $H(a,b)+1/m\in\mathbb Z$ for some $m$, then necessarily $m$ equals the reduced denominator of $H(a,b)$, so there is at most one candidate $m$ per interval $[a,b]$. For odd primes $p$ with $p^2>b$, a $p$-adic analysis shows a dichotomy: either $T_p\not\equiv 0\pmod p$ and then $v_p(m)=1$ with a forced congruence $m\equiv r_p\pmod{p^2}$, or $T_p\equiv 0\pmod p$ and then $p\nmid m$ (no $p^2$ constraint). Combining the “good” primes by CRT forces any solution to lie in a single residue class $m\equiv r(a,b)\pmod{Q(a,b)}$, with $Q(a,b)=\prod_{p\in P_1(a,b)}p^2$. This reframes the singleton case as a global obstruction problem: show that this forced class contains no $b$-smooth integer in the natural range $m\le \mathrm{lcm}(a,\dots,b)$ for all sufficiently large $b$. I also include computations for $b\le 1600$ (with code) as a consistency check.
  
  Note (preprint): doi:10.13140/RG.2.2.10862.47684/1 (https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.10862.47684/1)

## Local Proof Attempts
The background gives explicit examples where such an integer value occurs.

What remains open is the finiteness question itself, even in the restricted case \(|I_2|=1\).

Target:
Prove finiteness, or else construct infinitude, for interval pairs \((I_1,I_2)\) such that
\[
\sum_{n\in I_1}\frac1n+\sum_{n\in I_2}\frac1n\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Step A: Denominator growth constraints
Express interval harmonic sums in reduced form and track prime-power valuations of their denominators.
Step B: Valuation mismatch forcing
Show that for large interval endpoints, at least one prime valuation in the denominator cannot be canceled across the two interval sums.
Step C: Global finiteness deduction
Conclude that only bounded endpoint ranges can satisfy integrality.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a sharp \(p\)-adic valuation lemma for interval harmonic sums: for large intervals, there is a prime \(p\) with a uniquely minimal valuation term in the block, preventing integer cancellation when adding a second block.

Lemma 1 (Interval harmonic representation):
For interval \(I=[a,b]\),
\[
H(I):=\sum_{n=a}^b \frac1n = \frac{U_{a,b}}{\mathrm{lcm}(a,\dots,b)}
\]
for some integer \(U_{a,b}\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Multiply each \(1/n\) by \(L/\!n\), where \(L=\mathrm{lcm}(a,\dots,b)\). This expresses the sum as integers over the common denominator \(L\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Integrality criterion for a pair):
For disjoint intervals \(I_1,I_2\),
\[
H(I_1)+H(I_2)\in\mathbb Z
\]
if and only if the numerator in the common denominator \(L=\mathrm{lcm}(I_1\cup I_2)\) is divisible by \(L\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Write
\[
H(I_1)+H(I_2)=\frac{A}{L}
\]
with \(A\in\mathbb Z\). The rational number is integral exactly when \(L\mid A\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction to a valuation obstruction):
If one proves that for all sufficiently large interval pairs there exists a prime \(p\) with \(v_p(A)<v_p(L)\) in Lemma 2, then only finitely many integral pairs exist.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Then \(L\nmid A\), so integrality fails for all large pairs. Hence only finitely many exceptions remain.
\(\square\)
