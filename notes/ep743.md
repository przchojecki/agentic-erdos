# EP-743

## Problem Statement
Let $T_2,\ldots,T_n$ be a collection of trees such that $T_k$ has $k$ vertices. Can we always write $K_n$ as the edge disjoint union of the $T_k$?

## Site Remarks
A conjecture of Gy\'{a}rf\'{a}s, known as the tree packing conjecture.

Gy\'{a}rf\'{a}s and Lehel \cite{GyLe78} proved that this holds if all but at most $2$ of the trees are stars, or if all the trees are stars or paths. Fishburn \cite{Fi83} proved this for $n\leq 9$. Bollob\'{a}s \cite{Bo83} proved that the smallest $\lfloor n/\sqrt{2}\rfloor$ many trees can always be packed greedily into $K_n$.

Joos, Kim, K\"{u}hn, and Osthus \cite{JKKO19} proved that this conjecture holds when the trees have bounded maximum degree. Allen, B\"{o}ttcher, Clemens, Hladky, Piguet, and Taraz \cite{ABCHPT21} proved that this conjecture holds when all the trees have maximum degree $\leq c\frac{n}{\log n}$ for some constant $c>0$.

Janzer and Montgomery \cite{JaMo24} have proved that there exists some $c>0$ such that the largest $cn$ trees can be packed into $K_n$.

  Claimed proof (preprint):
  Parikshit Chalise, Antwan Clark, and Edinah K. Gnang posted “A Proof of the Tree Packing Conjecture” (arXiv:2410.13840; v2 dated 23 Oct 2024), which claims to prove Gyárfás’ Tree Packing Conjecture for all $n$.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13840
  
  High-level outline (as stated by the authors):
  The paper introduces a reformulation of tree packing into $K_n$ via “augmented functional trees” and reduces the existence of an edge-disjoint packing of $(T_2,\dots,T_n)$ into $K_n$ to a “complete labeling” problem.
  They define a “tree packing polynomial certificate” $P_g(X,y)=V(X)E_g(X,y)$ and (Proposition 3.4) relate existence of a complete labeling to a non-vanishing condition for a canonical representative of $P_g$.
  A key step is the “Composition Lemma” (Lemma 3.10), asserting that the relevant non-vanishing property is preserved under a local composition operation; iterating this is used to reduce general instances to a star-based instance, yielding Theorem 1.9 and the main claimed result.
