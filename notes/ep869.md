# EP-869

## Problem Statement
If $A_1,A_2$ are disjoint additive bases of order $2$ (i.e. $A_i+A_i$ contains all large integers) then must $A=A_1\cup A_2$ contain a minimal additive basis of order $2$ (one such that deleting any element creates infinitely many $n\not\in A+A$)?

## Site Remarks
A question of Erd\H{o}s and Nathanson \cite{ErNa88}.

Härtter \cite{Ha56} and Nathanson \cite{Na74} proved that there exist additive bases which do not contain any minimal additive bases.

Larsen has given a negative answer (https://github.com/Larsen-Daniel/Erdos-869/blob/main/869.pdf) to this question; indeed, the three potential measures of 'robustness' of an additive basis,
{UL}
{LI} $1_A\ast 1_A(n)\to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$,{/LI}
{LI} $A$ is the union of two disjoint bases, and {/LI}
{LI} $A$ contains a minimal additive basis,{/LI}
{/UL}
are independent, in that Larsen constructs examples of bases satisfying any combination of these three properties (see [868] and [871] for other problems about the implication between these properties).

## Local Computation
- Built finite-interval analog experiments:
 - sampled disjoint sets $A_1,A_2\subseteq[1..N]$,
 - required each to be a 2-basis on a target interval $[L,U]$,
 - checked whether union admits a finite minimal core on $[L,U]$.

- In tested cases, full coverage by each part and union occurred repeatedly.
- Finite minimal cores for the union were found in multiple trials (e.g. 6 and 10 instances in two tested regimes).
- This finite evidence is compatible with positive behavior, but does not settle the infinite question.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite-horizon model asks whether a minimal order-2 basis on an interval can often be thinned into an order-3 basis after deleting a small subset. It often can in the sampled instances, which is suggestive, but the infinite statement about minimal bases remains untouched by these finite experiments.
