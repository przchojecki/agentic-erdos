# EP-962

## Problem Statement
Let $k(n)$ be the maximal $k$ such that there exists $m\leq n$ such that each of the integers\[m+1,\ldots,m+k\]are divisible by at least one prime $>k$. Estimate $k(n)$ - in particular, is it true that\[\log k(n) \leq (\log n)^{1/2+o(1)}?\]

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er65} wrote it is 'not hard to prove' that\[\log k(n)\geq (\log n)^{1/2-o(1)}\]and it 'seems likely' that $k(n)=o(n^\epsilon)$, but had no non-trivial upper bound for $k(n)$. In \cite{Er76e} he gives an argument which proves\[\log k(n) \gg \sqrt{\log n\log\log n},\]and thought that this bound was 'fairly sharp'.

Tao in the comments has given a simple argument proving $k(n) \leq (1+o(1))n^{1/2}$. In \cite{Er76e} Erd\H{o}s reports he could prove that\[k(n) \leq \exp(-(\log n)^c)n^{1/2}\]for some constant $c>0$, but could not prove that $k(n) \leq n^{1/2-c}$ for a constant $c>0$, which he said 'seems a ridiculously weak result'.

Tang has proved (https://github.com/QuanyuTang/erdos-problem-962/blob/main/On_Erd%C5%91s_Problem_962.pdf) a lower bound of\[\log k(n)\geq \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-o(1)\right)\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}.\]

  A possible reformulation that may be worth recording. Setting $K = X^\kappa$ and $W_1(m) := \sum_{i=1}^K \omega_{>K}(m+i)$ for $m \in [X, 2X)$:
  
  Reduction. A bad block of length $2K$ at scale $X$ forces at least $K$ values of $m$ to satisfy $W_1(m) \ge K$. Markov on the fourth central moment gives
  
  $$ |{m : W_1(m) \ge K}| \le M_4(W_1)/(K-\mu)^4 $$
  
  with $\mu = K\log(1/\kappa) + o(K)$ by Mertens. So $M_4(W_1) \ll XK^{2+o(1)}$ uniformly for $\kappa > 1/e$ would yield $k(n) \ll n^{1/e + o(1)}$.
  
  Pairing identity. Writing $W_1 = \sum_{p>K} I_p$ and $f_p = I_p - \lambda_p$, expanding $M_4 = \sum_m (\sum_p f_p)^4$:
  
  $$ M_4(W_1) = 3X \cdot \mathrm{Var}(W_1)^2 + R $$
  
  where $R$ is the all-distinct + three-equal + four-equal cumulant residual. The three-equal and four-equal pieces are $O(XK)$ trivially; the all-distinct piece reduces to a level-4 sieve discrepancy bound (call it L6).
  
  Numerical certification. At $\kappa = 0.34$, across $X \in [10^5, 10^8]$: $M_4/(3X\sigma^4) = 1.00 \pm 0.02$. Kurtosis is exactly 3 over four decades. The standardized $W_1$ converges to standard Gaussian.
  
  The variance gap. The remaining content is $\mathrm{Var}(W_1) \ll K \log^B X$. Equivalently, with $T_{pq} = S_p \times S_q$ (Cartesian product of shifted intervals mod $p, q$) and $R_{pq}$ defined as the count of $m \in [X, 2X)$ with $m \bmod pq \in T_{pq}$, minus $XK^2/(pq)$:
  
  $$ \left| \sum_{K < p \ne q \le 2X} R_{pq} \right| \ll XK \log^B X \quad (\text{L5}) $$
  
  Numerically (sampling 50K of 5M pairs at $X=10^4$, $K=22$): $\sqrt{\langle R_{pq}^2\rangle} \approx 2.8$ vs trivial bound $K^2 = 484$ – already a 200x per-pair improvement. But Cauchy-Schwarz on $\sum R_{pq}^2$ still overshoots the true sum by about 600x. The remaining cancellation is signed across pairs, not magnitudinal – so $L^2$ approaches cannot close L5; only direct bilinear estimates can.
  
  By Fourier expansion:
  
  $$ \sum_{p \ne q} R_{pq} = \sum_{p \ne q} \frac{1}{pq} \sum_{(a,b) \ne 0} A_p(a) , A_q(b) , E_X(a/p + b/q) $$
  
  with $A_p(a) = \sum_{i=1}^K e(ai/p)$ and $E_X(\theta) = \sum_{m=X}^{2X-1} e(m\theta)$. The cancellation lives in the region where $|a/p + b/q|$ is small – additive collisions of fractions with prime denominators. Friedlander-Iwaniec dispersion (Opera de Cribro Ch. 17) appears to be the right tool. L6 is the same machinery at sieve dimension 4.
  
  Across $\kappa \in [0.40, 0.45]$, $\mathrm{Var}(W_1)/(K \rho(1/\kappa)) \approx 1.04$ – the variance constant numerically equals the Dickman function, which is suggestive of a closed form I cannot identify.
  
  I want to be explicit: I do not claim a theorem improving on Erdos’s [Er76e] $k(n) \le \exp(-(\log n)^c) n^{1/2}$. The chain to $n^{1/e+o(1)}$ is rigorous modulo L5 and L6, both of which I can numerically support but cannot prove. I’d value any pointers to existing work that closes either.
  
  Two specific questions:
  
  1. Has anyone applied the polynomial-savings Matomaki-Radziwill-Tao machinery to $f$ equal to the indicator of $K$-smooth integers? Its pretentious distance to 1 is $\sqrt{\log(1/\kappa)}$, so whether MRT gives unconditional polynomial savings here depends on the dependence on $\mathbb{D}(f, 1)$.
  1. The Tao-Teravainen [TaTe25] / Lau [Lau26] machinery handles $\omega(n+k)$ for consecutive integers via Maynard sieve + Pilatte. Does anything in section 5 of TaTe25 (the variance bound for $\sum \omega(n+h)/2^h$) adapt to unweighted sums in our prime range?

## Local Proof Attempts
Formal lemmas from the definition:
Let \(K(n)\) denote the EP-962 quantity (called k(n) above).
Lemma 1 (monotonicity in \(n\)):
If \(n_1\le n_2\), then
\[
K(n_1)\le K(n_2).
\]
Proof:
Any witness block with start \(m\le n_1\) is also admissible for \(n_2\).
Lemma 2 (universal upper bound \(K(n)\le n\)):
For every \(n\ge 1\),
\[
K(n)\le n.
\]
Proof:
Suppose a witness of length \(k\) starts at \(m+1\) with \(m\le n\).
Each term in the block has a prime factor \(>k\), so in particular each term is \(>k\).
Hence the first term satisfies \(m+1>k\), i.e. \(m\ge k\).
Since \(m\le n\), we get \(k\le n\).
Lemma 3 (certificate principle):
If explicit \(m,k\) satisfy
\[
m\le n,\quad \text{and each }m+1,\dots,m+k\text{ has a prime factor } >k,
\]
then \(K(n)\ge k\).
Thus every row in the computation containing a witness block is a rigorous lower-bound certificate for \(K(n)\).
Immediate baseline:
For every \(n\ge 2\),
\[
K(n)\ge 1.
\]
Proof:
Take \(m=1\), \(k=1\). The block is just \(\{2\}\), and \(2\) has a prime factor \(>1\). Since \(m=1\le n\), this is an admissible witness.
So \(K(n)\) is a nondecreasing integer-valued function with
\[
1\le K(n)\le n\quad (n\ge2).
\]
