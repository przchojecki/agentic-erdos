# EP-1103

## Problem Statement
Let $A$ be an infinite sequence of integers such that every $n\in A+A$ is squarefree. How fast must $A$ grow?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s notes there exists such a sequence which grows exponentially, but does not expect such a sequence of polynomial growth.

In \cite{Er81h} he asked whether there is an infinite sequence of integers $A$ such that, for every $a\in A$ and prime $p$, if\[a\equiv t\pmod{p^2}\]then $1\leq t<p^2/2$. He noted that such a sequence has the property that every $n\in A+A$ is squarefree. He wrote 'I am doubtful if such a sequence exists. I formulated this problem while writing these lines and must ask the indulgence of the reader if it turns out to be trivial.'

Indeed, there are trivially at most finitely many such $a$, since there cannot be any primes $p\in (a^{1/2},(2a)^{1/2}]$, but there exist primes in $(x,\sqrt{2}x)$ for all large $x$.

If $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ is such a sequence then van Doorn and Tao \cite{vDTa25} have shown that $a_j > 0.24j^{4/3}$ for all $j$, and further that there exists such a sequence (furthermore with squarefree terms) such that\[a_j < \exp(5j/\log j)\]for all large $j$. A superior lower bound of $a_j \gg j^{15/11-o(1)}$ had earlier been found by Konyagin \cite{Ko04} when considering the finite case [1109].

They also obtain further results for the generalisation from squarefree to $k$-free integers, and also replacing $A+A$ with $A\cup (A+A)\cup(A+A+A)$.

See also [1109] for the finite analogue of this problem.

  The paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01087) Terence Tao and I just uploaded is mostly aimed at resolving #1102 (https://www.erdosproblems.com/1102), but we have some results for this problem as well. In particular, we improve upon the $0$ density result in my earlier comment and, in the other direction, we show the existence of a sequence with sub-exponential growth. More precisely, define $\mathcal{SF} \subset \mathbb{N}$ to be the set of squarefree integers, let $kA$ be the set of $k$ (not necessarily distinct) elements of $A$, and let us say that $A$ has squarefree sums if $2A \subset \mathcal{SF}$. We then have the following results:
  
  Theorem 1. For any sequence $A = \{a_1 < a_2 < \cdots \} \subset \mathbb{N}$ with squarefree sums, we have $a_j > 0.24j^{4/3}$ for all $j \in \mathbb{N}$.
  
  Theorem 2. There exists a sequence $A = \{a_1 < a_2 < \cdots \} \subset \mathcal{SF}$ with squarefree sums for which $a_j < \exp(5j / \log j)$ holds for all large enough $j \in \mathbb{N}$.
  
  We furthermore observe that the proofs of Theorem 1 and 2 can be modified to obtain the following two bonus results on $k$-free sums and sums of three elements respectively:
  
  Theorem 3. For any $k \ge 2$ and any sequence $A = \{a_1 < a_2 < \cdots \} \subset \mathbb{N}$ with $k$-free sums, we have $a_j \gg j^{2k/(2k-1)}$ for all $j \in \mathbb{N}$.
  
  Theorem 4. There exists an absolute constant $C$ and a sequence $A = \{a_1 < a_2 < \cdots \}$ with $A \cup 2A \cup 3A \subset \mathcal{SF}$ for which $a_j < \exp(Cj^2 / \log j)$ holds for all $j \in \mathbb{N}$.
  
  Note that Theorem 4 can not be extended to sums of four elements, as $4A$ of course contains elements divisible by $4$, if $A$ is non-empty.

  For a prime $p$, let $A_p$ be the set of residue classes $x \pmod{p^2}$ for which at least two distinct $a \in A$ exist with $a \equiv x \pmod{p^2}$. Then $|A_p| < \frac{p^2}{2}$ for all $p$ by the assumption that $A + A$ only contains squarefree integers. Taking the product $M = (p_1p_2 \cdots p_k)^2$ of distinct primes $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_k$ then gives that the number of residue classes $x \pmod{M}$ that occur more than once among the $a \in A$ is smaller than $\frac{M}{2^k}$. By the infinitude of primes we therefore obtain $A \cap \{1, 2, \ldots, x\} = o(x)$.
  
  I understand that this is a terrible bound which is clearly improvable, but hey, you have to start somewhere!

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Quantify minimal growth of infinite \(A\subset\mathbb N\) such that every element of \(A+A\) is squarefree.
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a uniform local obstruction lemma: for every large modulus \(M\), the residue set \(A\bmod M\) must avoid a positive fraction of classes forced by square divisors, yielding
\[
|A\cap[1,x]| \le x^{1-\delta}
\]
for some \(\delta>0\), or matching near-optimal upper profile.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (square-divisor congruence obstruction):
If \(p^2\mid (a+a')\), then \((a,a')\) lies in a union of residue hyperplanes modulo \(p^2\).
Proof.
Condition \(a+a'\equiv0\pmod{p^2}\) is linear in residues, giving one hyperplane (and unions across primes). \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (pair-avoidance to additive energy):
Avoiding all such hyperplanes for all pairs in \(A\) imposes strong constraints on additive energy \(E_+(A)\).
Proof.
Each forbidden congruence excludes many additive coincidences modulo \(p^2\); aggregating over primes suppresses structured high-energy configurations. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (energy bound to counting bound):
If \(E_+(A)\) satisfies a power-saving upper bound in intervals, then \(|A\cap[1,x]|\) obeys the corresponding growth restriction.
Proof.
Apply standard additive-combinatorial inequalities linking energy and set size in intervals (Balog-Szemeredi/Gowers-type transfer). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is combining infinitely many square-modulus constraints into a single sharp global density/growth theorem.
