# EP-183

## Problem Statement
Let $R(3;k)$ be the minimal $n$ such that if the edges of $K_n$ are coloured with $k$ colours then there must exist a monochromatic triangle. Determine\[\lim_{k\to \infty}R(3;k)^{1/k}.\]

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s offers \$100 for showing that this limit is finite. An easy pigeonhole argument shows that\[R(3;k)\leq 2+k(R(3;k-1)-1),\]from which $R(3;k)\leq \lceil e k!\rceil$ immediately follows. The best-known upper bound is\[R(3;k)\leq (e-\tfrac{1}{6}) k!+1,\]due to Xu, Xie, and Chen \cite{XXC02} (improving previous bounds of Wan \cite{Wa97} and Whitehead \cite{Wh73}). See Eliahou \cite{El19} for more on the upper bound. These arise from the same type of inductive relationship and computational bounds for $R(3;k)$ for small $k$. The best-known lower bound (coming from lower bounds for Schur numbers) is\[R(3,k)\geq (380)^{k/5}-O(1),\]due to Ageron, Casteras, Pellerin, Portella, Rimmel, and Tomasik \cite{ACPPRT21} (improving previous bounds of Exoo \cite{Ex94} and Fredricksen and Sweet \cite{FrSw00}). Note that $380^{1/5}\approx 3.2806$.

See also [483].

This problem is #21 in Ramsey Theory (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/MulticolorR3.html) in the graphs problem collection.

  Here's a cute related problem that people might be interested in. Let $\chi(n)$ denote the smallest number of sum-free sets that partition $\mathbb{F}_2^n\setminus \{0\}$. Via a subtle recursion combined with the Schur argument, we (with Anurag Bishnoi and Ananth Ravi) have recently (well, the project started 2 years ago) proved that (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01760) for every integer $n\ge 2$,
  $$L:=\lim_{k \rightarrow \infty} R(3;k)^{1/k} \ge 2^{(n-1)/(\chi(n)-1)}.$$ So it's worth trying to find good upper bounds on $\chi(n)$ for small values of $n$. For example, $\chi(8)\le 5$ or $\chi(13)\le 8$ (if true) would already improve the lower bound on $L$ to $2^{7/4} \approx 3.364$ or $2^{12/7}\approx 3.2813$, respectively. Currently, the values of $\chi(n)$ are known for all $n\leq 7$.
  
  Asymptotically, $\frac{(1-o(1)) n}{ \log_2(n)} \le \chi(n)\leq \lfloor \frac{2n}{3} \rfloor +1$.
