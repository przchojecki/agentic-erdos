# EP-776

## Problem Statement
Let $r\geq 2$ and $A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}$ be such that $A_i\not\subseteq A_j$ for all $i\neq j$ and for any $t$ if there exists some $i$ with $\lvert A_i\rvert=t$ then there must exist at least $r$ sets of that size.

How large must $n$ be (as a function of $r$) to ensure that there is such a family which achieves $n-3$ distinct sizes of sets?

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Trotter. For $r=1$ and $n>3$ the maximum possible is $n-2$. For $r>1$ and $n$ sufficiently large $n-3$ is achievable, but $n-2$ is never achievable.

Let $n_0(r)$ be such that whenever $n>n_0(r)$ there exists such a family with $n-3$ distinct set sizes. He and Tang \cite{HeTa26b} (with ChatGPT) have proved that $n_0(2)=3$, $n_0(3)=8$, and for all $r\geq 4$\[2r+2\leq n_0(r)\leq 2r+2\log_2r+O(\log\log r),\]and hence $n_0(r)\sim 2r$ as $r\to \infty$.

- 08:44 on 12 Feb 2026 - Quanyu Tang (reply depth 2):
  Thank you, Zach. This is a very good question, which generalizes the original Erdős--Trotter problem. For integers $\delta\ge 3$ and $r\ge 2$, we define\[
  n_{0}^{(\delta)}(r):=\min\Bigl\{n_0:\ \forall\,n>n_0,\ g(n,r)\ge n-\delta\Bigr\}.
  \]Then one can obtain the following conclusions (proofs can be found in this note (https://github.com/QuanyuTang/ep776-antichains-with-multiplicity/blob/main/To_Hunter_v1.pdf)):
  1. If $\delta\ge 5$, then \(n_{0}^{(\delta)}(r)=O(\sqrt r)=o(r)\). In particular, the constant $C_{\delta}$ in your conjecture is $+\infty$.
  2. When $\delta=4$, one can get a partial result: \(n_{0}^{(4)}(r) \ge \lceil \frac{r + 3}{3} \rceil\) for $r\geq 12$. Therefore, the constant $C_{4}$ in your conjecture lies in the interval $[1/2,\,3]$.
  
  EDIT: I don't have a good guess for the specific value of $C_4$ at the moment, but I'm happy to hear your thoughts and discuss it further.

  Numerical experiments show that for $4 \leq r \leq 10$, we have $n_0(r) \leq 2r + 4$. Thus, I am not sure whether it is possible that for sufficiently large $r$, there exists a constant $C \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ such that $n_0(r) = 2r + C$. I have not yet found a construction that yields $n_0(r) = 2r + O(1)$, but such a construction might exist.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite optimization looks for antichains maximizing the number of realized sizes under the multiplicity constraint \(\ge r\). In the tested range the best constructions stay clearly below the \(n-3\) target, especially for \(r=3\), which is consistent with the eventual threshold occurring only at larger \(n\). The quantitative function \(n(r)\) remains open.
