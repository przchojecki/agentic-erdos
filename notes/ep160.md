# EP-160

## Problem Statement
Let $h(N)$ be the smallest $k$ such that $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ can be coloured with $k$ colours so that every four-term arithmetic progression must contain at least three distinct colours. Estimate $h(N)$.

## Site Remarks
Investigated by Erd\H{o}s and Freud. This has been discussed on MathOverflow (https://mathoverflow.net/questions/410808/what-are-bounds-on-this-van-der-waerden-esque-problem), where LeechLattice shows\[h(N) \ll N^{2/3}.\]In the comments of this site Hunter improves this to\[h(N) \ll N^{\frac{\log 3}{\log 22}+o(1)}\](note $\frac{\log 3}{\log 22}\approx 0.355$).

The observation of Zach Hunter in that question coupled with recent progress on the size of subsets without three-term arithmetic progression (see \cite{BlSi23} which improves slightly on the bounds due to Kelley and Meka \cite{KeMe23}) imply that\[h(N) \gg \exp(c(\log N)^{1/9})\]for some $c>0$.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - Local search found feasible colorings at small scales with empirical upper bounds:
 - $h(20)\le 4$, $h(30)\le 5$, $h(40)\le 6$, $h(60)\le 8$ (no witness found for $N=80$ up to $k=8$ in this run).

 - Finite trend supports growth of $h(N)$, but is too small-scale/noisy to infer exponents.

- Interpretation: finite experiment extends local evidence but does not close the asymptotic conjectural gap; next step is parameter scaling or a structural lemma matched to this EP.

- Proof attempt: if a coloring uses only 2 colors on [1..N], every 4-AP has at most 2 colors, so the condition fails immediately; hence h(N) >= 3 for N >= 4 (trivial baseline).
- Obstacle: extending this to nontrivial asymptotic lower bounds on h(N) needs a global counting/entropy argument over all 4-AP constraints, which is not closed here.
- Deeper computation: ran randomized local search at N=80 with k=4..9 (8 restarts, 10k steps each); no feasible coloring found in this budget.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine asymptotic growth of \(h(N)\) in the 4-AP coloring constraint, closing
the gap between known lower and upper behaviors.
Step A: Hypergraph entropy lower bound
Model colorings as labelings on vertices constrained by 4-AP hyperedges and
derive unavoidable palette growth.
Step B: Constructive upper architecture
Build colorings via AP-free templates and product lifts to keep palette small.
Step C: Exponent identification
Match lower and upper rates to pin the growth exponent of \(h(N)\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a tight counting lemma for feasible colorings under all 4-AP constraints
that yields nontrivial lower bounds on number of colors.

Proposition BH1 (Monotonicity):
The function \(h(N)\) is nondecreasing in \(N\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Restrict any feasible coloring on \([1,N+1]\) to \([1,N]\); feasibility is
preserved.
\(\square\)
Proposition BH2 (Template reduction):
If there exists a family of feasible colorings with
\[
k(N)\le C N^\theta
\]
for infinitely many \(N\), then \(h(N)\le C N^\theta\) along that subsequence.
If matching lower bounds are proved, asymptotic exponent \(\theta\) follows.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By definition, \(h(N)\) is minimal feasible color count.
\(\square\)
