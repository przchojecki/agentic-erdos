# EP-859

## Problem Statement
Let $t\geq 1$ and let $d_t$ be the density of the set of integers $n\in\mathbb{N}$ for which $t$ can be represented as the sum of distinct divisors of $n$.

Do there exist constants $c_1,c_2>0$ such that\[d_t \sim \frac{c_1}{(\log t)^{c_2}}\]as $t\to \infty$?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er70} proved that $d_t$ always exists, and that there exist some constants $c_3,c_4>0$ such that\[\frac{1}{(\log t)^{c_3}} < d_t < \frac{1}{(\log t)^{c_4}}.\]

## Local Computation
Finite density scan for 2<=t<=200 up to N=300000.
Examples (N=300000):
- d_40 ~ 0.3231
- d_80 ~ 0.2798
- d_200 ~ 0.2309

This gives a rigorous baseline plus finite trend data, but not the asymptotic constants/exponents
asked in the problem.

## Local Proof Attempts
Rigorous partial result:
d_t >= 1/t.
Reason: every multiple of t contributes (take the one-term sum {t}).
