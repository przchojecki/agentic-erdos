# EP-425

## Problem Statement
Let $F(n)$ be the maximum possible size of a subset $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ such that the products $ab$ are distinct for all $a<b$. Is there a constant $c$ such that\[F(n)=\pi(n)+(c+o(1))n^{3/4}(\log n)^{-3/2}?\]If $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}$ is such that all products $a_1\cdots a_r$ are distinct for $a_1<\cdots <a_r$ then is it true that\[\lvert A\rvert \leq \pi(n)+O(n^{\frac{r+1}{2r}})?\]

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er68} proved that there exist some constants $0<c_1\leq c_2$ such that\[\pi(n)+c_1 n^{3/4}(\log n)^{-3/2}\leq F(n)\leq \pi(n)+c_2 n^{3/4}(\log n)^{-3/2}.\]This problem can also be considered in the real numbers: that is, what is the size of the the largest $A\subset [1,x]$ such that for any distinct $a,b,c,d\in A$ we have $\lvert ab-cd\rvert \geq 1$? Erd\H{o}s had conjectured (see \cite{Er73} and \cite{Er77c}) that $\lvert A\rvert=o(x)$.

In \cite{ErGr80} Erd\H{o}s and Graham report that Alexander had given a construction disproving this conjecture, establishing that $\lvert A\rvert\gg x$ is possible. Alexander's construction is given in \cite{Er80}, and we sketch a simplified version. Let $B\subseteq [1,X^2]$ be a Sidon set of integers of size $\gg X$ and\[A=\{ X e^{b/X^2} : b\in B\}.\]It is easy to check that\[\lvert ab-cd\rvert \geq X^2\lvert 1-e^{1/X^2}\rvert \gg 1\]for distinct $a,b,c,d\in A$, and (after rescaling $A$ by some constant factor) this produces a set of size $\gg X$ with the desired property in the interval $[X,O(X)]$. Furthermore, a simple modification allows for $A$ to also be $1$-separated.

In \cite{Er77c} Erd\H{o}s considers a similar generalisation for sets of complex numbers or complex integers.

See also [490], [793], and [796].

  Interesting result. Can you describe the construction in some detail, please? I have been thinking about this problem in recent weeks and I only got $c\approx 1.703$ with a rather convoluted adjustment to Erdős's 1938 construction. Here is a sketch of a simple argument which gives a lower bound with $c=2^{3/4}\approx 1.681$, based on Erdős's 1938 paper :
  Take sets $A_1,A_2$ formed respectively of the primes below $\sqrt{n/2}$, and between $\sqrt{n/2}$ and $\sqrt{2n}$, so that $A_1 A_2 \subset \{1, \cdots,n\}$. By the PNT, these sets have approximately equal cardinality $M=\dfrac{\sqrt{2n}}{\log n}$. We form a projective plane of order $\approx M$ on $A_1$, and to each element $q$ of $A_2$ we associate a line of this plane, then form the products $pq$ for the $\approx\sqrt{M}$ primes $p$ on this line. There are $\approx M^{3/2}=\dfrac{2^{3/4}n^{3/4}}{(\log n)^{3/2}}$ such products in total, to which we append the primes above $\sqrt{2n}$. The set so constructed has cardinality about $\pi(n)+\dfrac{2^{3/4}n^{3/4}}{(\log n)^{3/2}}$ and has distinct products.

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Greedy constructions provide lower bounds only and do not identify the asymptotic constant or matching upper structure.

Target:
Determine asymptotic size and constant for maximal multiplicative Sidon-type sets \(A\subset[1,n]\).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a structural decomposition theorem: every near-extremal \(A\) splits into
1) a prime-dominated part and
2) a sparse composite correction,
with correction size bounded by \(C n^{3/4}(\log n)^{-3/2}(1+o(1))\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (injective product constraint):
Multiplicative Sidon condition is equivalent to injectivity of map \((a,b)\mapsto ab\) on unordered pairs from \(A\).
Proof.
By definition no nontrivial equality \(a_1a_2=b_1b_2\) among distinct unordered pairs is allowed. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (energy upper relation):
Injectivity in B1 forces multiplicative energy \(E_\times(A)\) near its diagonal minimum.
Proof.
\(E_\times(A)\) counts product collisions; Sidon-type condition suppresses off-diagonal contributions. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies asymptotic constant program):
If A identifies all near-extremal corrections with sharp bound, then maximal \(|A|\) asymptotics and constant follow.
Proof.
Combine prime baseline term with bounded correction asymptotic from A. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is proving universal upper structure on composite corrections matching known lower constructions.
