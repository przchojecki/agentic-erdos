# EP-574

## Problem Statement
Is it true that, for $k\geq 2$,\[\mathrm{ex}(n;\{C_{2k-1},C_{2k}\})=(1+o(1))(n/2)^{1+\frac{1}{k}}.\]

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Simonovits.

This has been disproved a number of times. It appears to have been first disproved by Lazebnik, Ustimenko, and Woldar \cite{LUW94b} for $k=3$ and $5$, since they construct bipartite graphs containing no $C_{2k}$ with\[\left(\frac{k-1}{k^{1+\frac{1}{k}}}+o(1)\right)n^{1+\frac{1}{k}}\]edges. (Note that the constant here for $k=3$ and $5$ is $\approx 0.462$ and $\approx 0.579$ respectively, while this problem predicts $\approx 0.396$ and $\approx 0.435$.)

An alternative disproof for $k=3$ is given by F\"{u}redi, Naor, and Verstra\"{e}te \cite{FNV06}, who prove that the extremal number for an $m\times 2m$ bipartite graph containing $C_6$ is (with $n=3m$)\[(2/3^{4/3}+o(1))n^{4/3}.\]See also [572], [573] for the specific case of $k=2$, and [1080].

This problem is #49 in Extremal Graph Theory (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/TuranConsecutiveCycles.html) in the graphs problem collection.

## Local Computation
- Implemented standalone randomized-greedy constructions for \(\{C_{2k-1},C_{2k}\}\)-free graphs.
- Measured finite-\(n\) ratios against \((n/2)^{1+1/k}\) for \(k=2,3\).
- This gives quantitative finite evidence but no full asymptotic proof.

- Deep run (\(\sim 21.8\)s) yields ratios near:
 - \(k=2\): from \(1.13\) down to \(1.06\) as \(n\) grows.
 - \(k=3\): from \(1.48\) down to \(1.37\) as \(n\) grows.
- The downward trend is consistent with finite-size relaxation toward the conjectural constant.
