# EP-1163

## Problem Statement
Describe (by statistical means) the arithmetic structure of the orders of subgroups of $S_n$.

  What's the right interpretation? There are at least three natural readings:
  
  1) Divisor-statistical reading. Study the set\[
   O_n=\{|H|:H\leq S_n\}
  \]as a subset of the divisor set $D_n=\{d:d\mid n!\}$.
  
  2) Uniform-subgroup reading. Choose $H$ uniformly from the finite set $\textrm{Sub}(S_n)$, and study the random variable $|H|$, or its invariants $\log |H|$, $v _p(|H|)$, $\omega(|H|)$, and so on.
  
  3) Random-generation reading. Choose random permutations $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r\in S_n$ and study\[
   |\langle \sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r\rangle|.
  \]

  I would probably interpret this as distribution over all subgroups of S_n. Perhaps it is asking something along the lines of "Let $Sub(S_n)$ be the set of all subgroups of $S_n$. Choose a subgroup uniformly at random from this finite set. Then treat $|H|$ as a random variable. What are its (statistical) properties as $n$ grows?
  
  Based off of this interpretation there are some relatively natural questions like, what is the typical siez of $|H|$ relative to $n!$, what is the distribution of $log (|H|)$, how often is $|H|$ divisible by a fixed prime $p$, what is the typical p-adic valuation $v_p(|H|)$ or what is the typical number of distinct prime divisors of $|H|$.
  Just a few questions that poppde into my head.
