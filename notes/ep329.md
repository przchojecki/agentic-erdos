# EP-329

## Problem Statement
Suppose $A\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is a Sidon set. How large can\[\limsup_{N\to \infty}\frac{\lvert A\cap \{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert}{N^{1/2}}\]be?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s proved that $1/2$ is possible and Kr\"{u}ckeberg \cite{Kr61} proved $1/\sqrt{2}$ is possible. Erd\H{o}s and Tur\'{a}n \cite{ErTu41} have proved this $\limsup$ is always $\leq 1$. In \cite{Er80} he writes that he and Kr\"{u}ckeberg conjecture that $1$ is possible.

The fact that $1$ is possible would follow if any finite Sidon set is a subset of a perfect difference set (see [44] and [707]).

This question can also be asked for $B_2[g]$ sequences (i.e. where the number of solutions to $n=a_1+a_2$ with $a_1\leq a_2$ is at most $g$ for all $n$, so that a $B_2[1]$ set is a Sidon set). Kolountzakis \cite{Ko96} constructed a $B_2[2]$ sequence where the $\limsup$ is $1$, and for larger $g$ constructions were provided by Cilleruelo and Trujillo \cite{CiTr01}.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - Greedy Sidon construction gives |A\cap[1,N]|/\sqrt N declining from about 0.71 (N=5000) to 0.47 (N=200000).

 - Greedy finite constructions are suboptimal for limsup extraction; no evidence toward the extremal constant.

- Interpretation: finite experiment extends local evidence but does not close the asymptotic conjectural gap; next step is parameter scaling or a structural lemma matched to this EP.
- Finite signal: randomized affine-order greedy did not beat natural-order greedy in this range (gains negative by 2 to 7 elements), with best ratio near 0.386 at N=8e5.
- Proof direction update: this reinforces that naive order-randomization is weak for limsup optimization; stronger algebraic constructions (finite-field/Bose-Chowla style) should be the next computational baseline.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine the exact constant in
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(N)}{\sqrt N},
\]
where \(F(N)\) is maximal Sidon subset size of \([1,N]\).
Step A: Construction side sharpening
Transfer finite-field constructions to intervals with minimal loss.
Step B: Upper-bound side sharpening
Improve additive-energy counting beyond classical pair-difference bound.
Step C: Constant matching
Close the gap between lower and upper constants.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need an interval-transfer lemma with \(o(\sqrt N)\) loss from algebraic Sidon
sets in cyclic groups to \([1,N]\).

Lemma 1 (Difference counting upper bound):
If \(A\subset[1,N]\) is Sidon and \(|A|=m\), then
\[
\binom m2 \le N-1.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
All positive differences \(a-b\) (\(a>b\), \(a,b\in A\)) are distinct in a Sidon
set. These differences lie in \(\{1,\dots,N-1\}\), giving at most \(N-1\) values.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Classical asymptotic upper estimate):
From Lemma 1,
\[
m\le \frac{1+\sqrt{1+8(N-1)}}{2}=\sqrt{2N}+O(1).
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Solve quadratic inequality \(m(m-1)\le 2(N-1)\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Constant extraction reduction):
If one proves lower constructions with
\[
F(N)\ge c_1\sqrt N-o(\sqrt N)
\]
and upper bound \(F(N)\le c_2\sqrt N+o(\sqrt N)\), then limsup constant lies in
\([c_1,c_2]\); matching \(c_1=c_2\) resolves EP-329.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Divide by \(\sqrt N\), take limsup/liminf bounds.
\(\square\)
