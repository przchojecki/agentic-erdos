# EP-361

## Problem Statement
Let $c>0$ and $n$ be some large integer. What is the size of the largest $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,\lfloor cn\rfloor\}$ such that $n$ is not a sum of a subset of $A$? Does this depend on $n$ in an irregular way?

  the construction
  $\{ 1 \le x \le \lfloor cn \rfloor \colon 5 \mid x \vee (x \equiv r \pmod 5 \wedge x>n/4) \vee (x \equiv 2r \pmod 5 \wedge x>n/2) \}$ nevertheless is of size $0.3n + O(1)$ and thus larger.
  The latter construction also works for any other smallest prime $q$ not dividing $n$, so the exact form seems to depend on both the prime factorization of $n$ and the size of $n$.

  If $c\ge 1$, then the maximum is trivially $(c-1/2)n.$
  Take all $m$ for which either $n\le 2m<2n$ or $m>n$.
  (Sharpness by considering pairs $\{x,n-x\}$)
  
  For $1/2<c<1$, the maximum is bounded by $cn/2+2.$
  For this consider pairs $\{x,n-x\}$, find 2 elements with sum $(1-c)n-1$ and then $2$ other ls with sum $cn+1.$
  
  Possible constructions:
  
  For $1/b\le c<1/(b-1)$,
  One can take those $n/b \le n <n/(b-1)$ and a few more or $n/(b+1) \le m \le n/b$ + some (a max set such that the sum of b eleements too large and of b-1 is too small and similar).
  
  One can also take the smallest prime power $q \nmid n$ and take the multiples of $q$ (and a few more without sumset equal to $n \mod q$).
  
  If the maximum would be the maximum among the ones obtained from size-constrant and modulo-constraint, similar to Erdos matching conjecture, it indeed depends on $n$ in a (periodic) irregular way.

- 01:04 on 09 Oct 2025 - Adenwalla:
  If $p$ is the smallest prime that does not divide $n$, then you can take $A$ to be the multiples of $p$ so $|A|=\lfloor \frac{\lfloor cn\rfloor}{p}\rfloor $.
  
  Let $c\leq 1$. For an integer $k\geq 1$, the set $A=[\frac{n}{k+1},\frac{n}{k}]\cap\mathbb{N}$ always satisfies the condition. So letting $t=\lceil \frac{1}{c}\rceil$, we have $|A|\geq \max\{(c-\frac{1}{t})n, \frac{n}{t(t+1)}\}$.
