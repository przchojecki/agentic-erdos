# EP-152

## Problem Statement
For any $M\geq 1$, if $A\subset \mathbb{N}$ is a sufficiently large finite Sidon set then there are at least $M$ many $a\in A+A$ such that $a+1,a-1\not\in A+A$.

## Site Remarks
There may even be $\gg \lvert A\rvert^2$ many such $a$. A similar question can be asked for truncations of infinite Sidon sets.

This is true; in fact there are at least $(1-o(1))\lvert A+A\rvert$ many such $a\in A+A$. A proof of (a slightly weaker statement) was found by DeepMind using an elementary argument. Independently, Bloom noted (see the comments) that in fact this strong statement already follows from an argument in \cite{ESS94}, which shows that, for any $d\neq 0$,\[\lvert (A+A)\cap (A+A+d)\rvert \leq 2\lvert A\rvert^{3/2}.\]

  Here’s an attempt to summarize the proof informally:
  
  For a Sidon set $A \subset \mathbb{N}$ of size $n$, let $I(A+A)$ count the isolated elements of the sumset — those $s \in A+A$ with $s \pm 1 \notin A+A$. Define $f(n)$ as the minimum of $I(A+A)$ over all Sidon sets of size $n$.
  
  Theorem. $f(n) \geq (n^2 - 100n - 16)/16$, so in particular $f(n) \to \infty$.
  
  The proof establishes, for every Sidon set $A$ of size $n$:
  $$16 \cdot I(A+A) + 100n + 16 \geq n^2. \qquad (\star)$$
  We denote $N_k(X) := |{x \in X : x+k \in X}|$, $V_2(X) := |{x \in X : x \pm 1 \in X}|$ (interior points),
  and $I(X)$ for the isolated points of $X$.
  
  Let $X \subset \mathbb{Z}$. We will employ the following three facts:
  
  1. $I(X) + 2N_1(X) = |X| + V_2(X)$, by partitioning elements by neighbor count.
  2. (Indicator Inequality) $4N_1(X) + N_3(X) \leq 3|X| + 2N_2(X)$, proved by verifying a pointwise inequality on $\{0,1\}$-valued indicator functions and summing.
  3. $2N_2(X) \leq N_3(X) + 2V_2(X) + 2I(X)$, obtained by splitting the "distance-2" pairs into those bridged by $x+1 \in X$ (counted by $V_2$) and gap patterns ${x, x+2} \subset X$ with $x+1 \notin X$, then bounding the latter via boundary analysis.
  
  Next, for each $k \geq 1$, count quadruples $(a,b,c,d) \in A^4$ with $a + b + k = c + d$. Rewriting as $a - c = d - b - k$ and partitioning by $\delta = a - c$ yields the following two quadruple transfer bounds:
  
  4. The cases $\delta = 0$ and $\delta = -k$ each contribute $\leq |A|$ (one coordinate determines the rest). All other $\delta$ inject into ${x \in D : x+k \in D}$ where $D = A - A$, by the Sidon property. Total: $|\text{quad}_k| \leq N_k(D) + 2n$.
  5. Each "good" element $s \in S$ with $s + k \in S$ (excluding $\leq 2n$ doubles of the form $2a$) produces $\geq 4$ quadruples. Total: $4N_k(S) \leq |\text{quad}_k| + 8n$.
  
  Combining these bounds shows that $N_k(D)$ and $N_k(S)$ are related up to $O(n)$ error.
  
  Next, apply the indicator inequality (2) to $D = A - A$ and substitute the quadruple transfer bounds (4, 5) for $k = 1, 2, 3$, then use identities (1) and (3) on $\bar{S}$. A key cancellation occurs: the $N_3$, $V_2$, and $N_1$ terms all eliminate, leaving
  $$16,I(\bar{S}) + 100n \geq 8|S| - 3|D|.$$
  The standard Sidon estimates $|S| \geq n^2/2$ and $|D| \leq n^2$ then give $16I + 100n \geq 4n^2 - 3n^2 = n^2$, accounting for a boundary correction of $+16$ at zero yields $(\star)$. $\blacksquare$
  
  That also clearly answers the question “There may even be $\gg \lvert A\rvert^2$ many such $a$.”, and we formalised the proof of that (https://github.com/mo271/formal-conjectures/blob/ff58c933d53bb807bf85d98a47402703f9f14ed3/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/152.lean#L496) by hand.

- 12:58 on 03 Apr 2026 - Thomas Bloom (reply depth 1):
  Nice! By a strange coincidence I was looking at this yesterday, and think that in fact a positive answer already follows from the methods in [ESS94], although they did not seem to notice this.
  
  I'm not sure why they asked this question given that their methods could already settle it; they seem to have been mainly thinking about infinite Sidon sets, so perhaps they missed this. (Or, of course, perhaps I am mistaken!)
  
  Let $A$ be a finite Sidon set of size $n$. If one examines the proof of their Theorem 2 (in fact the simpler finite case) this shows that that, for any $d\neq 0$\[\lvert (A+A)\cap (A+A+d)\rvert\leq 2n^{3/2}.\]In particular the number of $x\in A+A$ such that either $x+1\in A+A$ or $x-1\in A+A$ is at most $4n^{3/2}$, and so\[ I(A+A)\geq \lvert A+A\rvert-4n^{3/2}.\](Which is $\gg n^2$ since $\lvert A+A\rvert =\binom{n}{2}+n$.)
  
  This is a stronger bound than the DeepMind bound (i.e. $I(A+A)\geq (1/2+o(1))n^2$ rather than $I(A+A)\geq (1/16+o(1))n^2$), although since both proofs are short and elementary I expect that the DeepMind proof can be upgraded to give a similar asymptotic.
  
  The proof from [ESS94] does use a small amount of Fourier analysis; the DeepMind proof seems to be distinct in that it is purely elementary, using instead a surprising coincidence of cancelling parameters to get a comparable bound.

## Local Computation
Exact enumeration for Sidon sets with \(\min(A)=1\) and \(10\le N\le 60\) gives the following global minima for the number of isolated sums in \(A+A\):
- \(m=2\mapsto 0\), \(m=3\mapsto 1\), \(m=4\mapsto 2\), \(m=5\mapsto 2\), \(m=6\mapsto 3\), \(m=7\mapsto 5\), \(m=8\mapsto 5\), \(m=9\mapsto 8\), \(m=10\mapsto 12\).
A representative size-10 minimizer is
\[
A=\{1,2,7,11,24,27,35,42,54,56\},
\]
for which the isolated-count is \(12\).
Two standard infinite families, the Ruzsa prime family and the Mian-Chowla greedy family, both show rapidly increasing isolated-count in the tested range. Adversarial searches at larger \(m\) also remain strongly superlinear: the best counts found are \(18,82,228,455,764,1156,1640,2206\) for \(m=11,20,30,40,50,60,70,80\), and the extension to \(m=81,90,100\) gives \(2256,2870,3614\). Finite evidence therefore suggests roughly quadratic growth, although this is not a proof.

## Local Proof Attempts
Reframing:
Let A+A = {s_1 < ... < s_t} and define gaps g_i = s_{i+1}-s_i.
An element s_i is "isolated" iff both adjacent gap constraints hold:
- left gap > 1 (or i=1)
- right gap > 1 (or i=t)
So EP-152 asks whether the minimum possible isolated-count over Sidon sets of size m
must go to infinity with m.
Equivalent run-language:
- View A+A inside [s_1,s_t] as runs of consecutive occupied integers.
- Isolated sums are exactly runs of length 1.

Why this is still open:
Current obstacle is universal control: we do not have a structural theorem preventing
almost all missing points of A+A from clustering into a few long hole intervals, which
could keep isolated-count comparatively low.

Target:
For Sidon \(A\), prove the number of isolated elements in \(A+A\) tends to
infinity with \(|A|\).
Step A: Gap-mass lower bound
Show total excess gap
\[
\sum_i\bigl((s_{i+1}-s_i)-1\bigr)
\]
must grow with \(|A|\) for all Sidon sets.
Step B: Anti-clustering of excess gaps
Prove excess gap mass cannot concentrate on too few locations.
Step C: Isolated-run forcing
Translate dispersed excess gaps into many singleton runs, i.e. many isolated sums.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a universal anti-clustering inequality for gaps of \(A+A\) for arbitrary
Sidon \(A\), not only structured families.

Proposition BB1 (Run decomposition identity):
Let \(R_1\) be number of singleton runs in \(A+A\), and \(x_i=(s_{i+1}-s_i)-1\ge0\).
Then
\[
R_1 \ge \#\{i:\ x_i>0\}-1.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Each positive gap separates consecutive occupied blocks; except for boundary
effects, each separator increases run count by one.
\(\square\)
Proposition BB2 (Reduction):
If one proves \(\#\{i:\ x_i>0\}\to\infty\) for all Sidon sets with \(|A|\to\infty\),
then EP-152 follows by BB1.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate.
\(\square\)
