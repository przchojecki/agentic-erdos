# EP-1016

## Problem Statement
Let $h(n)$ be minimal such that there is a graph on $n$ vertices with $n+h(n)$ edges which contains a cycle on $k$ vertices, for all $3\leq k\leq n$. Estimate $h(n)$. In particular, is it true that\[h(n) \geq \log_2n+\log_*n-O(1),\]where $\log_*n$ is the iterated logarithmic function?

## Site Remarks
Such graphs are called pancyclic. A problem of Bondy \cite{Bo71}, who claimed a proof (without details) of\[\log_2(n-1)-1\leq h(n) \leq \log_2n+\log_*n+O(1).\]Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er71} believed the upper bound is closer to the truth, but could not even prove $h(n)-\log_2n\to \infty$.

A proof of the above lower bound is provided by Griffin \cite{Gr13}. The first published proof of the upper bound appears to be in Chapter 4.5 of George, Khodkar, and Wallis \cite{GKW16}.

  Bondy's paper actually claims the slightly weaker bounds $\log_2 (n-1) - 1 \leq h(n) \leq \log_2 n + \log_* n + O(1)$, contrary to what Erdos writes, but with no proof given for either inequality. An actual proof of the lower bound was only provided in 2013 by Bi-George-Rui (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.0274), though this 2023 paper of Alon and Krivelevich (https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~krivelev/pancyclic-rg.pdf) suggests that a equivalent statement was established by Shi in 1994 (https://pdf.sciencedirectassets.com/271536/1-s2.0-S0012365X00X02312/1-s2.0-0012365X94900310/main.pdf) (but it is difficult for me to confirm this as the notation is somewhat different). An obscure 1996 paper of Jia (https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet/article?mr=1431994) (which I was not able to directly obtain, but could see their results mentioned in this 2014 survey of Lai and Liu (https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%20091/vol-091-paper%204.pdf)) establishes $h(n) \leq \frac{3}{2} \log_2 n + 1$ for all $n$ and $h(n) \leq \log_2 n + \log_2 \log_2 n + O(1)$ for all sufficiently large $n$, and also makes the conjecture #1016. The first literature proof of the upper bound $h(n) \leq \log_2 n + \log_* n + O(1)$ that seems to exist is in Chapter 4.5 of this 2016 book of George, Khodkar, and Wallis (https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet/article?mr=3495006). The consensus seems to be that Bondy's bounds, which are now proven in the literature, remain the state of the art except for very small $n$ where there is some work by George, Marr, and Wallis (https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%20086/vol-086-paper%207.pdf).

## Local Computation
- Implemented standalone random witness search with exact cycle-length verification:
 - random $n$-vertex graphs with $m=n+h$ edges,
 - exact detection of present cycle lengths,
 - pancyclic check requiring all lengths $3,\dots,n$,
 - first successful $h$ per $n$ recorded.

- In this run, witness excesses found were:
 - $h(8)\le2$,
 - $h(10)\le3$,
 - $h(12)\le4$,
 - $h(14)\le6$.
- These are finite constructive upper bounds only; absence of a witness at smaller $h$ in random search is not a lower bound proof.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine minimal excess \(h(n)\) (above \(n\) edges) needed for pancyclic behavior in EP-1016 formulation.
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a robust expansion lemma: if \(e(G)\ge n+h\) and every proper induced subgraph has average degree below a threshold, then \(G\) contains cycles in a full interval of lengths whose span is \(\gg h\), eventually covering \(3,\dots,n\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (pancyclicity criterion):
If \(G\) contains cycles of every length \(3,\dots,n\), then \(G\) is pancyclic by definition.
Proof.
Immediate from definition. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (edge-excess to average degree):
\[
e(G)=n+h\quad\Rightarrow\quad \bar d(G)=2+\frac{2h}{n}.
\]
Proof.
Average degree equals \(2e(G)/n\). Substitute \(e(G)\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies asymptotic upper bounds on \(h(n)\)):
If A turns excess \(h\) into complete cycle-length coverage for large \(n\), then \(h(n)\) is bounded above by that threshold function.
Proof.
Any graph meeting the threshold satisfies pancyclic conclusion by A; minimal forcing excess is at most that value. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The key gap is converting moderate global edge excess into guaranteed full cycle-spectrum coverage rather than long but incomplete intervals of lengths.
