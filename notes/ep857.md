# EP-857

## Problem Statement
Let $m=m(n,k)$ be minimal such that in any collection of sets $A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}$ there must exist a sunflower of size $k$ - that is, some collection of $k$ of the $A_i$ which pairwise have the same intersection.

Estimate $m(n,k)$, or even better, give an asymptotic formula.

## Site Remarks
Related to [536] and [856]. In \cite{Er70} Erd\H{o}s asks this in the equivalent formulation with intersection replaced by union.

This is sometimes known as the weak sunflower problem (see [20] for the strong sunflower problem).

When $k=3$ this is strongly connected to the cap set problem (finding the maximal size of subsets of $\mathbb{F}_3^n$ with no three-term arithmetic progressions), as observed by Alon, Shpilka, and Umans \cite{ASU13}). Naslund and Sawin \cite{NaSa17} have proved that\[m(n,3) \leq (3/2^{2/3})^{(1+o(1))n}.\]

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite \(k=3\) proxy checks whether large local representation counts tend to coexist with finite minimal basis cores on an interval. They often do in the sampled windows, which is the right qualitative sign, but this falls far short of proving the infinite \(k\ge 3\) statement.
