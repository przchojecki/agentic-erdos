# EP-454

## Problem Statement
Let\[f(n) = \min_{i<n} (p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}),\]where $p_k$ is the $k$th prime. Is it true that\[\limsup_n (f(n)-2p_n)=\infty?\]

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Finite growth of maxima is suggestive but does not prove infinite limsup.

Target:
Show
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\bigl(f(n)-2p_n\bigr)=\infty,\quad
f(n)=\min_{i<n}(p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}).
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove existence of infinitely many indices \(n\) with a long local prime-gap desert around \(p_n\) in both directions, forcing all pair-sums \(p_{n-i}+p_{n+i}\) to exceed \(2p_n+M\) for arbitrarily large \(M\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (pair-sum expansion):
\[
p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}-2p_n
 =
(p_{n+i}-p_n)-(p_n-p_{n-i}).
\]
Proof.
Algebraic rearrangement. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (minimum control):
If for all \(1\le i<n\), both side gaps satisfy
\[
p_{n+i}-p_n\ge M_i,\quad p_n-p_{n-i}\le M_i-M,
\]
then \(p_{n+i}+p_{n-i}\ge2p_n+M\), hence \(f(n)-2p_n\ge M\).
Proof.
Insert inequalities into B1 and take minimum over \(i\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies limsup divergence):
If A provides such \(n\) for arbitrarily large \(M\), then \(\limsup(f(n)-2p_n)=\infty\).
Proof.
By definition of limsup using arbitrarily high attained values. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is proving two-sided prime-gap asymmetry patterns strong enough to force large minima of pair-sums.
