# EP-234

## Problem Statement
For every $c\geq 0$ the density $f(c)$ of integers for which\[\frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{\log n}< c\]exists and is a continuous function of $c$.

## Local Computation
Finite numerical profiles were computed for the candidate distribution function
\[
f(c)=\lim_x \mathbf P\!\left(\frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{\log n}<c\right)
\]
at scales up to \(x=10^6\).

The empirical CDFs at \(x=2\cdot 10^5\), \(5\cdot 10^5\), and \(10^6\) were fairly stable at coarse resolution and showed no obvious discontinuity spikes beyond grid noise.

These computations are only consistency checks; they cannot prove existence or continuity of the asymptotic density function.

## Local Proof Attempts
The question is whether the normalized prime-gap distribution admits a genuine limiting density function \(f(c)\) for every \(c\ge 0\), and whether that function is continuous.

Target:
Prove existence and continuity properties of the normalized prime-gap distribution function \(f(c)\).
Step A: Normalization and tightness
Show the empirical distributions of normalized gaps are tight and admit subsequential limits.
Step B: Uniqueness of the limiting law
Prove all subsequential limits coincide.
Step C: Continuity
Upgrade the limiting law to continuity in \(c\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform estimate on adjacent prime-gap counts in short intervals to pass
from subsequential convergence to full convergence and continuity.

Proposition CO1 (CDF compactness principle):
Any sequence of empirical distribution functions \(F_X(c)\) is relatively compact
in the weak topology if tightness holds.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By Prokhorov or Helly selection for probability measures on \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}\).
\(\square\)
Proposition CO2 (Uniqueness reduction):
If every subsequential weak limit has the same Laplace transform on an interval,
then the limiting distribution is unique; hence \(F_X(c)\to f(c)\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Laplace transform uniqueness identifies probability measures.
\(\square\)
