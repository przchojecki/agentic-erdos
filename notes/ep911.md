# EP-911

## Problem Statement
Let $\hat{R}(G)$ denote the size Ramsey number, the minimal number of edges $m$ such that there is a graph $H$ with $m$ edges that is Ramsey for $G$.

Is there a function $f$ such that $f(x)/x\to \infty$ as $x\to \infty$ such that, for all large $C$, if $G$ is a graph with $n$ vertices and $e\geq Cn$ edges then\[\hat{R}(G) > f(C) e?\]

## Local Computation
- family computation on dense graphs $G=K_t$:
 - computes $C=(t-1)/2$ and $e=\binom t2$;
 - converts Ramsey bounds into explicit lower/upper bounds on
 $\hat{R}(K_t)/e$ via $\hat{R}(K_t)=\binom{R(t,t)}2$;
 - tracks growth of $\bigl(\hat{R}(K_t)/e\bigr)/C$.

Deep run (DEPTH=5, TMAX=80) finds rapid growth in the clique family:
- already for moderate $t$, lower bounds force $\hat{R}(K_t)/e$ to dominate $C$ strongly;
- at $t=80$ ($C=39.5$), the lower-bound ratio satisfies
\[
\frac{\hat{R}(K_t)}{e} \gtrsim 1.9\times 10^{20},
\qquad
\frac{\hat{R}(K_t)/e}{C}\gtrsim 4.8\times10^{18}.
\]
This supports a strong positive trend on one dense family but does not resolve the
full EP-911 quantifier over all dense graphs.

## Local Proof Attempts
The present evidence comes from one dense family only, namely cliques, where classical Ramsey bounds force the size-Ramsey ratio \(\hat{R}(K_t)/e(K_t)\) to grow enormously compared with the edge-density parameter \(C\). That strongly supports the desired phenomenon on a benchmark family, but the full statement quantifies over all sufficiently dense graphs, so the general problem remains open.
