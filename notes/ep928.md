# EP-928

## Problem Statement
Let $\alpha,\beta\in (0,1)$ and let $P(n)$ denote the largest prime divisor of $n$. Does the density of integers $n$ such that $P(n)<n^{\alpha}$ and $P(n+1)<(n+1)^\beta$ exist?

## Site Remarks
Dickman \cite{Di30} showed the density of smooth $n$, with largest prime factor $<n^\alpha$, is $\rho(1/\alpha)$ where $\rho$ is the Dickman function (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickman_function).

Erd\H{o}s also asked whether infinitely many such $n$ even exist, but Meza has observed that this follows immediately from Schinzel's result \cite{Sc67b} that for infinitely many $n$ the largest prime factor of $n(n+1)$ is at most $n^{O(1/\log\log n)}$.

Erd\H{o}s asked whether the events $P(n)<n^\alpha$ and $P(n+1)<(n+1)^\beta$ are independent, in the sense that the density of $n$ satisfying both conditions is equal to $\rho(1/\alpha)\rho(1/\beta)$.

Ter\"{a}v\"{a}inen \cite{Te18} has proved the logarithmic density exists and is equal to $\rho(1/\alpha)\rho(1/\beta)$.

Wang \cite{Wa21} has proved the density is $\rho(1/\alpha)\rho(1/\beta)$ assuming the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture for friable integers.

In \cite{Er76e} Erd\H{o}s conjectured that the density of the set of $n$ such that all of $n,\ldots,n+r-1$ are $n^{\alpha}$ smooth (i.e. $P(n+i)\leq n^{\alpha}$ for $0\leq i<r$) is $\rho(1/\alpha)^r$.

See also [370].

## Local Computation
- deep empirical scan with:
 - sieve-based exact largest-prime-factor table up to $N=30{,}000{,}000$,
 - dense $(\alpha,\beta)$ grid (81 pairs),
 - checkpoint convergence at $N=5,10,20,30$ million.
- Recorded joint density, product of marginals, covariance surrogate $(\text{joint}-\text{product})$, and ratio joint/product.

- Runtime for this deeper run: about $6.2$ seconds.
- Persistent mild negative correlation across tested pairs: joint density stays below product density.
- Example at $N=30{,}000{,}000$:
 - $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.5,0.5)$: joint/product $\approx 0.9263$.
 - $(0.7,0.7)$: joint/product $\approx 0.9851$.
 - $(0.8,0.8)$: joint/product $\approx 0.9903$.
- As thresholds become less restrictive, ratio approaches $1$ from below, consistent with near-independence at higher smoothness exponents.

## Local Proof Attempts
The empirical density scan asks whether the joint smoothness event for \(n\) and \(n+1\) behaves approximately like a product of marginals. The answer in finite data is 'almost, but not quite': there is mild persistent negative correlation. That is informative about the arithmetic dependence, but not enough to prove existence of a natural density.
