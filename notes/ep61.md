# EP-61

## Problem Statement
For any graph $H$ is there some $c=c(H)>0$ such that every graph $G$ on $n$ vertices that does not contain $H$ as an induced subgraph contains either a complete graph or independent set on $\geq n^c$ vertices?

## Site Remarks
Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s and Hajnal \cite{ErHa89}, who proved that a complete graph or independent set must exist on\[\geq \exp(c_H\sqrt{\log n})\]many vertices, where $c_H>0$ is some constant. This was improved by Buci\'{c}, Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour \cite{BNSS23} to\[\geq \exp(c_H\sqrt{\log n\log\log n}).\]The conjecture has been proved:
{UL}
{LI} for $H$ with $\leq 4$ vertices by Erd\H{o}s and Hajnal \cite{ErHa89}.{/LI}
{LI} for the 'bull' graph on $5$ vertices (a $P_4$ with a new vertex adjacent to the two middle vertices) by Chudnovsky and Safra \cite{ChSa08}.{/LI}
{LI} for $C_5$ by Chudnovsky, Scott, Seymour, and Spirkl \cite{CSSS23}.{/LI}
{LI} for $P_5$ by Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour \cite{NSS26}.{/LI}
{/UL}
Alon, Pach, and Solymosi \cite{APS01} have proved that the family of $H$ for which the conjecture holds is closed under vertex substitution; together with the three final results above this means the conjecture is now known for all $H$ on $5$ vertices.

Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour \cite{NSS24} have proved that if $H$ is a path then all $H$-free graphs on $n$ vertices contain either a complete graph or independent set on\[\geq 2^{(\log n)^{1-o(1)}}\]many vertices.

A detailed account of this problem, including the proof of some special cases, is given in the PhD thesis (https://www.proquest.com/openview/e0419f0edce6b2c51c56f7b2ec1870d7/1?pq-origsite=gscholar&cbl=18750&diss=y) of Nguyen.

This problem is #80 in Extremal Graph Theory (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/RamseyTuranWithoutC5.html) in the graphs problem collection.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - In random cograph ($P_4$-free subclass) experiments, minimum observed $\max(\alpha,\omega)$ scales around exponent $\sim 0.64..0.66$ for $n=64..512$.

 - Special forbidden-induced classes exhibit strong polynomial behavior, but this does not settle general H.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Advance the Erdős-Hajnal-type exponent lower bound for fixed forbidden induced
subgraph \(H\), ideally toward the conjectural polynomial behavior.
Step A: Structured decomposition
Obtain a decomposition theorem for \(H\)-free graphs into modules/blocks with
controlled interaction graph.
Step B: Recursive homogeneous-pair extraction
From Step A, recursively extract large complete or empty bipartite pairs,
amplifying clique/independent-set size exponent.
Step C: Global exponent bound
Close recursion to deduce
\[
\max(\alpha(G),\omega(G))\ge |V(G)|^{\delta(H)}
\]
with improved \(\delta(H)\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform decomposition lemma giving polynomial-size homogeneous pairs in
every \(H\)-free graph, with losses stable under recursion depth.

Proposition P1 (Recursion to power-law):
Assume there exist \(c,\theta\in(0,1)\) such that every \(H\)-free \(n\)-vertex
graph contains disjoint \(X,Y\) with \(|X|,|Y|\ge c n^\theta\) and either all
or no edges between \(X,Y\). Then there is \(\delta(H)>0\) with
\[
\max(\alpha(G),\omega(G))\ge n^{\delta(H)}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Apply recursion on induced subgraphs \(X\) or \(Y\), combining homogeneous-pair
choices with Ramsey-type amplification. Depth is \(O(\log\log n)\), yielding a
power-law exponent depending on \(c,\theta\).
\(\square\)
Proposition P2 (If Step A holds, Step C follows):
If Step A implies the P1 hypothesis, then the desired Erdős-Hajnal bound follows.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate by Proposition P1.
\(\square\)
