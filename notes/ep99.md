# EP-99

## Problem Statement
Let $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$ be a set of $n$ points with minimum distance equal to 1, chosen to minimise the diameter of $A$. If $n$ is sufficiently large then must there be three points in $A$ which form an equilateral triangle of size 1?

## Site Remarks
Thue proved that the minimal such diameter is achieved (asymptotically) by the points in a triangular lattice intersected with a circle. In general Erd\H{o}s believed such a set must have very large intersection with the triangular lattice (perhaps as many as $(1-o(1))n$).

Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er94b} wrote 'I could not prove it but felt that it should not be hard. To my great surprise both B. H. Sendov and M. Simonovits doubted the truth of this conjecture.' In \cite{Er94b} he offers \$100 for a counterexample but only \$50 for a proof.

The stated problem is false for $n=4$, for example taking the points to be vertices of a square. The behaviour of such sets for small $n$ is explored by Bezdek and Fodor \cite{BeFo99}.

See also [103].

## Local Computation
A constrained optimization was used for the finite analogue, minimizing diameter subject to minimum distance normalized to \(1\), and then testing whether the optimized configurations contain unit equilateral triples.

In this run, the best configurations found had no detected unit equilateral triangles, within tolerance \(0.02\), up to \(n=16\).

These are heuristic local minima rather than certified global diameter minimizers, so they do not decide the asymptotic EP claim.

## Local Proof Attempts
Route:
Reviewed the conjecture against known asymptotic minimizers (triangular-lattice behavior) and exceptions at small n.

Hard point:
Proving that every asymptotically diameter-minimizing configuration must contain a unit equilateral triangle requires strong structural stability for near-optimal packings.

Target:
For sufficiently large \(n\), prove every diameter-minimizing \(1\)-separated
\(n\)-point set contains a unit equilateral triangle.
Step A: Stability of near-optimal packings
Show minimizers are close to triangular-lattice truncations after scaling/rigid
motions.
Step B: Pattern forcing in lattice-like sets
Prove any sufficiently large lattice-close set necessarily contains a unit
equilateral triple.
Step C: Transfer through stability
Deduce the same for all true minimizers.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a robust geometric stability theorem with error small enough to preserve
exact unit-length triangles.

Proposition X1 (Template transfer):
Assume every minimizer \(P_n\) is \(\varepsilon_n\)-close to a template set
\(T_n\) that contains a unit equilateral triangle, with \(\varepsilon_n\to0\)
and nondegenerate angle margins. Then \(P_n\) contains a unit equilateral
triangle for all large \(n\).
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Choose the witness triple in \(T_n\) with margin \(\eta>0\) away from degenerate
configurations. For \(\varepsilon_n\ll \eta\), perturbation preserves equality of
the three side lengths at value \(1\) after the normalization used in minimizers.
\(\square\)
Proposition X2 (Reduction):
If Step A provides the closeness in X1 and Step B supplies witness triangles in
all large templates, then EP-99 asymptotic form follows.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate from X1.
\(\square\)
