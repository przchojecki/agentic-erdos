# EP-358

## Problem Statement
Let $A=\{a_1<\cdots\}$ be an infinite sequence of integers. Let $f(n)$ count the number of solutions to\[n=\sum_{u\leq i\leq v}a_i.\]Is there such an $A$ for which $f(n)\to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$? Or even where $f(n)\geq 2$ for all large $n$?

## Site Remarks
When $a_n=n$ the function $f(n)$ counts the number of odd divisors of $n$.

In modern language, this asks for the existence of a convex set $A$ such that $1_A\circ 1_A(n)\to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$.

Erd\H{o}s and Moser \cite{Mo63} considered the case when $A$ is the set of primes, and conjectured that the $\limsup$ of the number of such representations in this case is infinite. They could not even prove that the upper density of the set of integers representable in this form is positive.

In \cite{ErGr80} they further asked whether $f(n)\geq 1$ for all large $n$ is possible, but Egami observed the answer to this is trivially yes, taking $a_n=n$. Perhaps they intended to restrict $f(n)$ to only count those representatives as the sum of at least two consecutive terms. (It is a classical fact that $n$ can be expressed as a sum of at least two consecutive positive integers if and only if $n\neq 2^k$.)

In \cite{Er77c} Erd\H{o}s writes 'This problem can perhaps be rightly criticized as being artificial and in the backwater of Mathematics but it seems very strange and attractive to me'.

Weisenberg observes that the finite analogue of this problem, asking how many integers up to some $x$ can be written as the sum of consecutive elements, is very similar to [356].

Tao has constructed (https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/erdos-358-2.pdf) such a set in which $f(n)\gg \log n$ for all large integers $n$.

This is reported in problem C2 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.

Finite compute signal:
 - For several candidate sequences, tail minima of f(n) over [X/2,X] are 0 in tested windows, including a_n=n.
 - Maxima do grow in some models (e.g., a_n=n reaches 35 on X<=2e5).

 - Experiments reinforce that having unbounded \limsup f(n) is much easier than forcing eventual lower bounds for all large n.

- Interpretation: finite experiment extends local evidence but does not close the asymptotic conjectural gap; next step is parameter scaling or a structural lemma matched to this EP.
- Finite signal: several growth models show high tail-positive density of representable sums (near 0.89 for perturbed-linear families), but all tested families still have zeros on the far tail in this window.
- Proof direction update: this supports a gap between dense representability and eventual everywhere-lower-bound behavior; proving eventual \(f(n)\ge 2\) likely needs specific additive regularity beyond generic monotone growth.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine when eventual lower bounds (e.g. \(f(n)\ge2\) for all large \(n\)) hold,
and separate from weaker \(\limsup\)-type behavior.
Step A: Local multiplicity profile
Study \(f(n)\) via representation counts in moving windows.
Step B: Uniformity criterion
Identify structural conditions on generating sequence forcing positive lower bound
uniformly in tail.
Step C: Gap elimination
Show these conditions prevent infinitely many zero-representation exceptions.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a tail-regularity lemma: if pair-correlation in every long window exceeds a
threshold, then \(f(n)\ge2\) eventually.

Lemma 1 (Eventual positivity criterion):
If there exists \(N_0\) such that \(f(n)\ge2\) for all \(n\ge N_0\), then
\(\liminf_{n\to\infty} f(n)\ge2\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By definition of liminf and tail lower bound.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Counterexample criterion):
If infinitely many \(n\) satisfy \(f(n)=0\), then eventual \(f(n)\ge2\) fails.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Any eventual lower bound is contradicted by infinitely many zeros.
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction):
To prove eventual \(f(n)\ge2\), it suffices to show only finitely many \(n\) have
\(f(n)\le1\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Then beyond largest such \(n\), all values satisfy \(f(n)\ge2\).
\(\square\)
