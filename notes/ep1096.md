# EP-1096

## Problem Statement
Let $1<q<1+\epsilon$ and consider the set of numbers of the shape $\sum_{i\in S}q^i$ (for all finite $S$), ordered by size as $0=x_1<x_2<\cdots$.

Is it true that, provided $\epsilon>0$ is sufficiently small, $x_{k+1}-x_k \to 0$?

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Jo\'{o} posed in the 1991 problem session (https://westcoastnumbertheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/wcnt-problems-1991.pdf) of Great Western Number Theory.

They speculate that the threshold may be $q_0$, where $q_0\approx 1.3247$ is the real root of $x^3=x+1$, and is the smallest Pisot-Vijayaraghavan number (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot%E2%80%93Vijayaraghavan_number).

In \cite{EJK90} Erd\H{o}, Jo\'{o}, and Komornik prove that any Pisot-Vijayaraghavan number cannot have this property, and also prove that, for any $1<q\leq 2$, $x_{k+1}-x_k\leq 1$ for all $k$.

The sequence always begins $0,1,q$.

Bugeaud \cite{Bu96} proved that $1<q\leq 2$ is a Pisot-Vijayaraghavan number if and only if\[\liminf (x_{k+1}^m-x_k^m)>0\]for all $m\geq 1$, where $x_k^m$ is the set of those numbers which can be written as a finite sum $\sum_{n\geq 0}c_nq^n$ for some $c_n\in \{0,\ldots,m\}$ (so that the sequence in the question is $x_k^1$). Erd\H{o}s, Jo\'{o}, and Schnitzer \cite{EJS96} improved this to show that, if $1<q<(1+\sqrt{5})/2$, then $q$ is a Pisot-Vijayaraghavan number if and only if\[\liminf (x_{k+1}^2-x_k^2)>0.\]This was first resolved by Erd\H{o}s and Komornik \cite{ErKo98}, who proved that\[\lim_{n\to \infty}(x_{n+1}-x_n)=0\]whenever $1<q<\sqrt{q_1}\approx 1.175$, where $q_1\approx 1.38$ is the second Pisot-Vijayaraghavan number.

Feng \cite{Fe16} has proved that\[\liminf_{n\to \infty}(x_{n+1}-x_n)=0\]if and only if $1<q<2$ is not a Pisot-Vijayaraghavan number, and if $1<q<\sqrt{2}$ and $q^2$ is not a Pisot-Vijayaraghavan number then\[\lim_{n\to \infty}(x_{n+1}-x_n)=0.\]

## Local Computation
Route:
Computed finite subset-sum spectra
{sum_{i=0}^M c_i q^i: c_i in {0,1}}
for several q in (1,2) and measured tail gap statistics.

- (M=18) shows very small average tail gaps in the last quartile of sorted values, though occasional large (~1) gaps persist in finite truncations.

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Finite truncations do not decide asymptotic behavior of the full infinite ordered set.

Target:
For \(q\in(1,2)\), analyze gap behavior in ordered sums
\[
\Sigma_q:=\left\{\sum_{i\ge0} c_i q^i:\ c_i\in\{0,1\},\ \text{finite support}\right\},
\]
especially vanishing-gap versus persistent-gap regimes.
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Establish a dichotomy by algebraic type of \(q\): non-Pisot \(q\) implies arbitrarily small normalized tail gaps in \(\Sigma_q\), while Pisot \(q\) implies a uniform positive gap floor.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (finite-level approximation):
Let \(\Sigma_q^{(M)}\) be sums up to \(q^M\). Then \(\Sigma_q^{(M)}\subset\Sigma_q\) and sorted-gap statistics on \(\Sigma_q^{(M)}\) approximate lower-tail behavior of \(\Sigma_q\) as \(M\to\infty\).
Proof.
Nestedness is immediate. Every infinite set element is limit of truncations; thus lower-tail gap properties are probed by large \(M\) levels. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (Pisot rigidity direction):
If \(q\) is Pisot, then fractional parts of \(q^m\) tend to 0 exponentially, inducing discrete separation patterns in \(\Sigma_q\).
Proof.
Pisot property gives exponential decay of conjugate contributions, yielding near-integer behavior and hence rigid spacing constraints for digit sums. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (non-Pisot density transfer):
If additive semigroup images modulo 1 are dense with quantitative discrepancy decay, then sorted gaps in \(\Sigma_q\) must have liminf \(0\).
Proof.
Quantitative density modulo 1 forces arbitrarily close projected sums; lifting back to ordered real sums gives arbitrarily small gaps. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The hardest ingredient is a uniform quantitative non-Pisot equidistribution theorem strong enough to turn qualitative density into explicit gap decay rates.
