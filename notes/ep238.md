# EP-238

## Problem Statement
Let $c_1,c_2>0$. Is it true that, for any sufficiently large $x$, there exist more than $c_1\log x$ many consecutive primes $\leq x$ such that the difference between any two is $>c_2$?

- 14:28 on 13 Jan 2026 - Przemek Chojecki (reply depth 1):
  Indeed, looks like the following argument would work:
  
  Let $c_1,c_2>0$ and put $c=\lfloor c_2\rfloor$. We seek a block of $>c_1\log x$ consecutive primes $\le x$ whose adjacent gaps all exceed $c$. A natural conditional route is to assume a short-interval, uniformly averaged Hardy--Littlewood $k$-tuple conjecture at the scale $T\asymp(\log x)^2$: for each fixed $m\ge1$, uniformly over intervals $I=[u,u+T]\subset[x/2,x]$ and distinct shifts $H=\{h_1,\dots,h_m\}\subset[0,T+c]$,\[
  \#\{n\in I:\ n+h_i\ \text{prime for all }i\}
  =
  (1+o(1))\,\mathfrak S(H)\,\frac{T}{(\log x)^m},\]as $x\to \infty$, with $o(1)$ uniform in $I,H$. (One can weaken “uniform in $I$” to “for all but $o(1)$ of such intervals”, consistent with Maier-type irregularities.) This hypothesis implies both concentration of the prime count $N(I)$ around $T/\log x$ and Poisson-type statistics for rare local patterns such as prime pairs at separations $\le c$ inside a typical interval of length $T$.
  
  Take $T=A(\log x)^2$ with fixed $A>c_1$ and partition $[x/2,x]$ into disjoint intervals $I_j$ of length $T$. By the $m=1,2$ cases, $N(I_j)$ has mean $\sim A\log x$ and variance $O(\log x)$, so $N(I_j)\ge c_1\log x$ for all but $o(1)$ of the $I_j$ (Chebyshev). Let $B(I_j)$ be the number of prime pairs $(p,q)\subset I_j$ with $0<q-p\le c$. Using the fixed-$m$ cases of the conjecture and inclusion--exclusion/moment methods (à la Gallagher), one gets that $B(I_j)$ converges in distribution to $\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$ with $\lambda=A\sum_{h=1}^c \mathfrak S(\{0,h\})$, hence $\mathbb P(B(I_j)=0)\to e^{-\lambda}>0$ for a positive proportion of intervals. Therefore some $I_j$ simultaneously satisfies $N(I_j)\ge c_1\log x$ and $B(I_j)=0$; the primes in that interval are automatically consecutive in the global sequence and, since there is no pair within distance $\le c$, every adjacent prime gap inside the block exceeds $c$, yielding $>c_1\log x$ consecutive primes $\le x$ with all pairwise differences $>c_2$.

  I think only the weaker second formulation is easy to establish. (There are $\sim x/\log x$ prime gaps in $[x/2,x]$, of which $O(c_2 x/\log^2 x)$ of them are of size $\leq c_2$ by standard sieve theory bounds; hence, by the pigeonhole principle, one can find a sequence of $\gg \frac{1}{c_2} \log x$ consecutive gaps in $[x,2x]$ of size $> c_2$, giving the second form (in fact one can take $c_1$ proportional to $1/c_2$.)) The stronger first version might still be true (it seems plausible from standard probabilistic heuristics, which in fact predict consecutive runs as large as $\gg_{c_2} \log x \log\log x$), but looks much harder to establish unconditionally.

## Local Computation
- direct counting of long gap-runs versus thresholds c_1\log x for several (c_1,c_2) choices.
- In this finite range, many runs exceeded moderate logarithmic thresholds, with counts decreasing as c_2 and c_1 increased.
- Obstacle: the EP asks for universal asymptotics for all sufficiently large x; finite-range statistics cannot settle full parameter-uniform claims.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background records that Erdos proved this for any fixed \(c_2>0\) when \(c_1\) is chosen sufficiently small.

What is not settled here is the uniform claim for arbitrary prescribed positive \(c_1,c_2\).

Target:
For fixed \(c_1,c_2>0\), decide when long runs of consecutive prime gaps
exceeding \(c_2\) force run length \(>c_1\log x\) for all large \(x\).
Step A: Long-gap Bernoulli model with correction
Model events \((p_{n+1}-p_n)>c_2\) and quantify dependencies.
Step B: Run-length large deviations
Prove lower and upper bounds for maximum run length among primes up to \(x\).
Step C: Parameter map
Identify the admissible \((c_1,c_2)\)-region for guaranteed inequalities.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need uniform estimates for dependence between nearby long-gap events to justify
run-length asymptotics beyond independent heuristics.

Proposition CQ1 (Run-threshold equivalence):
Let \(R_x(c_2)\) be maximum length of consecutive prime gaps \(>c_2\) among
primes \(\le x\). Then the EP statement is equivalent to
\[
R_x(c_2)>c_1\log x
\]
for all large \(x\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
This is exactly the definition of the counted run condition.
\(\square\)
Proposition CQ2 (Reduction):
If one proves
\[
R_x(c_2)\sim \kappa(c_2)\log x
\]
with explicit \(\kappa(c_2)\), then the inequality in CQ1 holds iff
\(c_1<\kappa(c_2)\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Compare asymptotic coefficients.
\(\square\)
