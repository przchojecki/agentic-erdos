# EP-860

## Problem Statement
Let $h(n)$ be such that, for any $m\geq 1$, in the interval $(m,m+h(n))$ there exist distinct integers $a_i$ for $1\leq i\leq \pi(n)$ such that $p_i\mid a_i$, where $p_i$ denotes the $i$th prime.

Estimate $h(n)$.

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Pomerance \cite{ErPo80}, who proved that\[h(n) \ll \frac{n^{3/2}}{(\log n)^{1/2}}.\]Erd\H{o}s and Selfridge proved $h(n)>(3-o(1))n$, and Ruzsa proved $h(n)/n\to \infty$.

This is discussed in problem B32 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.

See also [375].

## Local Computation
- Implemented a deeper finite Hall-matching proxy:
 - for fixed $n$ and $m$, binary-searched the smallest interval length $h$ admitting a matching from primes $\le n$ to distinct multiples in $(m,m+h]$;
 - took worst case over $1\le m\le m_{\max}$.

- For tested finite ranges $(m\le 3000)$:
 - $n=30$: worst $h=46$,
 - $n=50$: worst $h=86$,
 - $n=80$: worst $h=94$,
 - $n=100$: worst $h=97$.
- Ratios $h/n$ are near-constant scale in these finite windows, but this does not determine asymptotic behavior.
