# EP-165

## Site Remarks
It is known that there exists some constant $c>0$ such that for large $k$\[(c+o(1))\frac{k^2}{\log k}\leq R(3,k) \leq (1+o(1))\frac{k^2}{\log k}.\]The lower bound is due to Kim \cite{Ki95}, the upper bound is due to Shearer \cite{Sh83}, improving an earlier bound of Ajtai, Koml\'{o}s, and Szemer\'{e}di \cite{AKS80}.

The value of $c$ in the lower bound has seen a number of improvements. Kim's original proof gave $c\geq 1/162$. The bound $c\geq 1/4$ was proved independently by Bohman and Keevash \cite{BoKe21} and Pontiveros, Griffiths and Morris \cite{PGM20}. The latter collection of authors conjecture that this lower bound is the true order of magnitude.

This was, however, improved by Campos, Jenssen, Michelen, and Sahasrabudhe \cite{CJMS25} to $c\geq 1/3$, and further by Hefty, Horn, King, and Pfender \cite{HHKP25} to $c\geq 1/2$. Both of these papers conjecture that $c=1/2$ is the correct asymptotic.

See also [544], and [986] for the general case. See [1013] for a related function.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - Triangle-free process proxy gives empirical witnesses with independence number below $k$ at $n\approx(0.64..0.75)\,k^2/\log k$ for tested $k=20,30,40,50$.

 - Finite process behavior matches the expected $k^2/\log k$ scale but does not identify the true asymptotic constant.

- Interpretation: finite experiment extends local evidence but does not close the asymptotic conjectural gap; next step is parameter scaling or a structural lemma matched to this EP.

- Proof attempt: standard random/triangle-free-process heuristics suggest alpha(G) should be on the order of sqrt(n log n), yielding n ~ c k^2/log k for alpha(G)<k; this supports scale but does not determine the sharp constant c.
- Obstacle: current finite-process and greedy-alpha estimates are not rigorous enough to convert into a proof of the exact asymptotic Ramsey constant.
- Deeper computation: tested k=30,40,50,60,70 with n at ratios 0.55,0.65,0.75,0.85 of k^2/log k (10 process runs each); empirical n/(k^2/log k) thresholds were ~0.748 (k=30,40) and ~0.850 (k=50,60,70).

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine the sharp constant \(c\) in
\[
R(3,k)\sim c\,\frac{k^2}{\log k}.
\]
Step A: Triangle-free process calibration
Obtain rigorous concentration for independence number in process-generated
triangle-free graphs at terminal density.
Step B: Universal upper-constant control
Improve container/supersaturation upper bounds to matching constant scale.
Step C: Constant matching
Show lower and upper constants coincide.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a sharp limit law for \(\alpha(G)\) in near-extremal triangle-free graphs
that is strong enough to determine the front constant.

Proposition BK1 (Ramsey equivalence):
\(R(3,k)>n\) iff there exists a triangle-free graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices with
\(\alpha(G)<k\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Standard red/blue interpretation with red graph \(G\) triangle-free and blue
complement having no \(K_k\).
\(\square\)
Proposition BK2 (Constant extraction reduction):
If one proves constants \(c_-,c_+\) with
\[
\alpha(G)\ge (c_-+o(1))\sqrt{n\log n}
\]
for all triangle-free \(G\), and constructions with
\[
\alpha(G)\le (c_++o(1))\sqrt{n\log n},
\]
then
\[
\frac{1}{c_+^2}\le \liminf \frac{R(3,k)\log k}{k^2}
\le \limsup \frac{R(3,k)\log k}{k^2}\le \frac{1}{c_-^2}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply BK1 and invert \(k\asymp \sqrt{n\log n}\).
\(\square\)
