# EP-108

## Problem Statement
For every $r\geq 4$ and $k\geq 2$ is there some finite $f(k,r)$ such that every graph of chromatic number $\geq f(k,r)$ contains a subgraph of girth $\geq r$ and chromatic number $\geq k$?

## Site Remarks
Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s and Hajnal. R\"{o}dl \cite{Ro77} has proved the $r=4$ case (see [923]). The infinite version (whether every graph of infinite chromatic number contains a subgraph of infinite chromatic number whose girth is $>k$) is also open.

In \cite{Er79b} Erd\H{o}s also asks whether\[\lim_{k\to \infty}\frac{f(k,r+1)}{f(k,r)}=\infty.\]See also the entry in the graphs problem collection (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/SubgraphsOfLargeGirth.html) and [740] for the infinitary version.

## Local Computation
- The r=4 case is known solved (Rödl, per background).
- The general r>=5 case remains open in this record.
- The related infinitary version is also recorded open.

- an explicit finite analogue of the EP implication “high chromatic host => high-girth high-chromatic subgraph”.
- For sampled hosts, exhaustive subset checks produced girth-qualified subgraphs with chromatic number below host chromatic number; the gap widened for denser hosts where high-girth subsets were rarer.
- Obstacle: finite proxies show tension but do not establish asymptotic existence of finite f(k,r); the theorem-level step requires non-probabilistic structural extraction methods.

## Local Proof Attempts
I aligned the statement with known solved/open subcases in the background.

Hard point:
Extending from triangle-free high-chromatic subgraphs (r=4) to larger girth
requires stronger cycle-control while preserving large chromatic number.

Target:
For each \(k\ge2, r\ge4\), prove existence of finite \(f(k,r)\) such that
\(\chi(G)\ge f(k,r)\) implies \(G\) contains a subgraph \(H\) with
\(\chi(H)\ge k\) and girth at least \(r\).
Step A: High-chromatic sparse core
Extract a subgraph with large average degree and controlled local cycle profile.
Step B: Cycle-pruning with chromatic retention
Delete a carefully chosen edge/vertex set to kill all short cycles
\(<r\) while keeping chromatic number \(\ge k\).
Step C: Parameter closure
Track losses to produce explicit finite \(f(k,r)\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a short-cycle hitting lemma with chromatic stability: removing all cycles
below length \(r\) should not collapse chromatic number too much.

Proposition AD1 (Chromatic retention criterion):
If \(G\) has a subgraph \(H\) with
\[
\chi(H)\ge k,\qquad \text{girth}(H)\ge r,
\]
then \(G\) satisfies the EP conclusion for \((k,r)\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate since \(H\subseteq G\) is exactly the required witness.
\(\square\)
Proposition AD2 (Reduction to pruning lemma):
Suppose every graph \(G\) with \(\chi(G)\ge F\) contains \(H\subseteq G\) such
that deleting a short-cycle transversal \(X\) gives
\[
\chi(H-X)\ge k,\qquad \text{girth}(H-X)\ge r.
\]
Then \(f(k,r)\le F\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Take \(f(k,r)=F\). Any \(G\) with \(\chi(G)\ge F\) yields witness \(H-X\), then
apply AD1.
\(\square\)
