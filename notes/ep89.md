# EP-89

## Problem Statement
Does every set of $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ determine $\gg n/\sqrt{\log n}$ many distinct distances?

## Site Remarks
A $\sqrt{n}\times\sqrt{n}$ integer grid shows that this would be the best possible. Nearly solved by Guth and Katz \cite{GuKa15} who proved that there are always $\gg n/\log n$ many distinct distances.

A stronger form (see [604]) may be true: is there a single point which determines $\gg n/\sqrt{\log n}$ distinct distances, or even $\gg n$ many such points, or even that this is true averaged over all points - for example, if $d(x)$ counts the number of distinct distances from $x$ then in \cite{Er75f} Erd\H{o}s conjectured\[\sum_{x\in A}d(x) \gg \frac{n^2}{\sqrt{\log n}},\]where $A\subset \mathbb{R}^2$ is any set of $n$ points.

See also [661], and [1083] for the generalisation to higher dimensions.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - On square-grid samples, distinct-distance counts normalize to about $1.09..1.10$ times $n/\sqrt{\log n}$.

 - Finite grid behavior is consistent with the conjectured sharp scale, but does not constitute a proof for all point sets.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Prove the distinct-distance lower bound in the plane at the conjectural scale
\[
D(P)\gg \frac{|P|}{\sqrt{\log |P|}}
\]
for every finite point set \(P\subset\mathbb R^2\), with sharp constants if possible.
Step A: Energy-incidence decomposition
Control repeated distances via distance energy and incidence bounds with circles
and perpendicular bisectors.
Step B: Structural dichotomy
Either energy is small (already yielding many distances) or \(P\) has rigid
lattice-like structure.
Step C: Structured-case optimization
Show the rigid case still satisfies the target lower bound, matching the grid
benchmark up to constants.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a refined structured-case theorem: near-extremizers for distance energy
must be quantitatively close to product/lattice sets.

Proposition R1 (Energy-to-distinct-distance inequality):
Let \(m_t\) be multiplicities of each realized distance in \(P\), with
\(\sum_t m_t=\binom{n}{2}\), \(n=|P|\). Then
\[
D(P)\ge \frac{\binom{n}{2}^2}{\sum_t m_t^2}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz:
\[
\Bigl(\sum_t m_t\Bigr)^2\le D(P)\sum_t m_t^2.
\]
Rearrange.
\(\square\)
Proposition R2 (Reduction to fourth-moment control):
If one proves
\[
\sum_t m_t^2\ll n^3\sqrt{\log n},
\]
then
\[
D(P)\gg \frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Insert into Proposition R1 and use \(\binom{n}{2}\asymp n^2\).
\(\square\)
