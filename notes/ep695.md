# EP-695

## Problem Statement
Let $p_1<p_2<\cdots$ be a sequence of primes such that $p_{i+1}\equiv 1\pmod{p_i}$. Is it true that\[\lim_k p_k^{1/k}=\infty?\]Does there exist such a sequence with\[p_k \leq \exp(k(\log k)^{1+o(1)})?\]

## Site Remarks
Such a sequence is sometimes called a prime chain.

If we take the obvious 'greedy' chain with $2=p_1$ and $p_{i+1}$ is the smallest prime $\equiv 1\pmod{p_i}$ then Linnik's theorem (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnik%27s_theorem) implies that this sequence grows like\[p_k \leq e^{e^{O(k)}}.\]It is conjectured that, for any prime $p$, there is a prime $p'\leq p(\log p)^{O(1)}$ which is congruent to $1\pmod{p}$, which would imply this sequence grows like\[p_k\leq \exp(k(\log k)^{1+o(1)}).\]An extensive study of the growth of finite prime chains was carried out by Ford, Konyagin, and Luca \cite{FKL10}.

See also [696].

## Local Computation
- Implemented standalone greedy-chain search for many starting primes.
- For each step, computed the smallest prime of the form \(1 \bmod p_i\), then tracked growth profiles \(p_k^{1/k}\).
- This does not prove asymptotics, but provides finite-scale growth envelopes across many starts.

- Deep run parameters: 46 starting primes (all primes \(\le 200\)), chain depth up to \(k=14\), search cap 800000 candidates per edge.
- Largest observed \(p_{14}^{1/14}\) in this run was about \(14.94\) (chain starting at 37).
- Finite behavior supports rapid growth in greedy constructions, but does not separate the two asymptotic questions.

## Local Proof Attempts
The approach here was to grow many prime chains greedily, always taking the least prime congruent to \(1\bmod p_i\), and then monitor the growth surrogate \(p_k^{1/k}\). This gives finite-scale evidence that such chains can grow rapidly, but it does not decide either asymptotic direction: whether every such chain must satisfy \(p_k^{1/k}\to\infty\), or whether one can still realize a chain with \(p_k\le \exp(k(\log k)^{1+o(1)})\).
