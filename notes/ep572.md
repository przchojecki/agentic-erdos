# EP-572

## Problem Statement
Show that for $k\geq 3$\[\mathrm{ex}(n;C_{2k})\gg n^{1+\frac{1}{k}}.\]

## Site Remarks
It is easy to see that $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_{2k+1})=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ for any $k\geq 1$ (and $n>2k+1$) (since no bipartite graph contains an odd cycle). Erd\H{o}s and Klein \cite{Er38} proved $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)\asymp n^{3/2}$.

Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er64c} and Bondy and Simonovits \cite{BoSi74} showed that\[\mathrm{ex}(n;C_{2k})\ll kn^{1+\frac{1}{k}}.\]Benson \cite{Be66} has proved this conjecture for $k=3$ and $k=5$. Lazebnik, Ustimenko, and Woldar \cite{LUW95} have shown that, for arbitrary $k\geq 3$,\[\mathrm{ex}(n;C_{2k})\gg n^{1+\frac{2}{3k-3+\nu}},\]where $\nu=0$ if $k$ is odd and $\nu=1$ if $k$ is even. See \cite{LUW99} for further history and references.

See also [765].

This problem is #46 in Extremal Graph Theory (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/TuranEvenCycleLowerBound.html) in the graphs problem collection.

- 09:53 on 30 Dec 2025 - Alfaiz:
  Building on the work of [BoSi74], J. Verstraete in [Ve00] (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0204222) improved the bound to $\mathrm{ex}(n, C_{2k}) ≤ 8(k − 1)n^{1+1/k}.$
  
  Pikhurko in [Pi12] (https://www.ams.org/journals/proc/2012-140-11/S0002-9939-2012-11274-2/S0002-9939-2012-11274-2.pdf) improved the bound further to $\mathrm{ex}(n, C_{2k}) ≤(k − 1)n^{1+1/k} + O(n).$
  
  Bukh and Jiang in [BuJi14] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1601v4) have improved the bound to $\mathrm{ex}(n, C_{2k}) ≤ 80 \sqrt{k} \log k · n^{1+1/k} + 10k^2n$
  
  Z. He in [He21] (https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v28i2p41) has improved the bound to $\mathrm{ex}(n, C_{2k}) \leq (16 \sqrt{5} \sqrt{k \log k}+o(1))· n^{1+1/k}$
