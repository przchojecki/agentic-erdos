# EP-887

## Problem Statement
Is there an absolute constant $K$ such that, for every $C>0$, if $n$ is sufficiently large then $n$ has at most $K$ divisors in $(n^{1/2},n^{1/2}+C n^{1/4})$.

## Site Remarks
A question of Erd\H{o}s and Rosenfeld \cite{ErRo97}, who proved that there are infinitely many $n$ with $4$ divisors in $(n^{1/2},n^{1/2}+n^{1/4})$, and ask whether $4$ is best possible here. They also proved that, if $n$ is large enough depending on $C$, then $n$ has at most $1+C^2$ divisors in $(n^{1/2},n^{1/2}+C n^{1/4})$.

Chan \cite{Ch14} has resolved this when $n$ is a square, proving that if $n$ is a square then $n$ has at most $5$ divisors in\[[ n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/7}, n^{1/2}+n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/7}].\]In \cite{Ch15} Chan further proves that if $n=(N-a)(N-b)$ for some $0\leq a\leq b\leq \exp((\log n)^{2/7})$ then $n$ has at most $18$ divisors in\[[ n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/14}, n^{1/2}+n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/14}].\]See also [886].

  Tsz Ho Chan (https://eudml.org/doc/279082, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2230) has studied this problem. They have shown this conjecture when $n$ is a perfect square or near a perfect square. In general, the problem reduces to showing that if $Q_1(x, y), \cdots, Q_K(x, y)$ are certain polynomials of degree $1$, then there exists only finitely many $(x, y)$ such that they are simultaneously perfect squares. This should be correct under Bomberi-Lang, though unconditionally it appears very hard.

## Local Computation
- Ran a deeper finite scan up to $n\le 3\cdot 10^6$.
- For each $C\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$, counted divisors in the target interval and tracked maxima.

- Max counts found in range:
 - $C=1$: 1,
 - $C=2$: 4,
 - $C=3$: 5,
 - $C=4$: 6,
 - $C=6$: 8.
- Finite evidence is consistent with boundedness for fixed $C$, but no universal constant conclusion follows.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite scans support the idea that for each fixed \(C\), the number of divisors near \(\sqrt n\) stays bounded in practice, but the observed maxima still grow with \(C\), so they provide no evidence for a universal constant independent of \(C\). The global boundedness question remains open.
