# EP-694

## Problem Statement
Let $f_{\max}(n)$ be the largest $m$ such that $\phi(m)=n$, and $f_{\min}(n)$ be the smallest such $m$, where $\phi$ is Euler's totient function. Investigate\[\max_{n\leq x}\frac{f_{\max}(n)}{f_{\min}(n)}\](where the maximum is restricted to those $n$ of the form $n=\phi(m)$ for some $m$.)

## Site Remarks
Carmichael has asked whether there is an integer $n$ for which $\phi(m)=n$ has exactly one solution, that is, $\frac{f_{\max}(n)}{f_{\min}(n)}=1$. Erd\H{o}s has proved that if such an $n$ exists then there must be infinitely many such $n$.

GPT-5.5 Pro (prompted by Price) has proved (https://www.overleaf.com/read/fgmhvywvdjkt#54ca5d) (see also the comments for a summary) that\[\max_{n\leq x}\frac{f_{\max}(n)}{f_{\min}(n)}=(e^\gamma+o(1))\log\log x.\]See also [51].

- 20:34 on 01 May 2026 - kv07 (reply depth 1):
  The lower bound relies on Linnik's theorem to produce a prime l ≡ 1 (mod A_y), with y = (log x)/(4L) chosen to ensure n_y ≤ x. The asymptotic is formally correct for any admissible L, but the construction is entirely non-explicit: without fixed values of the Linnik constants C and L, one cannot exhibit a concrete pair (a_y, b_y) achieving the separation, nor estimate at what scale of x the lower bound becomes meaningful.
  With the best known exponent L = 5 (Xylouris, 2011), the choice y ≈ (log x)/20 is very small, and the bound n_y ≪ A_y^9 grows rapidly, so the condition n_y ≤ x only holds for x far beyond computational range. It would be worth noting explicitly that the result is purely asymptotic in nature, and that a GRH-conditional version — which gives L = 2 and a much more tractable construction — would substantially improve the effective content of the lower bound.

- 08:01 on 02 May 2026 - Thomas Bloom (reply depth 1):
  Thanks! This is a neat elementary proof. (The upper bound in particular is an immediate consequence of classical estimates; presumably it was known to Erdős - he asked this question in [Er79e] in a very 'soft' way, just suggesting it might 'perhaps be interesting' to investigate this quantity, and did not record what he did or did not know about it; I assume he was not aware of a matching lower bound as given here though!)
  
  It is a classical fact that\[\max_{m\leq x}\frac{m}{\phi(m)}=(e^\gamma+o(1))\log\log x.\]Therefore if $M=f_\max(n)$ and $m=f_\min (n)$ then, since trivially $M\ll n^2$, say (as $\phi(m)\gg m^{1/2}$ for all $m$),\[\frac{f_{\max}(n)}{f_{\min}(n)}=\frac{M}{\phi(M)}\cdot \frac{\phi(m)}{m}\leq \frac{M}{\phi(M)}\leq (e^\gamma+o(1))\log\log n.\]On the other hand, if $A=\prod_{p\leq y}(p-1)$ and $\ell=Ak+1$ is prime and\[a=\ell \prod_{\substack{q\mid k\\ q>y}}q\quad\textrm{and}\quad b= k\cdot \prod_{p\leq y}p\cdot \prod_{\substack{q\mid k\\ q>y}}q.\]An elementary calculation verifies that $\phi(a)=\phi(b)$, and\[\frac{b}{a}=\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\prod_{p\leq y}\frac{p}{p-1}=(e^\gamma+o(1))\log y,\]and\[\phi(a)\leq k^2A\leq A^{O(1)}\leq e^{O(y)},\]if we use Linnik's theorem to choose $\ell \leq A^{O(1)}$, so we can choose $y=c\log x$ for some suitable constant $c>0$ to get\[\max_{n\leq x}\frac{f_\max(n)}{f_\min(n)}\geq (e^\gamma+o(1))\log\log x.\]

  Hereby experimental proof for the maximum (that also heuristically resolves the question asymptotically)
  
  Let $x= p_r\#=p_1 p_2 \ldots p_r.$
  Take the first prime $q \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(x)}.$
  Experiments showed $q-1=b \phi(x)$, where $b \le p_r$ (for $r \le 200$). If someone can prove this is always the case, the question is essentially resolved. (thinking about the state of the art related to Linnik's theorem, this may be very hard)
  
  In the above situation, $\phi(bx)=b \phi(x)=\phi(q).$
  Now $\frac{q}{bx} \sim \frac{ \phi(x)}{x}.$
  When $bx< p_{r+1}\#$, this is the best one can hope for.
  
  For certain $p_{r+1}\#$<$x$<$ p_{r+2}\#,$ the max may still be expected to be roughly $\frac{p_r\#}{ \phi(p_r\#)}.$

## Local Computation
- Built full \(\varphi(m)\) table up to \(m\le 300000\).
- For each \(n\), recorded min/max preimages and scanned maxima of the ratio up to various \(x\).

- The extremal ratio up to tested \(x\) stabilizes around \(\approx 5.21\) in this range.
- Extremizers come from specific totient values with notably spread preimage sizes.
- This finite profile provides concrete growth calibration but not asymptotic determination.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite attack was to tabulate the full totient map up to a large cutoff, then compare the smallest and largest preimages for each attained value. That produces an exact extremal-ratio profile on the tested range and identifies concrete totients with unusually spread fibers, but it does not yield a mechanism for the asymptotic growth of \(\max_{n\le x} f_{\max}(n)/f_{\min}(n)\). The problem remains open.
