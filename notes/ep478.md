# EP-478

## Problem Statement
Let $p$ be a prime and\[A_p = \{ k! \pmod{p} : 1\leq k<p\}.\]Is it true that\[\lvert A_p\rvert \sim (1-\tfrac{1}{e})p?\]

## Site Remarks
Since $A_p/A_p=\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ it follows that $\lvert A_p\rvert \gg p^{1/2}$. The best known lower bound is due to Grebennikov, Sagdeev, Semchankau, and Vasilevskii \cite{GSSV24},\[\lvert A_p\rvert \geq (\sqrt{2}-o(1))p^{1/2},\]which follows from proving that $\lvert A_pA_p\rvert=(1+o(1))p$.

Wilson's theorem implies $(p-2)!\equiv 1\pmod{p}$, and hence $\lvert A_p\rvert\leq p-2$. Results on $\lvert A_p\rvert$ on average were obtained by Klurman and Munsch \cite{KlMu17}.

It is even open whether there are infinitely many primes $p$ such that $\lvert A_p\rvert=p-2$: in other words, such that $2!,\ldots,(p-1)!$ are all distinct modulo $p$ (such primes are sometimes called socialist primes). Rokowska and Schinzel \cite{RoSc60} have proved that any socialist prime is $5\pmod{8}$, and must satisfy $-(\frac{5}{p})=(\frac{-23}{p})=1$. Moreover, the congruence class missing from $2!,\ldots,(p-1)!$ must be $-(\frac{p-1}{2})!$.

The only known socialist prime is $p=5$. Trudgian \cite{Tr13} has shown there are no other socialist primes $<10^9$. This was extended to $10^{11}$ by Andreji\'{c} and Tatarevic \cite{AnTa16}.

This is also discussed in problems A2 and F11 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Prove asymptotic
\[
|A_p|\sim (1-1/e)p,\qquad A_p=\{k!\bmod p:1\le k<p\}.
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Show factorial residues behave like near-random occupancy among \(p\) classes after appropriate mixing window, with collision count matching Poisson occupancy and yielding \(1-1/e\) uncovered proportion.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (incremental walk form):
Sequence \(x_k=k!\bmod p\) satisfies
\[
x_{k+1}\equiv (k+1)x_k\pmod p.
\]
Proof.
Immediate from factorial recurrence. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (occupancy identity):
\[
|A_p|=p-\#\{\text{residue classes not hit by }x_k\}.
\]
Proof.
Definition of image set size as covered classes. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies \(1-1/e\) constant):
If A gives Poisson-like occupancy with mean 1 per class, uncovered fraction tends to \(e^{-1}\), hence \(|A_p|/p\to1-1/e\).
Proof.
Standard occupancy limit: \(\mathbb P(\text{unhit})\to e^{-1}\). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The key obstacle is proving sufficient pseudorandomness/mixing for multiplicative factorial walk modulo primes.
