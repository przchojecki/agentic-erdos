# EP-1014

## Problem Statement
Let $R(k,l)$ be the Ramsey number, so the minimal $n$ such that every graph on at least $n$ vertices contains either a $K_k$ or an independent set on $l$ vertices.

Prove, for fixed $k\geq 3$, that\[\lim_{l\to \infty}\frac{R(k,l+1)}{R(k,l)}=1.\]

- 23:49 on 25 Feb 2026 - Zeraoulia Rafik:
  This is just an attempt that might solve the case \(k=3\).
  
  We prove that\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{R(3,n+1)}{R(3,n)}=1.
  \]By the Greenwood-Gleason recurrence,\[
  R(3,n+1)\le R(2,n+1)+R(3,n)=(n+1)+R(3,n),
  \]since \(R(2,m)=m\). Dividing by \(R(3,n)\) yields\[
  \frac{R(3,n+1)}{R(3,n)}\le 1+\frac{n+1}{R(3,n)}.
  \]Monotonicity gives \(R(3,n+1)\ge R(3,n)+1\), so\[
  \frac{R(3,n+1)}{R(3,n)}\ge 1+\frac{1}{R(3,n)}.
  \]It is known (Ajtai–Komlós–Szemerédi 1980, Kim 1995) that
  \(R(3,n)=\Theta(n^2/\log n)\). Hence there exists \(c>0\) with
  \(R(3,n)\ge c\,n^2/\log n\) for large \(n\), implying\[
  \frac{n+1}{R(3,n)}\le\frac{(n+1)\log n}{c\,n^2}\longrightarrow0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.
  \]From the lower bound we get \(\liminf R(3,n+1)/R(3,n)\ge 1+\lim 1/R(3,n)=1\).
  From the upper bound and the limit above we get \(\limsup R(3,n+1)/R(3,n)\le 1+0=1\).
  Thus \(\liminf=\limsup=1\), so the limit exists and equals \(1\). ∎

- 01:10 on 26 Feb 2026 - TerenceTao (reply depth 1):
  The monotonicity of $R(3,n)$ is not known to be strict, but otherwise the argument here seems to be correct. Such a result would already have been known to Erdos at the time of [Er71], given that Erdos himself proved (https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-06.pdf) $R(3,n) \gg n^2/\log^2 n$ back in 1961, and the Greenwood-Gleason paper (https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/BF0DEBC881488344266BCD77CBBCD86B/S0008414X00030091a.pdf/combinatorial_relations_and_chromatic_graphs.pdf) is from 1955. Looking at [Er71 (https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1971-25.pdf), p.99] it seems that Erdos was attributing the $k=3$ case of determining the exact rate of growth of $R(k,l)$ as open, not the problem of establishing that the ratio $R(k,l+1)/R(k,l)$ converges to $1$. (And Erdos seems to have mistyped the known upper and lower bounds of $R(3,l)$ (which he calls $f(3,l)$) in his equation (2); he writes $n^2 \log n / \log\log n \ll f(3,n) \ll n^2 \log^2 n$ but I think he meant $n^2 / \log^2 n \ll f(3,n) \ll n^2 \log\log n / \log n$. The correct order of growth $f(3,n) \asymp n^2 / \log n$ was determined by Kim (https://people.tamu.edu/~huafei-yan/Teaching/Math689/ramsey5.pdf) in 1995.)

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
For fixed \(k\ge3\), prove
\[
\frac{R(k,\ell+1)}{R(k,\ell)}\to1\quad(\ell\to\infty).
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Show
\[
R(k-1,\ell+1)=o(R(k,\ell)).
\]
Combined with the standard recursion, this directly forces ratio convergence.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (recursive upper bound):
\[
R(k,\ell+1)\le R(k,\ell)+R(k-1,\ell+1).
\]
Proof.
Classical Ramsey recursion from neighborhood partition at one vertex. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (trivial lower monotonicity):
\[
R(k,\ell+1)\ge R(k,\ell).
\]
Proof.
Avoiding \(K_k\) and \(\overline K_\ell\) is weaker than avoiding \(K_k\) and \(\overline K_{\ell+1}\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies limit 1):
If A holds, then
\[
1\le \frac{R(k,\ell+1)}{R(k,\ell)}\le 1+\frac{R(k-1,\ell+1)}{R(k,\ell)}=1+o(1).
\]
Proof.
Combine B1 and B2 and divide by \(R(k,\ell)\). Squeeze theorem gives limit \(1\). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is proving sufficiently strong separation between adjacent \(k\)-layers of Ramsey growth.
