# EP-709

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n)$ be minimal such that, for any $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\subseteq [2,\infty)\cap\mathbb{N}$ of size $n$, in any interval $I$ of $f(n)\max(A)$ consecutive integers there exist distinct $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in I$ such that $a_i\mid x_i$.

Obtain good bounds for $f(n)$, or even an asymptotic formula.

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Sur\'{a}nyi \cite{ErSu59}, who proved\[(\log n)^c \ll f(n) \ll n^{1/2}\]for some constant $c>0$. The lower bound here can be improved, using van Doorn's lower bound for [711] from \cite{vD26}, to\[\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\ll f(n).\]See also [708].

  f(n)\gg {\log n\over \log\log n}

  Let $F(n,m)$ denote the least integer $L$ such that the interval $(m,m+L]$ contains distinct multiples of $1,2,\dots,n$.
  
  For the set $A=\{2,\dots,n\}$, the defining property of $f(n)$ implies $F(n,m)\le f(n)n$ for every $m$. On the other hand, van Doorn proved that for all sufficiently large $n$ there exists $m=m(n)$ such that $
  F(n,m)\gg n{\log n\over \log\log n}.$ Combining the two inequalities gives the claim.
  
  It is natural to guess that the truth is much closer to the lower bound than to the upper bound.

## Local Computation

 - left side: elements of \(A\),
 - right side: numbers in interval,
 - edge if divisibility holds.
- Exhaustively tested all small \(n\)-sets up to max element 22, searching minimal factor in this finite regime.

- Deep finite exhaustive scan gave estimated factor 2 for \(n=2,3,4\) in the tested regime.
