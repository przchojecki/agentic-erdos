# EP-456

## Problem Statement
Let $p_n$ be the smallest prime $\equiv 1\pmod{n}$ and let $m_n$ be the smallest integer such that $n\mid \phi(m_n)$.

Is it true that $m_n<p_n$ for almost all $n$? Does $p_n/m_n\to \infty$ for almost all $n$? Are there infinitely many primes $p$ such that $p-1$ is the only $n$ for which $m_n=p$?

## Site Remarks
Linnik's theorem (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnik%27s_theorem) implies that $p_n\leq n^{O(1)}$. It is trivial that $m_n\leq p_n$ always.

If $n=q-1$ for some prime $q$ then $m_n=p_n$. Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er79e} writes it is 'easy to show' that for infinitely many $n$ we have $m_n <p_n$, and that $m_n/n\to \infty$ for almost all $n$.

van Doorn in the comments has noted that if $n=2^{2k+1}$ then $m_n\leq 2n$ and $p_n\geq 2n+1$.

  I put together a write-up that settles the first two questions of Erdős Problem #456 unconditionally, and the third question conditionally on Dickson's conjecture for one explicit admissible triple of linear forms. The writeup is at this Overleaf link (https://www.overleaf.com/read/hqwctkrwwkgx#334083)
  
  Specifically:
  
  (Q1, Q2) Unconditional: there is a constant $c > 0$ such that $\#\{n \le x : m_n = p_n\} \ge cx$ for all sufficiently large $x$. In particular, the assertions "$m_n < p_n$ for almost all $n$" and "$p_n/m_n \to \infty$ for almost all $n$" both fail, on a set of positive lower density. No prime-tuple conjecture is used in this part of the proof.
  
  (Q3) Dickson-conditional: assume Dickson's conjecture for the admissible triple of linear forms $t$, $2t+1$, $8t+1$. Then there are infinitely many primes $p$ such that $p-1$ is the unique integer $n$ with $m_n = p$. More precisely, whenever $\ell$, $2\ell+1$, and $8\ell+1$ are all prime, the prime $p = 8\ell+1$ has this uniqueness property.
  
  Thus, I claim a (mixed) solution to this problem: the first two questions are settled outright, and the third is settled conditionally on a standard prime-tuple hypothesis stemming from Dickson's conjecture. The manuscript is lengthy (71 pages); all in all, the Q3 Dickson-conditional construction occupies only about two pages (Section 3), while the remaining sixty-odd pages (Sections 4 through 11 and the three appendices) are devoted to the unconditional Q1/Q2 proof.
  
  Some brief remarks on what is novel.
  The unconditional Q1/Q2 result rests on a weighted-logarithmic-density tightness theorem for an auxiliary divisor sum that controls the cofactors arising in the construction. This theorem is built by transferring Fan and Pomerance's natural-density tightness for the shifted-prime-divisor function $\omega^*(n)$ to weighted logarithmic density with fixed multipliers, by handling a squarefree large-ratio tail through a fractional Euler-product moment, and by treating the early-prime contribution as a level-set bound on a multiscale normal set (which is what allows the argument to proceed without any unrestricted first-moment claim).
  Downstream the architecture is 'standard': a dyadic arithmetic-progression form of Goldfeld's large-prime-factor argument, with uniformity for $b \le (\log X)^A$ and built from Siegel-Walfisz, Bombieri-Vinogradov, and Brun-Titchmarsh, supplies the base population; two- and three-linear-form upper-bound sieves which are applied only in their long-interval regimes, with line-by-line divisor-weight averages over the relevant coefficient range in the latter case, delete the bad witnesses; and a second-moment Cauchy-Schwarz step deduplicates the survivors into distinct integers.
  
  By contrast, the Dickson-conditional Q3 result, is elementary: the maximal-divisor characterization of $p$ being a uniqueness prime reduces the question, for $p = 8\ell + 1$, to checking that $\varphi(3(2\ell+1)) = 4\ell$ and $\varphi(15) = 8$ both fall below $8\ell$, which they do whenever $\ell$, $2\ell+1$, $8\ell+1$ are all prime; the triple $(t, 2t+1, 8t+1)$ is admissible, so Dickson supplies infinitely many such $\ell$.

## Local Computation
I computed exact (m_n,p_n) for 2<=n<=1500.

- In this range, m_n < p_n for 358 out of 1499 values (~23.9%).
- Equality m_n=p_n occurs for ~76.1% in this range.
- Power-of-two odd-exponent samples (n=2^(2k+1)) consistently satisfy strict
 inequality in tested cases with potentially large ratios (e.g. n=512,
 p_n/m_n ~ 9.96).

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Finite frequencies up to 1500 are far from proving almost-all behavior.

Target:
Establish almost-all inequalities between
\[
m_n:=\min\{m:\ n\mid \phi(m)\},\qquad
p_n:=\min\{p\equiv1\pmod n,\ p\ \text{prime}\},
\]
including \(m_n<p_n\) and \(p_n/m_n\to\infty\) for almost all \(n\).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a dense family construction: for density-1 set of \(n\), one can build \(m\) with \(n\mid\phi(m)\) using many small prime-power factors, giving
\[
m_n \le n\,(\log n)^{O(1)},
\]
while \(p_n\) is typically much larger on the same set.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (trivial comparison baseline):
\[
m_n\le p_n
\]
always, since \(\phi(p_n)=p_n-1\equiv0\pmod n\).
Proof.
Definition of \(p_n\) gives \(n\mid (p_n-1)=\phi(p_n)\), so \(p_n\) is admissible for \(m_n\). \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (strict inequality criterion):
If there exists \(m<p_n\) with \(n\mid\phi(m)\), then \(m_n<p_n\).
Proof.
By minimality of \(m_n\) among admissible \(m\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies almost-all ratio divergence):
If A gives \(m_n\le n(\log n)^C\) on density-1 set and known lower behavior gives \(p_n\) larger by superpolylog factor there, then \(p_n/m_n\to\infty\) a.e.
Proof.
Take ratio on density-1 set and use comparative growth bounds. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is proving a uniform dense-family upper bound for \(m_n\) strong enough to beat almost-all behavior of the least prime \(1\bmod n\).
