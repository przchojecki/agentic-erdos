# EP-1112

## Problem Statement
Let $1\leq d_1<d_2$ and $k\geq 3$. Does there exist an integer $r$ such that if $B=\{b_1<\cdots\}$ is a lacunary sequence of positive integers with $b_{i+1}\geq rb_i$ then there exists a sequence of positive integers $A=\{a_1<\cdots\}$ such that\[d_1\leq a_{i+1}-a_i\leq d_2\]for all $i\geq 1$ and $(kA)\cap B=\emptyset$, where $kA$ is the $k$-fold sumset?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s and Graham \cite{ErGr80} noted that if $B=\{b_1<b_2<\cdots\}$ with $b_1\geq 5$ and $b_{i+1}\geq 2b_i$ then there is a set $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ with $2\leq a_{k+1}-a_k\leq 3$ for all $k$ such that $(A+A)\cap B=\emptyset$. Bollob\'{a}s, Hegyv\'{a}ri, and Jin \cite{BHJ97} showed that such an $A$ must exist if $b_{i+1}\geq 2b_i-O(1)$, and that this is best possible.

Erd\H{o}s and Graham go on to say 'whether such behavior can hold for $A+A+A$ (or more summands) is not known'. The above question is my best interpretation of what they intended.

Bollob\'{a}s, Hegyv\'{a}ri, and Jin \cite{BHJ97} provide a negative answer in that, for any sequence of integers $1\leq r_1<r_2<\cdots$, there is a $B$ as above with $b_{i+1}\geq r_ib_i$ such that $(A+A+A)\cap B\neq\emptyset$ for any $A$ with $2\leq a_{i+1}-a_i\leq 3$.

They define, more generally, $r_k(d_1,d_2)$ as the smallest $r$ (if it exists) such that if $b_{i+1}\geq rb_i$ then there exists $A$ with $d_1\leq a_{i+1}-a_i\leq d_2$ such that $(kA)\cap B=\emptyset$, where $kA$ is the $k$-fold sumset.

It follows from the above results that $r_2(2,3)=2$ and that $r_3(2,3)$ does not exist. Chen \cite{Ch00} proved that $r_2(a,b)\leq 2$ for any integers $a<b$ with $b\neq 2a$, and that $r_2(a,2a)\geq 2$ for all integers $a$. The more general question of existence of $r_k(a,b)$ for $k\geq 3$ remains open.

Some further technical non-existence results are given by Tang and Yang \cite{TaYa21}.

[Note the stated problem is a generous interpretation of a very ambiguous remark in \cite{ErGr80}, so it might be more appropriate to call this a problem 'inspired by Erd\H{o}s and Graham'.]

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - Greedy bounded-gap constructions for k=3, d1=2, d2=3 are highly sensitive to B: very long avoidance for B={2^i}, immediate obstruction for B={3^i}, intermediate for random lacunary examples.

 - Finite behavior is strongly model-dependent, consistent with the known delicate/non-universal nature of k>=3 cases.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
For fixed \(k\ge3\) and lacunary \(B\), decide when there exist bounded-gap sequences avoiding \(k\)-term progression/difference patterns induced by \(B\).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a dichotomy criterion on \(B\): either a uniform modular obstruction exists forcing bounded-length avoidance sets, or no such obstruction exists and one can build arbitrarily long bounded-gap avoiders.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (finite-window obstruction inheritance):
If a residue-class obstruction modulo \(M\) blocks one admissible extension step, then periodic lifting blocks infinitely many steps.
Proof.
Constraint depends only on residues modulo \(M\). Once a residue is forbidden at one level, all integers in that class remain forbidden by periodicity. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (bounded-gap to density proxy):
A sequence with gaps \(\le D\) up to \(N\) has lower density at least \(1/D\) on \([1,N]\) up to boundary terms.
Proof.
At most \(D\) integers per selected element in partition by forward intervals of length \(D\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies qualitative classification):
If Lemma A gives necessary-and-sufficient modular obstruction criterion, then each \((k,B)\) case is decidable in principle.
Proof.
Check criterion; obstruction side gives impossibility, non-obstruction side gives constructive arbitrarily long (or infinite) bounded-gap avoiders. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The main gap is proving that all long-range failures come from finite modular obstructions rather than genuinely aperiodic global phenomena.
