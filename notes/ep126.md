# EP-126

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n)$ be maximal such that if $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ has $\lvert A\rvert=n$ then $\prod_{a\neq b\in A}(a+b)$ has at least $f(n)$ distinct prime factors. Is it true that $f(n)/\log n\to\infty$?

## Site Remarks
Investigated by Erd\H{o}s and Tur\'{a}n \cite{ErTu34} (prompted by a question of L\'{a}z\'{a}r and Gr\"{u}nwald) in their first joint paper, where they proved that\[\log n \ll f(n) \ll n/\log n\](the upper bound is trivial, taking $A=\{1,\ldots,n\}$). Erd\H{o}s says that $f(n)=o(n/\log n)$ has never been proved, but perhaps never seriously attacked.

This problem has been formalised in Lean (https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/126.lean) as part of the Google DeepMind Formal Conjectures project (https://google-deepmind.github.io/formal-conjectures/).

## Local Computation
- explicit computation of distinct prime factors in \prod_{a\neq b\in A}(a+b) for candidate sets A.
- Exact finite minima on bounded ranges grew from 2 (n=4) to 4 (n=6), and heuristic best values continued increasing for larger n.
- Obstacle: finite bounded-range minima do not directly imply asymptotic lower growth of f(n)/\log n; proving divergence needs global arithmetic structure beyond search.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background gives the classical bounds \(\log n\ll f(n)\ll n/\log n\).

What remains unproved is growth beyond a constant multiple of \(\log n\), together with the sharper upper refinements mentioned in the note.

Target:
Show
\[
\frac{f(n)}{\log n}\to\infty,
\]
where \(f(n)\) is the guaranteed number of distinct prime divisors of
\(\prod_{a\ne b\in A}(a+b)\), \(|A|=n\).
Step A: Prime-support expansion
Prove many distinct residue classes mod \(p\) must appear among pair sums as
\(A\) varies.
Step B: Multiplicative large-sieve transfer
Convert residue-spread into lower bounds for number of contributing primes.
Step C: Uniform minimax extraction
Upgrade average prime-divisor growth to worst-case guaranteed growth over all
\(A\) of size \(n\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a minimax large-sieve inequality tailored to the pair-sum multiset
\(\{a+b:a\ne b\in A\}\).

Proposition AN1 (Prime divisor counting identity):
Let
\[
P_A=\prod_{a\ne b\in A}(a+b),\qquad \omega(P_A)=\#\{p:\ p\mid P_A\}.
\]
Then
\[
\omega(P_A)=\sum_{p} \mathbf 1_{\exists a\ne b,\ p\mid (a+b)}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By definition, \(p\mid P_A\) iff \(p\) divides at least one factor \(a+b\).
\(\square\)
Proposition AN2 (Reduction to prime-hit lower bounds):
If one proves that for every \(|A|=n\),
\[
\#\{p\le X:\exists a\ne b,\ p\mid(a+b)\}\ge \log n\cdot \omega_1(n),
\quad \omega_1(n)\to\infty,
\]
for some \(X=X(n)\), then \(f(n)/\log n\to\infty\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By AN1, \(f(n)\ge \omega(P_A)\) for worst-case \(A\), and the displayed bound
forces super-logarithmic growth.
\(\square\)
