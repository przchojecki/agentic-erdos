# EP-413

## Problem Statement
Let $\omega(n)$ count the number of distinct primes dividing $n$. Are there infinitely many $n$ such that, for all $m<n$, we have $m+\omega(m) \leq n$?

Can one show that there exists an $\epsilon>0$ such that there are infinitely many $n$ where $m+\epsilon \omega(m)\leq n$ for all $m<n$?

## Site Remarks
In \cite{Er79} Erd\H{o}s calls such an $n$ a 'barrier' for $\omega$. Some other natural number theoretic functions (such as $\phi$ and $\sigma$) have no barriers because they increase too rapidly. Erd\H{o}s believed that $\omega$ should have infinitely many barriers. In \cite{Er79d} he proves that $F(n)=\prod k_i$, where $n=\prod p_i^{k_i}$, has infinitely many barriers (in fact the set of barriers has positive density in the integers).

Erd\H{o}s also believed that $\Omega$, the count of the number of prime factors with multiplicity), should have infinitely many barriers. Selfridge found the largest barrier for $\Omega$ which is $<10^5$ is $99840$.

In \cite{ErGr80} this problem is suggested as a way of showing that the iterated behaviour of $n\mapsto n+\omega(n)$ eventually settles into a single sequence, regardless of the starting value of $n$ (see also [412] and [414]).

Erd\H{o}s and Graham report it could be attacked by sieve methods, but 'at present these methods are not strong enough'.

Lau \cite{La26} (see [679]) has provided a positive answer to the second question, and proved a weaker version of the first: there exists a constant $C$ such that, for infinitely many $n$,\[m+\omega(m) \leq n\]for all $1\leq m\leq n-C$.

The sequence of barriers for $\omega$ is A005236 (https://oeis.org/A005236) in the OEIS.

This is discussed in problem B8 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}. See also [647] and [679]

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Prove infinitude of barrier integers for conditions like
\[
m+\omega(m)\le n\quad\forall m<n
\]
and weakened \(\varepsilon\omega(m)\)-versions.
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Show a controlled-extremal lemma: maxima of \(m+\alpha\omega(m)\) on \([1,n)\) are attained on a sparse structured subsequence with explicit growth gaps, enabling construction of infinitely many barrier \(n\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (barrier definition equivalence):
\(n\) is a barrier for coefficient \(\alpha\) iff
\[
\max_{m<n}\bigl(m+\alpha\omega(m)\bigr)\le n.
\]
Proof.
Direct rewrite of universal inequality over \(m<n\). \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (monotone envelope):
Define \(M_\alpha(x):=\max_{m\le x}(m+\alpha\omega(m)-m)=\alpha\max_{m\le x}\omega(m)\). Barrier growth is controlled by increments of this envelope.
Proof.
Separate linear \(m\) term and additive fluctuation \(\alpha\omega(m)\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies infinitude):
If A gives infinitely many \(x\) where envelope increment is smaller than next integer step, corresponding \(n\) values are barriers infinitely often.
Proof.
At such \(n\), maximal prior value stays below \(n\), satisfying B1. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is obtaining sharp extremal control of \(\omega(m)\) along initial segments with enough uniformity to force infinitely many barriers.
