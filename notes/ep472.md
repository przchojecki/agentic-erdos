# EP-472

## Problem Statement
Given some initial finite sequence of primes $q_1<\cdots<q_m$ extend it so that $q_{n+1}$ is the smallest prime of the form $q_n+q_i-1$ for $n\geq m$. Is there an initial starting sequence so that the resulting sequence is infinite?

## Site Remarks
A problem due to Ulam. For example if we begin with $3,5$ then the sequence continues $3,5,7,11,13,17,\ldots$. It is possible that this sequence is infinite.

## Forum Comments
- 06:18 on 24 Aug 2025 - StijnC:
  That sequence should work indeed.
  Heuristically reasons:
  $q_n+q_i-1$ is odd, and residues are not fixed modulo an odd prime.
  The probability that a number of the order $x$ is prime is roughly $1/ \log x$.
  Thus on average $q_{n+1}=q_n+\log(q_n)^{2+o(1)}$ and the expectation of failing should be small (we estimate $q_n=n^{1+o(1)}$ and probability of failing $(1-1/\log q_n)^n=exp(-n^{1-o(1)})$.
  An easy check shows that the list continues at least a million steps, and $\sum_{n=10^6}^{\infty} \exp(-n^{1-o(1)})$ will be very small.

## Local Proof Attempts
Attempt route:
Tested growth behavior for standard seed examples and looked for termination
mechanisms from modular obstructions.

Obstacle:
Long finite runs do not prove infinitude.

Target:
Show existence (or impossibility) of a seed producing an infinite prime sequence under
\[
q_{n+1}=\min\{q_n+q_i-1:\ 1\le i\le n,\ \text{prime}\}.
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a nontermination criterion: if seed-generated set of offsets \(\{q_i-1\}\) hits infinitely many admissible residue classes modulo every fixed modulus, then prime candidates persist indefinitely.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (candidate form):
Each step searches primes in arithmetic-progressive translate set
\[
\mathcal C_n=\{q_n+q_i-1:1\le i\le n\}.
\]
Proof.
Definition of recurrence. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (termination condition):
Process terminates at \(n\) iff all elements of \(\mathcal C_n\) are composite.
Proof.
By minimal-prime selection rule. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies infinitude):
If A ensures at least one prime in \(\mathcal C_n\) for all \(n\), recurrence continues forever.
Proof.
Then B2 never occurs. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is proving sufficiently uniform prime occurrence in the evolving candidate sets \(\mathcal C_n\), which are highly dependent on earlier steps.
