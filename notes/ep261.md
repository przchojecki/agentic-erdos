# EP-261

## Problem Statement
Are there infinitely many $n$ such that there exists some $t\geq 2$ and distinct integers $a_1,\ldots,a_t\geq 1$ such that\[\frac{n}{2^n}=\sum_{1\leq k\leq t}\frac{a_k}{2^{a_k}}?\]Is this true for all $n$? Is there a rational $x$ such that\[x = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{a_k}{2^{a_k}}\]has at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ solutions?

## Site Remarks
Related to [260].

In \cite{Er88c} Erd\H{o}s notes that Cusick had a simple proof that there do exist infinitely many such $n$. Erd\H{o}s does not record what this was, but a later paper by Borwein and Loring \cite{BoLo90} provides the following proof: for every positive integer $m$ and $n=2^{m+1}-m-2$ we have\[\frac{n}{2^n}=\sum_{n<k\leq n+m}\frac{k}{2^k}.\]Tengely, Ulas, and Zygadlo \cite{TUZ20} have verified that all $n\leq 10000$ have the required property.

In \cite{Er88c} Erd\H{o}s weakens the second question to asking for the existence of a rational $x$ which has two solutions.

- 23:10 on 27 Apr 2026 - Zeraoulia Rafik:
  I would like to point out a small observation about the weakened question
  mentioned in [Er88c], namely the existence of a rational number \(x\)
  which has two representations.
  
  Interpreting a representation as a choice of a set \(A\subseteq \mathbb N\)
  of distinct positive integers, so that\[
  x=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{a}{2^a},
  \]one gets such an example from the finite identity\[
  \frac{4}{2^4}=\frac{5}{2^5}+\frac{6}{2^6}.
  \]Both sides are equal to \(1/4\).
  
  Now use the classical identity\[
  \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{2^m}=2.
  \]Let\[
  A=\mathbb N\setminus\{5,6\},
  \qquad
  B=\mathbb N\setminus\{4\}.
  \]Then\[
  \sum_{a\in A}\frac{a}{2^a}
  =
  2-\frac{5}{2^5}-\frac{6}{2^6}
  =
  2-\frac14
  =
  \frac74,
  \]whereas\[
  \sum_{b\in B}\frac{b}{2^b}
  =
  2-\frac{4}{2^4}
  =
  2-\frac14
  =
  \frac74.
  \]Therefore the rational number\[
  x=\frac74
  \]has two different representations:\[
  \frac74
  =
  \sum_{a\in \mathbb N\setminus\{5,6\}}\frac{a}{2^a}
  =
  \sum_{b\in \mathbb N\setminus\{4\}}\frac{b}{2^b}.
  \]Thus, under this literal interpretation, the weakened question has a positive
  answer. Of course, this uses two representations with a large common tail;
  if the intended question requires some stronger non-cancellation or
  non-degeneracy condition, then that would be a different version.

Let \(B\subset\mathbb N\) be representable integers with
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathbb N}\min\{b\in B: b\ge x\}-x \le L.

all sufficiently large integers are representable.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Given large \(x\), choose \(b\in B\) with \(x\le b\le x+L\). Backward/forward
closure across a length-\(L\) interval fills the gap.
\(\square\)

\textit{Proof.}
