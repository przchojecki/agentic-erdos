# EP-824

## Problem Statement
Let $h(x)$ count the number of integers $1\leq a<b<x$ such that $(a,b)=1$ and $\sigma(a)=\sigma(b)$, where $\sigma$ is the sum of divisors function.

Is it true that $h(x)>x^{2-o(1)}$?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er74b} proved that $\limsup h(x)/x= \infty$, and claimed a similar proof for this problem. A complete proof that $h(x)/x\to \infty$ was provided by Pollack and Pomerance \cite{PoPo16}.

A similar question can be asked if we replace the condition $(a,b)=1$ with the condition that $a$ and $b$ are squarefree. Weisenberg suggests another variant, with the condition that there are no proper factors $u\mid a$ and $v\mid b$ such that $\sigma(u)=\sigma(v)$ and $(u,a/u)=(v,b/v)=1$, which is the weakest restriction one can impose that is still strong enough to eliminate trivial duplicates.

  [PoPo16] closely ties this problem to that of locating many primes which are one less than a smooth number (one uses products of such primes to create lots of collisions $\sigma(a)=\sigma(b)$). In particular, if for any fixed $\theta>1$ and large $y$ it can be shown that there are $\gg y^{\theta-o(1)}$ primes $p \leq y^\theta$ with $p+1$ $y$-rough, then the arguments in that paper give a positive answer to this question. (This type of result is known for $\theta \approx 3.377$, giving rise to a lower bound roughly of the form $h(x) \gg x^{1.4}$.)

## Local Computation
- Implemented deeper finite counting for $x=10^4,2\cdot10^4,3\cdot10^4,4\cdot10^4,5\cdot10^4$.
- For each $x$, grouped integers by equal $\sigma$ value and counted coprime pairs exactly.

- Measured values increase from $h(10^4)=15073$ to $h(5\cdot10^4)=125083$.
- Ratios $h(x)/x$ increase (about $1.51\to 2.50$), matching known superlinear behavior.
- Effective finite log-slope is around $1.08$, still far below the conjectural near-$2$ regime.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite computation groups integers by equal divisor-sum and then counts coprime pairs exactly, giving a direct profile for \(h(x)\) on explicit ranges. The growth is clearly superlinear in the tested window, matching known theory, but the effective exponent is still nowhere near the conjectural near-quadratic scale.
