# EP-766

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n;k,l)=\min \mathrm{ex}(n;G)$, where $G$ ranges over all graphs with $k$ vertices and $l$ edges.

Give good estimates for $f(n;k,l)$ in the range $k<l\leq k^2/4$. For fixed $k$ and large $n$ is $f(n;k,l)$ a strictly monotone function of $l$?

## Local Computation
- Built standalone sampled finite proxy:
 - sample many \(k\)-vertex, \(\ell\)-edge graphs,
 - approximate \(\mathrm{ex}(n;G)\) by greedy edge insertion avoiding copies of \(G\),
 - take sampled minimum over the sampled \(G\)'s.
- Ran for \(k=6\), \(\ell\in\{7,8,9\}\), \(n\in\{9,10,11\}\).

- Sampled minima were in a relatively narrow range (about 12 to 19 edges in tested sizes).
- Within this small finite scan, monotonic separation between \(\ell=8\) and \(\ell=9\) was not clear (some rows coincide), suggesting monotonicity is subtle at small scales.
- These are sampled proxies only, not exact values of \(f(n;k,\ell)\).

## Local Proof Attempts
I used a sampled finite proxy for the interior extremal problem by generating many \((k,\ell)\)-graphs and approximating \(\mathrm{ex}(n;G)\) through copy-avoiding greedy construction. The resulting values suggest that strict monotonicity in \(\ell\) is subtle even on small instances, but sampled proxies are far too weak to determine the true function \(f(n;k,\ell)\).
