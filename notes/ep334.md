# EP-334

## Problem Statement
Find the best function $f(n)$ such that every $n$ can be written as $n=a+b$ where both $a,b$ are $f(n)$-smooth (that is, are not divisible by any prime $p>f(n)$.)

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s originally asked if even $f(n)\leq n^{1/3}$ is true. This is known, and the best bound is due to Balog \cite{Ba89} who proved that\[f(n) \ll_\epsilon n^{\frac{4}{9\sqrt{e}}+\epsilon}\]for all $\epsilon>0$. (Note $\frac{4}{9\sqrt{e}}=0.2695\ldots$.)

It is likely that $f(n)\leq n^{o(1)}$.

See also Problem 59 (https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf) on Green's open problems list.

  I asked the Deep Research tool of Gemini for the relevant literature on this problem twice, and both times it couldn't really find anything interesting beyond the already mentioned result by Balog. It did mention that a bound of $f(n) < \exp(c \sqrt{\log n \log \log n})$ is known for sums $a+b+c$ of three elements instead of two (see e.g. this (https://akjournals.com/view/journals/10473/67/4/article-p333.xml) paper by Sárközy), similar to how ternary Goldbach is significantly more approachable than binary Goldbach.
  
  To paraphrase from the comments on Green's list: even a bound of $f(n) \le n^{0.1}$ seems extremely difficult, as it implies small quadratic non-residues. To elaborate on that, if all large enough $n$ can be written as $a+b$ with both $a$ and $b$ $n^{0.1}$-smooth, then this in particular holds for all sufficiently large primes $p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$. For these primes this implies that one of $a, b$ is a quadratic non-residue, implying that a quadratic non-residue exists which is at most $p^{0.1}$. The best known upper bound for this problem has exponent $\frac{1}{4\sqrt{e}} + \epsilon \approx 0.15$ instead of $0.1$, however.
  
  Finally, in the Sárközy paper linked above he states that Erdős conjectures that the bound $f(n) < \exp(c \sqrt{\log n \log \log n})$ also holds for the binary problem considered here. I did not find the slightly stronger $f(n) < \exp(c \sqrt{\log n})$ conjectured anywhere.

## Local Proof Attempts
The old n^{1/3} target is already known true (and improved in background to exponent 4/(9\sqrt e)+epsilon).

Hard point:
Best asymptotic order of f(n) (e.g. n^{o(1)}) is not settled by finite scans.

Target:
Determine asymptotic order of minimal smoothness threshold \(f(n)\) in
\(n=a+b\) with both \(a,b\) \(y\)-smooth.
Step A: Friable counting in additive slices
Estimate number of \(y\)-smooth integers in \([1,n]\) and pair sums hitting \(n\).
Step B: Exceptional-set control
Bound integers \(n\) with no \(y\)-smooth decomposition by sieve over short
intervals.
Step C: Optimize \(y=y(n)\)
Choose \(y\) to make exception set sparse enough for all large \(n\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform additive friable theorem: for chosen \(y(n)\), every sufficiently
large \(n\) has at least one representation \(n=a+b\) with both \(a,b\in
\Psi(\cdot,y)\).

Lemma 1 (Representation counting identity):
Let \(S_y(n)=\{m\le n:\ P^+(m)\le y\}\). Number of \(y\)-smooth decompositions is
\[
R_y(n)=\#\{a\in S_y(n):\ n-a\in S_y(n)\}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Each decomposition \(n=a+b\) corresponds to unique \(a\), with \(b=n-a\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Sufficient condition for existence):
If \(R_y(n)\ge1\), then \(f(n)\le y\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Direct from definition of \(f(n)\) as minimal such \(y\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction):
To prove \(f(n)\le Y(n)\) eventually, it suffices to prove \(R_{Y(n)}(n)\ge1\)
for all sufficiently large \(n\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply Lemma 2 pointwise for large \(n\).
\(\square\)
