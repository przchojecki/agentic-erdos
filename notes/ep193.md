# EP-193

## Problem Statement
Let $S\subseteq \mathbb{Z}^3$ be a finite set and let $A=\{a_1,a_2,\ldots,\}\subset \mathbb{Z}^3$ be an infinite $S$-walk, so that $a_{i+1}-a_i\in S$ for all $i$. Must $A$ contain three collinear points?

## Site Remarks
Originally conjectured by Gerver and Ramsey \cite{GeRa79}, who showed that the answer is yes for $\mathbb{Z}^2$, and for $\mathbb{Z}^3$ that the largest number of collinear points can be bounded.

## Site References
- [GeRa79] Gerver, Joseph L. and Ramsey, L. Thomas, On certain sequences of lattice points. Pacific J. Math. (1979), 357-363.

## Forum Comments
- 13:23 on 07 Oct 2025 - Adenwalla:
  Gerver and Ramsey proved that there exists an infinite $S$-walk in $\mathbb{Z}^3$ that has no $5^{11}+1$ collinear points. Recent work by Lidbetter in Improved bound for the Gerver-Ramsey collinearity problem (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2023.113718) improved this bound to $189$.
  It is not known if there exists an $n$ such that there exists an infinite $S$-walk in $\mathbb{Z}^n$ that avoids $3$ collinear points.

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Finite failure to extend walks is not a proof of inevitability of collinear triples
for all infinite walks and all finite S.

Target:
For every finite \(S\subset\mathbb Z^3\), prove every infinite \(S\)-walk
contains three collinear points, or classify exceptional step sets.
Step A: Directional-combinatorial encoding
Translate walk increments into constrained sequences in finitely many directions.
Step B: Collinearity forcing criterion
Establish a density/recurrence condition that guarantees 3-collinearity.
Step C: Uniformity over \(S\)
Show every finite \(S\) meets the forcing condition (or isolate counterexamples).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a finite-step-set Ramsey-type theorem for polygonal lines in \(\mathbb Z^3\)
forcing collinear triples under infinite extension.

Proposition BZ1 (Finite obstruction principle):
If there exists an infinite \(S\)-walk with no collinear triple, then for every
\(L\) there exists a length-\(L\) \(S\)-walk with no collinear triple.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Take initial segments of length \(L\).
\(\square\)
Proposition BZ2 (Compactness reduction):
If one proves a universal \(L(S)\) such that every length-\(L(S)\) \(S\)-walk has
a collinear triple, then every infinite \(S\)-walk has one.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply BZ1 contrapositively.
\(\square\)
