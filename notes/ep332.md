# EP-332

## Problem Statement
Let $A\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ and $D(A)$ be the set of those numbers which occur infinitely often as $a_1-a_2$ with $a_1,a_2\in A$. What conditions on $A$ are sufficient to ensure $D(A)$ has bounded gaps?

## Site Remarks
Prikry, Tijdeman, Stewart, and others (see the survey articles \cite{St78} and \cite{Ti79}) have shown that a sufficient condition is that $A$ has positive density.

One can also ask what conditions are sufficient for $D(A)$ to have positive density, or for $\sum_{d\in D(A)}\frac{1}{d}=\infty$, or even just $D(A)\neq\emptyset$.

- 14:40 on 04 May 2026 - aditya:
  An observation by GPT 5.5 pro : the known sufficient condition “positive natural density” can be weakened to positive upper Banach density.
  
  (Please see this (https://drive.google.com/file/d/1B_i5L-7iBVx2vl-2EgKn6F4_r9gr8qX8/view?usp=drivesdk) for the full proof)
  
  Let\[
  d^*(A)=\limsup_{L\to\infty}\sup_M \frac{|A\cap [M,M+L)|}{L}.
  \]If \(d^*(A)>0\), then \(D(A)\) has bounded gaps.
  
  Proof sketch. Put \(\delta=d^*(A)\), and let\[
  P=\{h\in\mathbb Z: d^*(A\cap(A-h))>0\}.
  \]Then \(P\cap\mathbb N\subseteq D(A)\). I claim \(P\) is syndetic.
  
  Indeed, if \(t_0,\dots,t_m\in\mathbb Z\) and no nonzero difference \(t_i-t_j\) lies in \(P\), then the translates \(A-t_i\) have pairwise intersections of upper Banach density zero. Taking intervals on which \(A\) has density close to \(\delta\), and shifting them by only \(O_{t_i}(1)\), all \(A-t_i\) have density close to \(\delta\) on a common sequence of intervals. Inclusion-exclusion then gives\[
  1\ge (m+1)\delta-o(1).
  \]So \(m+1\le 1/\delta\).
  
  Thus any subset of \(\mathbb Z\) whose nonzero pairwise differences avoid \(P\) has size at most \(1/\delta\). Choose such a set \(F\) maximal. By maximality, every \(z\in\mathbb Z\) satisfies \(z-f\in P\) for some \(f\in F\), so \(P+F=\mathbb Z\). Hence \(P\) is syndetic. Therefore \(P\cap\mathbb N\), and hence \(D(A)\), has bounded gaps.
  
  This is not meant as a full characterization of the sets \(A\) for which \(D(A)\) has bounded gaps, just a slightly stronger sufficient condition.

## Local Computation
I ran finite proxies on several families (squares, primes, powers of 2,
random sparse/dense sets), tracking difference-frequency gap behavior.

Finite scans show dense/random sets quickly produce very small difference gaps,
while sparse structured sets (e.g. powers of 2) keep large gaps under stricter
frequency thresholds.
This is consistent with the background point that positive density is a strong
sufficient condition.

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Finite frequency proxies are not equivalent to the infinite-occurrence definition
of D(A), so this does not produce new sharp sufficient criteria.

Target:
Find sufficient conditions on \(A\subset\mathbb N\) ensuring bounded gaps in
\[
D(A)=\{d\ge1:\ d=a_1-a_2\ \text{infinitely often with }a_i\in A\}.
\]
Step A: Correlation lower bound hypothesis
Assume quantitative pair-correlation:
\[
|\{(x,x+d)\in A^2:\ x\le X\}|\ge cX
\]
for many \(d\) uniformly.
Step B: Infinite-occurrence upgrade
Convert positive-density-in-\(X\) pair counts into \(d\in D(A)\).
Step C: Gap control
Show every interval of \(d\)-values of bounded length contains one satisfying
Step A.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform correlation-to-coverage theorem: from averaged pair-correlation on
short \(d\)-intervals, deduce at least one infinitely-often difference in each.

Lemma 1 (Infinite occurrences from linear counts):
If for fixed \(d\),
\[
\limsup_{X\to\infty}\#\{x\le X:\ x,x+d\in A\}=+\infty,
\]
then \(d\in D(A)\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Unbounded count provides infinitely many distinct witness pairs.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Bounded-gap criterion):
If there exists \(L\) such that every interval \([t,t+L]\) contains some \(d\)
with \(d\in D(A)\), then \(D(A)\) has gaps at most \(L\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By definition, no run of \(L+1\) consecutive integers can avoid \(D(A)\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction):
To prove bounded gaps in \(D(A)\), it suffices to establish an \(L\) for which
each \([t,t+L]\) contains a \(d\) satisfying Lemma 1.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply Lemma 2 once Lemma 1 conditions are verified interval-by-interval.
\(\square\)
