# EP-873

## Problem Statement
Let $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ and let $F(A,X,k)$ count the number of $i$ such that\[[a_i,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{i+k-1}] < X,\]where the left-hand side is the least common multiple. Is it true that, for every $\epsilon >0$, there exists some $k$ such that\[F(A,X,k)<X^\epsilon?\]

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Szemer\'{e}di, who proved that for every $A$\[F(A,X,3) \ll X^{1/3}\log X,\]and there is an $A$ such that\[F(A,X,3) \gg X^{1/3}\log X\]for infinitely many $X$. There may be a sequence for which this holds for every $X$.

  I would like to share my partial result on this problem (https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qP1jYDBVdunSfbdvoGAdl3XJgGRXsK3U/view?usp=sharing).
  
  This partial result proves two unconditional bounds that go beyond the usual triple-block exponent. In particular, it proves\[
  F(A,X,11)\ll X^{223/676}\log X
  \]and\[
  F(A,X,6)\ll X^{437/1320}\log X.
  \]Both exponents are strictly smaller than \(1/3\), so the result shows that the classical triple-block barrier can be crossed for longer consecutive blocks.
  
  The main idea is to combine the Vandermonde lcm inequality with gcd structure forced inside compact near-critical blocks. Around the difficult scale \(a_i\asymp X^{1/2}\), a good block must contain substantial pairwise gcd structure. The argument organizes this information through incident-star and complete-edge configurations, which then give stronger span bounds and lead to the counting estimates above.
  
  I view this as genuine partial progress on the problem, rather than a solution of the full \(X^\varepsilon\) question. I used GPT 5.4 Thinking for helping me with algebraic notation and verifying the contribution, and it passed. ## Local Computation
- Computed empirical profiles of $F(A,X,k)$ for multiple model sequences:
 - naturals, odds, primes, powers of 2,
 - $k=3,4,5,6$,
 - $X$ from $10^3$ to $10^6$.
- Recorded endpoint log-log slopes as finite scaling proxies.

- For all tested sequences, increasing $k$ lowers the empirical scaling slope.
- Powers-of-2 model gives especially small slopes in this finite range.
- These finite profiles support the direction that larger $k$ suppresses counts, but do not prove the universal quantifier claim.

## Local Proof Attempts
The bounded-depth intersection search tries to witness the common-difference phenomenon directly by intersecting the sets \(D(N_i)\). It recovers the small solved cases in range, but non-findings at higher \(k\) are inconclusive because the search itself is heavily truncated.
