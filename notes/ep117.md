# EP-117

## Problem Statement
Let $h(n)$ be minimal such that any group $G$ with the property that any subset of $>n$ elements contains some $x\neq y$ such that $xy=yx$ can be covered by at most $h(n)$ many Abelian subgroups.

Estimate $h(n)$ as well as possible.

## Site Remarks
Pyber \cite{Py87} has proved there exist constants $c_2>c_1>1$ such that $c_1^n<h(n)<c_2^n$. Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er97f} writes that the lower bound was already known to Isaacs.

## Local Computation
- exact algebraic computations in a concrete group family tied to the EP condition.
- In tested dihedral cases, the computed minimum abelian-subgroup cover matched the maximal pairwise noncommuting subset size, giving a clean finite profile.
- Obstacle: EP-117 asks for worst-case asymptotic behavior across all groups under a global commutativity-threshold condition; small-family exact data gives structure but not the universal exponential-rate constant.

## Local Proof Attempts
Background cites Pyber and earlier lower-bound work proving constants \(c_2>c_1>1\) with \(c_1^n<h(n)<c_2^n\), so the growth type is already known to be exponential in \(n\).

The exact asymptotic rate, such as the sharp exponential base or finer secondary terms, is not determined by the cited references.

Target:
Determine sharp asymptotics of \(h(n)\), the minimal number of abelian subgroups
needed in the covering problem under bounded noncommuting-set size.
Step A: Structural reduction
Reduce to almost-simple / nilpotent-by-abelian decomposition classes with
uniform control on clique number of noncommuting graph.
Step B: Matching upper/lower entropy bounds
Prove exponential upper covers and exponential lower obstructions with matching
base.
Step C: Extremal family identification
Characterize families achieving the asymptotic base.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a universal inequality linking abelian-cover number to noncommuting-clique
number with asymptotically sharp constant factor.

Proposition AH1 (Graph-theoretic reformulation):
Let \(\Gamma_G\) be the noncommuting graph of a finite group \(G\). Then an
abelian-subgroup cover of \(G\) corresponds to a clique cover of the complement
\(\overline{\Gamma_G}\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Vertices commute exactly when adjacent in \(\overline{\Gamma_G}\). Any abelian
subgroup induces a clique in \(\overline{\Gamma_G}\), and covering elements by
abelian subgroups is equivalent to covering vertices by such cliques.
\(\square\)
Proposition AH2 (Reduction to extremal graph parameter):
If one proves, for all admissible \(G\),
\[
c_1^n \le \theta(\overline{\Gamma_G}) \le c_2^n
\]
with matching base \(c_1=c_2\), then \(h(n)\) has a sharp exponential rate.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By AH1, \(h(n)\) is exactly the extremal clique-cover number over the class.
Matching exponentials determine the base.
\(\square\)
