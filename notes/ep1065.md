# EP-1065

## Problem Statement
Are there infinitely many primes $p$ such that $p=2^kq+1$ for some prime $q$ and $k\geq 0$? Or $p=2^k3^lq+1$?

## Forum Comments
- 17:52 on 01 Nov 2025 - StijnC:
  By the Bateman–Horn conjecture, the answer should be yes for every fixed combination of $k>0$ and $\ell.$
  In particular, the number of Sophie-Germaine primes is expected to be infinite.
  
  In the other direction, $q=271129$ is an example for which no $2^n\cdot q+1$ is prime.
  See [SierpinskiNumbers] (https://t5k.org/glossary/page.php?sort=SierpinskiNumber).

## Local Proof Attempts
Both prime forms occur often in the scanned range, which is compatible with infinitude heuristics. The real difficulty is proving that this persistence survives asymptotically, rather than merely observing positive finite-range density.
