# EP-123

## Problem Statement
Let $a,b,c\geq 1$ be three integers which are pairwise coprime. Is every large integer the sum of distinct integers of the form $a^kb^lc^m$ ($k,l,m\geq 0$), none of which divide any other?

## Site Remarks
A sequence is said to be $d$-complete if every large integer is the sum of distinct integers from the sequence, none of which divide any other. This particular case of $d$-completeness was conjectured by Erd\H{o}s and Lewin \cite{ErLe96}, who (among other related results) prove this when $a=3$, $b=5$, and $c=7$.

As a partial record of progress so far, the sequence $\{a^kb^lc^m\}$ is known to be $d$-complete when:
{UL}
{LI}$a=3$, $b=5$, $c=7$ (Erd\H{o}s and Lewin \cite{ErLe96}).{/LI}
{LI}$a=2$, $b=5$, $c\in \{7,11,13,17,19\}$ (Erd\H{o}s and Lewin \cite{ErLe96}).{/LI}
{LI}$a=2$, $b=5$, $c\in \{9,21,23,27,29,31\}$ - more generally, $a=2$, $b=5$, and any $c>6$ with $(c,10)=1$ such that there exists $N$ where every integer in $(N,25cN)$ is the sum of distinct elements of $\{2^k3^lc^m\}$, none of which divide any other (Ma and Chen \cite{MaCh16}).{/LI}
{LI} $a=2$, $b=5$, $3\leq c\leq 87$ with $(c,10)=1$, or $a=2$, $b=7$, $3\leq c\leq 33$ with $(c,14)=1$, or $a=3$, $b=5$, $2\leq c\leq 14$ with $(c,15)=1$ (Chen and Yu \cite{ChYu23b}).{/LI}
{/UL}
In \cite{Er92b} Erd\H{o}s makes the stronger conjecture (for $a=2$, $b=3$, and $c=5$) that, for any $\epsilon>0$, all large integers $n$ can be written as the sum of distinct integers $b_1<\cdots <b_t$ of the form $2^k3^l5^m$ where $b_t<(1+\epsilon)b_1$.

See also [845], and [1110] for the case of two powers.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
For pairwise-coprime triples \((a,b,c)\), prove (or classify exceptions to)
\(d\)-completeness-type representation in large intervals.
Step A: Local congruence elimination
Establish complete local solvability for target residues modulo relevant moduli.
Step B: Global combinatorial covering
Use additive semigroup growth/greedy covering to represent all large integers in
a target interval.
Step C: Uniform threshold bound
Prove explicit \(N_0(a,b,c)\) such that all \(N\ge N_0\) satisfy the property.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a robust Frobenius-type bound for constrained coefficient sets with
antichain-style restrictions used in the EP setup.

Proposition AK1 (Local-global reduction template):
Suppose for fixed \((a,b,c)\):
\[
\text{(i) all sufficiently large }N\text{ are locally representable mod }M,
\]
\[
\text{(ii) every interval }[N/2,N]\text{ contains a globally representable
integer in each admissible residue class.}
\]
Then the target representation property holds for all large \(N\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Choose admissible class of \(N\) modulo \(M\), apply (ii) in \([N/2,N]\), then
lift by (i) and closure under adding admissible generators.
\(\square\)
Proposition AK2 (Threshold consequence):
If one proves AK1 with effective constants, then EP-123 is resolved for that
triple family with explicit threshold \(N_0\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Take \(N_0\) as max of constants in (i)-(ii).
\(\square\)
