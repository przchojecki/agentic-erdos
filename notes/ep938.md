# EP-938

## Problem Statement
Let $A=\{n_1<n_2<\cdots\}$ be the sequence of powerful numbers (if $p\mid n$ then $p^2\mid n$).

Are there only finitely many three-term progressions of consecutive terms $n_k,n_{k+1},n_{k+2}$?

## Local Computation
- Method: exact scan of powerful numbers up to \(B\), then test
\[
n_k+n_{k+2}=2n_{k+1}
\]
for each consecutive triple.
- Run: \(B=4\cdot 10^7\).
- Counts:
 - powerful numbers \(\le B\): \(13236\)
 - consecutive-index 3-term APs: \(6\)
- Sample APs:
 - \((1728,1764,1800)\)
 - \((6912,7056,7200)\)
