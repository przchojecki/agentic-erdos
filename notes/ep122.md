# EP-122

## Problem Statement
For which number theoretic functions $f$ is it true that, for any $F(n)$ such that $F(n)/f(n)\to 0$ for almost all $n$, there are infinitely many $x$ such that\[\frac{\#\{ n\in \mathbb{N} : n+f(n)\in (x,x+F(x))\}}{F(x)}\to \infty?\]

## Site Remarks
In \cite{Er97} Erd\H{o}s is only considering number theoretic functions which grow 'slowly' (i.e. slower than $(\log n)^{1-c}$ for some $c>0$).

This is considered by Erd\H{o}s, Pomerance, and S\'{a}rk\"{o}zy \cite{EPS97}, who prove in particular that if $\omega(n)$ counts the number of distinct prime divisors of $n$, then for all large $x$ there are intervals $I,J\subset [1,x]$ of width\[\lvert I\rvert\asymp \left(\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\right)^{1/2}\]and\[\lvert J\rvert \asymp (\log\log x)^{1/2}\]such that if $n\in I$ then $n+\omega(n)\in J$. (Note the normal order of $\omega$ is $\log\log x$, and hence $(\log\log n)^{1/2}/\omega(n)\to 0$ for almost all $n$.)

In \cite{Er97} and \cite{Er97e} Erd\H{o}s reports that he, Pomerance, and S\'{a}rkz\"{o}zy can prove the more general claim above for $f$ being $\tau(n)$, the divisor function, or $\omega(n)$, and states it 'probably fails' for $\phi(n)$ or $\sigma(n)$.

  Let\[
  R(x):=\frac{\#\{n\in \mathbb{N}: n+f(n)\in (x,x+F(x))\}}{F(x)}.
  \]I am only addressing the current online version of problem#122 as written above. I am not claiming to have resolved the historical original problem behind Problem #122.
  
  Assume throughout that\[
  f:\mathbb{N}\to [0,\infty).
  \]I claim that for every such nonnegative function \(f\), there exists an integer-valued function \(F:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\) such that\[
  \frac{f(n)}{F(n)}\to 0 \qquad (n\to\infty),
  \]but at the same time\[
  R(x)<2 \qquad \text{for every }x\in\mathbb{N}.
  \]This shows that the current online formulation is false under any natural interpretation in which the displayed ratio is supposed to become arbitrarily large as \(x\) varies.
  
  Indeed, define\[
  F(n):=1+\left\lceil n^2+f(n)^2\right\rceil \qquad (n\in\mathbb{N}).
  \]Then \(F(n)\in\mathbb{N}\) and\[
  F(n)\ge 1+n^2+f(n)^2.
  \]Hence\[
  0\le \frac{f(n)}{F(n)}\le \frac{f(n)}{n^2+f(n)^2}.
  \]By the elementary inequality\[
  n^2+f(n)^2\ge 2nf(n),
  \]we get\[
  \frac{f(n)}{n^2+f(n)^2}\le \frac{1}{2n}.
  \]Therefore\[
  0\le \frac{f(n)}{F(n)}\le \frac{1}{2n}\to 0.
  \]Now fix \(x\in\mathbb{N}\), and let\[
  A_x:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}: n+f(n)\in (x,x+F(x))\}.
  \]If \(n\in A_x\), then\[
  n+f(n)<x+F(x).
  \]Since \(f(n)\ge 0\), it follows that\[
  n\le n+f(n)<x+F(x).
  \]Thus every element of \(A_x\) is a natural number strictly smaller than \(x+F(x)\). Consequently,\[
  \#A_x\le x+F(x).
  \]Dividing by \(F(x)\), we obtain\[
  R(x)=\frac{\#A_x}{F(x)}\le 1+\frac{x}{F(x)}.
  \]But\[
  F(x)\ge 1+x^2+f(x)^2\ge 1+x^2,
  \]so\[
  R(x)\le 1+\frac{x}{x^2+1}<2.
  \]Hence\[
  \frac{\#\{n\in \mathbb{N}: n+f(n)\in (x,x+F(x))\}}{F(x)}<2
  \qquad \text{for all }x\in\mathbb{N}.
  \]

## Local Computation
- direct occupancy computation for shifted values n+f(n) in intervals (x,x+F(x)).
- Observed finite local repeats for all four functions but occupancy normalized by F(x) decreased in these tested ranges.
- Obstacle: EP-122 concerns asymptotic divergence regimes under very general F; finite-window behavior is indicative only and does not settle the function-dependent infinite criterion.

## Local Proof Attempts
Route:
Recast the statement as a local-overcrowding criterion for shifted arithmetic-function values under very weak almost-everywhere size assumptions.

Hard point:
The criterion is highly function-dependent and difficult to unify into a clean necessary-and-sufficient condition.

Target:
Characterize arithmetic functions \(f\) for which local overcrowding of values
\(n+f(n)\) in windows \((x,x+F(x))\) must diverge under broad growth assumptions
on \(F\).
Step A: Multiplicity decomposition
Write local counts via fibers of \(n\mapsto n+f(n)\) and separate smooth/rough
input regimes.
Step B: Mean-square control
Estimate second moments of local multiplicities to detect forced peaks.
Step C: Function-class criterion
Extract necessary/sufficient conditions on distribution of \(f(n+1)-f(n)\) or
inverse-image growth.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a general inverse-image bound: for most \(y\), control of
\(\#\{n: n+f(n)=y\}\) with error uniform enough for moving windows.

Proposition AJ1 (Second-moment forcing):
Let \(M_x(y)\) count \(n\) with \(x<n+f(n)\le x+F(x)\) and value \(y\). Then
\[
\max_y M_x(y)\ge \frac{\left(\sum_y M_x(y)\right)^2}{\sum_y M_x(y)^2}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Since \(\sum_y M_x(y)^2 \le (\max_y M_x(y))\sum_y M_x(y)\), rearrange.
\(\square\)
Proposition AJ2 (Reduction):
If one proves \(\sum_y M_x(y)\gg F(x)\) and
\(\sum_y M_x(y)^2\ll F(x)/\omega(x)\) for some \(\omega(x)\to\infty\), then
\[
\max_y M_x(y)\to\infty.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply AJ1 to obtain \(\max_y M_x(y)\gg \omega(x)\).
\(\square\)
