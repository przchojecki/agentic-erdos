# EP-884

## Problem Statement
Is it true that, for any $n$, if $d_1<\cdots <d_t$ are the divisors of $n$, then\[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq t}\frac{1}{d_j-d_i} \ll 1+\sum_{1\leq i<t}\frac{1}{d_{i+1}-d_i},\]where the implied constant is absolute?

  I can disprove this conjecture assuming the prime tuples conjecture (details here (https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/erdos-884.pdf)). The basic idea is to take an $n$ of the form $n = (t-h_1) \dots (t-h_K)$ where $h_1,\dots,h_K$ is an admissible tuple of diameter $\asymp K^2$ (with consecutive elements $\gg K$-separated) and $t$ is a large natural number chosen to make all the $t-h_1,\dots,t-h_K$ prime (this is where the prime tuples conjecture is used). The numerology *almost* lets one remove the prime tuples hypothesis, but I was unable to do so; perhaps someone else would like to try their luck.

## Local Computation
- Performed direct finite scan up to $n\le 200000$.
- Computed ratio
 $$R(n)=\frac{\sum_{i<j}1/(d_j-d_i)}{\sum_i 1/(d_{i+1}-d_i)}$$
for each $n$ with at least 3 divisors.

- Maximum observed ratio in this range:
 $$R(n)\approx 3.56587\quad\text{at }n=166320.$$
- Running maxima stayed in a small-constant range over checkpoints.
- Finite evidence is consistent with the conjectured bounded-ratio phenomenon.

## Local Proof Attempts
The direct scan computes the full divisor-gap ratio for every \(n\) in a large finite range and tracks its running maximum. The observed values remain bounded by a small constant, which is exactly the phenomenon the conjecture predicts, but finite boundedness is not a proof of a universal ratio bound.
