# EP-33

## Problem Statement
Let $A\subset\mathbb{N}$ be such that every large integer can be written as $n^2+a$ for some $a\in A$ and $n\geq 0$. What is the smallest possible value of\[\limsup \frac{\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert}{N^{1/2}}?\]Is\[\liminf \frac{\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert}{N^{1/2}}>1?\]

## Site Remarks
Such a set $A$ is called an additive complement of the set of squares. Erd\H{o}s observed that there exist $A$ for which the $\limsup$ is finite and $>1$. Moser \cite{Mo65} proved that, for any such $A$,\[\liminf \frac{\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert}{N^{1/2}}>1.06.\]The best-known lower bound is\[\liminf \frac{\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert}{N^{1/2}}\geq\frac{4}{\pi}\approx 1.273\]proved by Cilleruelo \cite{Ci93}, Habsieger \cite{Ha95}, and Balasubramanian and Ramana \cite{BaRa01}.

The problem of minimising the $\limsup$ appears to have been much less studied. van Doorn has a construction (https://github.com/Woett/Mathematical-shorts/blob/main/The%20smallest%20set%20such%20that%20every%20positive%20integer%20is%20the%20sum%20of%20a%20square%20and%20an%20element%20from%20this%20set.pdf) of such an $A$ in which, for all $N$,\[\frac{\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert}{N^{1/2}}< 2\phi^{5/2}\approx 6.66,\]where $\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ is the golden ratio.

- 10:26 on 06 Mar 2026 - Sayan Dutta (reply depth 1):
  This method generalises to additive complement of $k$-th powers in the following way (assuming my calculations were correct).
  
  Define
  $$C_k(r):=\frac{k\,r^{1-\frac 1k}(r-1)^{1-\frac 1k}}{r^{1-\frac 1k}-1}$$
  for a given $k$, and a parameter $r$. Then, there exists an additive complement of $k$-th powers, $A_k$ for which
  $$\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{A_k(x)}{x^{1-\frac 1k}} \,\le\, \inf_{r>1} C_k(r)$$
  and it isn't hard to see that $C_k(r)$ minimises at $r=\rho_k^k$ where $\rho_k$ is the unique root of $x^{2k-1}-2x^k+1=0$ greater than $1$. For $k=3,4,5$, the RHS is approximately $7.93$, $9.02$ and $10.08$ respectively.
  
  Needless to say, $C_2(\phi^2)=2\phi^{5/2}$.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine whether bounded-shift square-based complements can cover the full tail
interval in the EP-33 setting, and at what shift budget scale.
Step A: Square-Shift Density Envelope
Quantify maximal coverage achievable with \(s\) shifts of square translates on
\([X/2,X]\) as \(X\to\infty\).
Step B: Obstruction Classification
Identify unavoidable uncovered residue/spacing classes for fixed \(s\), yielding
explicit lower bounds on uncovered density.
Step C: Budget Threshold
Determine growth rate of \(s=s(X)\) needed for full (or asymptotically full)
coverage.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform local-equidistribution failure theorem for square translates that
gives nontrivial uncovered mass at fixed shift budget, independent of \(X\).

Let \(C_s(X)\) be maximal covered count in \(I_X=[X/2,X]\) using at most \(s\)
allowed square-shifts from the EP-33 model.
Proposition D1 (Threshold implication):
If there exists \(s_0\) such that for all large \(X\),
\[
C_{s_0}(X)=|I_X|,
\]
then EP-33 has a positive answer with bounded shift budget \(s_0\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
The identity states exact full coverage of each large interval \(I_X\), which is
precisely the required eventual-coverage statement with fixed budget.
\(\square\)
Proposition D2 (Lower-density obstruction):
If for every fixed \(s\) there exists \(\eta_s>0\) and infinitely many \(X\) with
\[
C_s(X)\le (1-\eta_s)|I_X|,
\]
then no fixed \(s\) can solve EP-33.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
A fixed \(s\) giving eventual full coverage would force \(C_s(X)=|I_X|\) for all
sufficiently large \(X\), contradicting the infinitely many deficient \(X\).
\(\square\)
Thus the unresolved mathematical task is to prove either D1 (constructive) or D2
(obstruction) at theorem level.
