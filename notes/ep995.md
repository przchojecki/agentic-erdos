# EP-995

## Problem Statement
Let $n_1<n_2<\cdots$ be a lacunary sequence of integers and $f\in L^2([0,1])$. Estimate the growth of, for almost all $\alpha$,\[\sum_{1\leq k\leq N}f(\{ \alpha n_k\}).\]For example, is it true that, for almost all $\alpha$,\[\sum_{1\leq k\leq N}f(\{ \alpha n_k\})=o(N\sqrt{\log\log N})?\]

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er49d} constructed a lacunary sequence and $f\in L^2([0,1])$ such that, for every $\epsilon>0$, for almost all $\alpha$\[\limsup_{N\to \infty}\frac{1}{N(\log\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}}\sum_{1\leq k\leq N}f(\{\alpha n_k\})=\infty.\]Erd\H{o}s also proved that, for every lacunary sequence and $f\in L^2$, for every $\epsilon>0$, for almost all $\alpha$,\[\sum_{1\leq k\leq N}\sum_{1\leq k\leq N}f(\{\alpha n_k\})=o( N(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}).\]Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er64b} thought that his lower bound was closer to the truth.

- 02:33 on 21 Apr 2026 - ho boon suan:
  I have, with the assistance of GPT-5.4 Pro, obtained a rather intricate construction that gives negative answers to both [995] and [996]; see https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18535 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18535). The idea is simple enough, but quite a lot of bookkeeping is required to get things right.
  
  The central result is as follows: Given a decreasing function $\omega\colon[N_0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$, suppose $N\omega(N)^2$ is nondecreasing and that for every $\kappa>0$ we have $A^{1/2}\omega(\exp(\exp(\kappa A\log A)))\to\infty$ as $A\to\infty$. Then there exists a real-valued mean-zero function $f\in L^2(\mathbb T)$ and a lacunary integer sequence $(n_j)$ with $n_{j+1}/n_j\ge2$ such that $\|f-S_Nf\|_2\ll\omega(N)$ for all sufficiently large $N$, while $\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{j\le N}f(n_jx)=+\infty$ for almost every $x\in\mathbb T$.
  
  The proof constructs $f$ and $(n_j)=(2^{m_j})$ in stages. The function is built as $f=\sum_{k\ge1}F_k$, where a typical block has the form\[
  F(x)=\frac1{\sqrt{AL}}\sum_{q=1}^{L}\psi_d(2^{U+qD}x)
  \]with dyadic spikes\[
  \psi_d(x)=2^{d/2}\bigl(\mathbf 1_{[0,2^{-d-1})}(x)-\mathbf 1_{[1/2,1/2+2^{-d-1})}(x)\bigr).
  \]Each stage runs a number of trials. A trial is an arithmetic progression of exponents, and the relevant trial sum has the form\[
  \sum_{r=1}^\ell F(2^{M+rD}x).
  \]Some delicate bookkeeping is required to make the signal produced at one stage survive the contributions of all past and future stages. The construction is essentially a careful refinement of Erdős's construction in his 1949 paper On the Strong Law of Large Numbers (https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1949-0032971-4), replacing the Rademacher functions he uses with dyadic spike block trials. The new point is that the spike blocks interact with the repeated trials through a convolution that amplifies signal: a single rare central spike, once hit, is counted throughout an entire trial because the relevant indices enter as \(q+r\), so one hit creates a macroscopic average. The parameters are then tuned so that the same block is cheap in \(L^2\), has a controlled Fourier tail, and is shielded from past and future stages by separation in dyadic valuation layers.
  
  Currently my paper leaves the endpoint case \(\omega(N)=(\log\log N)^{-1/2}\) open, but I believe the same strategy should extend without too much difficulty to show that $\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{j\le N}f(n_jx)=+\infty$ in this case as well. ChatGPT says this appears doable if one replaces the two-sided dyadic spike with a one-sided one and weakens the requirement of "success for every stage" to "success for infinitely many stages." I am in the process of scrutinizing this approach, and if it works out, it should actually simplify the paper by a decent amount. I will probably upload a revised version of the manuscript to arXiv soon.

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
The exact almost-sure growth scale in full generality remains open.

Rigorous model theorem (doubling + step function):
In the model
\[
n_k=2^k,\qquad f(x)=\mathbf 1_{[0,1/2)}(x)-\mathbf 1_{[1/2,1)}(x),
\]
and \(\alpha\) uniformly random in \([0,1)\), define
\[
X_k(\alpha):=f(\{2^k\alpha\}).
\]
Then \((X_k)_{k\ge1}\) are i.i.d. Rademacher variables (\(\pm1\) with probability \(1/2\)).
Reason:
\(X_k\) depends only on the \(k\)-th binary digit of \(\alpha\), and binary digits are independent under Lebesgue measure.
Hence
\[
S_N(\alpha):=\sum_{k=1}^N X_k(\alpha)
\]
is a simple random walk.
Therefore, almost surely,
\[
S_N=O\!\left(\sqrt{N\log\log N}\right)
\]
(law of the iterated logarithm scale), and in particular
\[
\frac{S_N}{N\sqrt{\log\log N}}\to 0.
\]
So this benchmark model is provably consistent with the conjectured small growth regime and cannot produce a counterexample.

Target:
For lacunary \(n_k\) and \(f\in L^2\), determine almost-sure growth scale of
\[
S_N(\alpha)=\sum_{k\le N} f(\{\alpha n_k\}).
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Show a martingale-approximation theorem:
\[
S_N = M_N + R_N,\qquad M_N\ \text{martingale with }\langle M\rangle_N\asymp N,\ \ R_N=o(\sqrt{N\log\log N})\ \text{a.s.}
\]
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (model i.i.d. benchmark):
For \(n_k=2^k\) and dyadic step \(f\), \(S_N\) is simple random walk.
Proof.
Each term depends on one independent binary digit of \(\alpha\). \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (LIL consequence):
If \(S_N\) admits decomposition in A with \(R_N\) negligible at LIL scale, then
\[
S_N=O(\sqrt{N\log\log N})\ \text{a.s.}
\]
Proof.
Apply LIL to \(M_N\), absorb \(R_N\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies no linear-growth counterexample):
Under A,
\[
\frac{S_N}{N\sqrt{\log\log N}}\to0
\]
along full sequence a.s.
Proof.
From B2, numerator is \(O(\sqrt{N\log\log N})\), divide by \(N\sqrt{\log\log N}\). \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The hard part is constructing martingale approximations for general \(L^2\) observables and non-dyadic lacunary sequences with quantitative remainders.
