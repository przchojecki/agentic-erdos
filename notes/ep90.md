# EP-90

## Problem Statement
Does every set of $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ contain at most $n^{1+O(1/\log\log n)}$ many pairs which are distance 1 apart?

## Site Remarks
The unit distance problem. In \cite{Er94b} Erd\H{o}s dates this conjecture to 1946. In \cite{Er82e} he offers \$300 for the upper bound $n^{1+o(1)}$.

This would be the best possible, as is shown by a set of lattice points. It is easy to show that there are $O(n^{3/2})$ many such pairs. The best known upper bound is $O(n^{4/3})$, due to Spencer, Szemer\'{e}di, and Trotter \cite{SST84}. In \cite{Er83c} and \cite{Er85} Erd\H{o}s offers \$250 for an upper bound of the form $n^{1+o(1)}$.

Part of the difficulty of this problem is explained by a result of Valtr (see \cite{Sz16}), who constructed a metric on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and a set of $n$ points with $\gg n^{4/3}$ unit distance pairs (with respect to this metric). The methods of the upper bound proof of Spencer, Szemer\'{e}di, and Trotter \cite{SST84} generalise to include this metric. Therefore to prove an upper bound better than $n^{4/3}$ some special feature of the Euclidean metric must be exploited.

See a survey by Szemer\'{e}di \cite{Sz16} for further background and related results.

See also [92], [96], [605], and [956]. The higher dimensional generalisation is [1085].

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Improve upper bounds for the maximum number \(u(n)\) of unit distances among
\(n\) planar points, ideally toward \(n^{1+o(1)}\) or better.
Step A: Incidence partition
Partition point pairs by local directional/rich-line structure and treat each
part by tailored incidence inequalities.
Step B: Polynomial partition + additive structure
Use partitioning to isolate cells, then show heavy cell interactions force
additive structure that limits unit-distance multiplicity.
Step C: Global recombination
Sum cell and boundary contributions with balanced parameters to beat current
exponents.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a robust anti-concentration theorem for unit vectors between large point
subsets after partitioning; current losses dominate.

Proposition S1 (Incidence graph formulation):
Let \(P\) be an \(n\)-point set. Form bipartite graph \(G\subseteq P\times P\)
with \((p,q)\in E(G)\) iff \(|p-q|=1\), \(p\neq q\). Then
\[
u(n)=\frac12|E(G)|.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Each unordered unit-distance pair corresponds to exactly two oriented edges.
\(\square\)
Proposition S2 (Reduction via edge decomposition):
If \(E(G)=E_1\sqcup E_2\sqcup E_3\) and each class satisfies
\[
|E_i|\le B_i(n),
\]
then
\[
u(n)\le \frac12(B_1(n)+B_2(n)+B_3(n)).
\]
Hence any nontrivial improvement in the dominant \(B_i\) improves \(u(n)\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate from S1 and disjointness of classes.
\(\square\)
