# EP-18

## Problem Statement
We call $m$ practical if every integer $1\leq n<m$ is the sum of distinct divisors of $m$. If $m$ is practical then let $h(m)$ be such that $h(m)$ many divisors always suffice.

Are there infinitely many practical $m$ such that\[h(m) < (\log\log m)^{O(1)}?\]Is it true that $h(n!)<n^{o(1)}$? Or perhaps even $h(n!)<(\log n)^{O(1)}$?

## Site Remarks
It is easy to see that almost all numbers are not practical. Erd\H{o}s originally showed that $h(n!) <n$. Vose \cite{Vo85} proved the existence of infinitely many practical $m$ such that $h(m)\ll (\log m)^{1/2}$.

The sequence of practical numbers is A005153 (https://oeis.org/A005153) in the OEIS.

The reward of \$250 is offered in \cite{Er81h} for a proof or disproof of whether\[h(n) <(\log \log n)^{O(1)}\]for infinitely many practical $n$.

See also [304] and [825].

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Small-range behavior does not prove infinitely many polylog-scale h(m) examples or asymptotics for h(n!).

Target:
Address infinitude of practical \(m\) with small \(h(m)\), and sharpen behavior
of \(h(n!)\).
Step A: Structural Formula for \(h(m)\)
Express \(h(m)\) through divisor-set additive basis parameters using practical
number decomposition.
Step B: Controlled Construction Family
Build infinite families of practical numbers where divisor blocks have bounded
additive covering complexity, yielding explicit upper bounds for \(h(m)\).
Step C: Factorial Specialization
Apply Step A+B to \(m=n!\), using prime exponent structure to derive polylog or
near-polylog upper bounds.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a practical-divisor additive basis lemma: for practical \(m\), the divisor
set can be partitioned into \(O(h(m))\) structured blocks with provably efficient
subset-sum coverage bounds.
