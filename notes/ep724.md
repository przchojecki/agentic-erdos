# EP-724

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n)$ be the maximum number of mutually orthogonal Latin squares (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutually_orthogonal_Latin_squares) of order $n$. Is it true that\[f(n) \gg n^{1/2}?\]

## Site Remarks
Euler conjectured that $f(n)=1$ when $n\equiv 2\pmod{4}$, but this was disproved by Bose, Parker, and Shrikhande \cite{BPS60} who proved $f(n)\geq 2$ for $n\geq 7$.

Chowla, Erd\H{o}s, and Straus \cite{CES60} proved $f(n) \gg n^{1/91}$. Wilson \cite{Wi74} proved $f(n) \gg n^{1/17}$. Beth \cite{Be83c} proved $f(n) \gg n^{1/14.8}$.

The sequence of $f(n)$ is A001438 (https://oeis.org/A001438) in the OEIS.

## Local Computation
- Computed explicit guaranteed lower bounds via a MacNeish-type product bound:
\[
f(n) \ge \min_{p^a\parallel n}(p^a-1).
\]
- Generated full profile for all \(2\le n\le 500\).

- Dense scan confirms strong variability by arithmetic structure:
 - prime-power \(n=q\): bound is \(q-1\), much larger than \(\sqrt n\),
 - highly composite \(n\) with a small prime-power factor can force much smaller guaranteed bounds.
- So current guaranteed behavior is highly non-uniform; the universal \(n^{1/2}\) lower target remains far beyond this baseline method.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite route here is to compute the explicit MacNeish-type lower bound from the prime-power factorization of \(n\) and then inspect how that guarantee behaves across many values of \(n\). This shows that arithmetic structure creates strong fluctuations, with prime powers looking much better than highly composite numbers, but it also shows that the baseline product method is far too weak to reach a universal \(n^{1/2}\) lower bound.
