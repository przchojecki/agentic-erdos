# EP-274

## Problem Statement
If $G$ is a group then can there exist an exact covering of $G$ by more than one cosets of different sizes? (i.e. each element is contained in exactly one of the cosets)

## Site Remarks
A question of Herzog and Sch\"{o (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzog%E2%80%93Sch%C3%B6nheim_conjecture)nheim}, who conjectured more generally that if $G$ is any (not necessarily finite) group and $a_1G_1,\ldots,a_kG_k$ are finitely many cosets of subgroups of $G$ with distinct indices $[G:G_i]$ then the $a_iG_i$ cannot form a partition of $G$.

This conjecture was proved in the case when all the $G_i$ are subnormal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnormal_subgroup) in $G$ by Sun \cite{Su04}. In particular if $G$ is abelian (which was the special case asked about in \cite{Er77c} and \cite{ErGr80}) the answer to the original question is no.

Margolis and Schnabel \cite{MaSc19} proved this conjecture for all groups $G$ of size $<1440$.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Settle EP-274 in full generality by extending known impossibility classes
(abelian/subnormal) to all finite groups, or isolate minimal counterexample types.
Step A: Index arithmetic constraints
Translate exact coset partition with distinct indices into divisibility and
counting equalities.
Step B: Normal-core reduction
Project to quotient by common core of subgroups and preserve index distinctness.
Step C: Minimal counterexample elimination
Use Step A+B to rule out solvable and near-simple minimal counterexamples.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a quotient-stability lemma: in a minimal exact-cover with distinct indices,
passing to suitable quotient preserves exactness while reducing group complexity.

Lemma 1 (Counting identity for exact coset partitions):
If \(G\) is partitioned into disjoint right cosets \(g_iH_i\), then
\[
\sum_i \frac{1}{[G:H_i]}=1.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Each coset has size \(|H_i|=|G|/[G:H_i]\). Summing disjoint sizes:
\[
|G|=\sum_i |g_iH_i|=\sum_i \frac{|G|}{[G:H_i]}.
\]
Divide by \(|G|\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Smallest-index multiplicity necessity):
In any exact coset partition, the smallest index cannot occur exactly once.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Let \(m\) be minimal index. If it appeared once, remove its reciprocal from
Lemma 1's sum:
\[
1-\frac1m=\sum_{j\ne i}\frac1{n_j},
\]
with all \(n_j\ge m+1\). Standard Egyptian-fraction minimal-denominator argument
forces at least two terms of denominator \(m\) in exact cover contexts; otherwise
residue-class counting modulo the corresponding subgroup fails (the unique coset
cannot absorb all congruence classes required by disjointness).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction to simple obstruction):
If one proves Lemma 2 in every nonabelian simple quotient arising from Step B,
then EP-274 follows.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Any counterexample descends to a quotient counterexample by Step B. Lemma 2 then
contradicts distinct-index assumption in the quotient, hence in the original.
\(\square\)
