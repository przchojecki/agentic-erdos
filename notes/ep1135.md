# EP-1135

## Problem Statement
Define $f:\mathbb{N}\to \mathbb{N}$ by $f(n)=n/2$ if $n$ is even and $f(n)=\frac{3n+1}{2}$ if $n$ is odd.

Given any integer $m\geq 1$ does there exist $k\geq 1$ such that $f^{(k)}(m)=1$?

## Site Remarks
The infamous Collatz conjecture (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collatz_conjecture). For a detailed discussion of the history and theory surrounding this problem we refer to the overview by Lagarias \cite{La10}.

This is not a problem due to Erd\H{o}s; it was first devised by Collatz before 1952. Erd\H{o}s referred to this problem on several occasions as 'hopeless'. As Lagarias \cite{La16} notes, the closest Erd\H{o}s ever came to working on problems of this nature is the theorem described in the remarks to [1134].

It is often claimed that Erd\H{o}s offered \$500 for a solution to this problem; this claim originated in a survey article by Lagarias \cite{La85}.

Lagarias reported, in personal communication, that this came from a conversation he had with Erd\H{o}s and Graham around 1983, in which Graham asked Erd\H{o}s to make an estimate of what value Erd\H{o}s would put the problem on his prize scale, to which Erd\H{o}s replied \$500. Therefore, strictly speaking, Erd\H{o}s never offered \$500 specifically as a prize, but we include this prize value here for comparing those problems which Erd\H{o}s rated as 'prize problems'.

This is Problem E16 in Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}, in which Guy quotes Erd\H{o}s as saying "Mathematics may not be ready for such problems".

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Prove global convergence for the accelerated Collatz map (EP-1135 formulation).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Show there is no divergent orbit and no nontrivial cycle by proving a uniform descent-on-average inequality on every sufficiently long block of iterates.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (minimal counterexample reduction):
If a nontrivial cycle or divergent orbit exists, there exists one with minimal odd part (or minimal height in an appropriate ordering).
Proof.
Take infimum in well-ordered positive integers among odd representatives encountered in the bad set; choose witness attaining minimal value. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (parity-vector encoding):
Any cycle of length \(m\) determines a parity vector and exact rational identity
\[
n = \frac{A}{2^m-3^s}
\]
with integers \(A,s\) derived from the parity pattern.
Proof.
Unroll the affine iterates of \(T(n)=(3n+1)/2^{\nu_2(3n+1)}\); after one period solve linear equation in starting value. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A rules out bad objects):
If Lemma A gives strict average descent over every long block, then cycles with average multiplier \(\ge1\) and divergent trajectories are impossible.
Proof.
Average descent implies logarithmic iterate decreases eventually, contradicting periodic return or divergence to infinity. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is proving a deterministic, all-orbit average-descent estimate strong enough to dominate exceptional parity patterns.
