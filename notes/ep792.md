# EP-792

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n)$ be maximal such that in any $A\subset \mathbb{Z}$ with $\lvert A\rvert=n$ there exists some sum-free subset $B\subseteq A$ with $\lvert B\rvert \geq f(n)$, so that there are no solutions to\[a+b=c\]with $a,b,c\in B$. Estimate $f(n)$.

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er65} gave a simple proof that shows $f(n) \geq n/3$. Alon and Kleitman \cite{AlKl90} improved this to $f(n)\geq \frac{n+1}{3}$, and Bourgain \cite{Bo97} further improved this to $\frac{n+2}{3}$. The best lower bound known is\[f(n)\geq \frac{n}{3}+c\log\log n\]for some constant $c>0$, due to Bedert \cite{Be25b}. The best upper bound known is\[f(n) \leq \frac{n}{3}+o(n),\]due to Eberhard, Green, and Manners \cite{EGM14}.

This problem is Problem 1 on Green's open problems list (https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf).

## Local Computation
- Exact small-\(n\) search on sampled families (including interval adversaries).
- Heuristic larger-\(n\) extension with greedy sum-free construction.

- In tested finite samples, worst-case values stay near \(n/2\):
 - exact rows \(n=16,20,24\): worst is 8,10,12.
 - heuristic rows \(n=28,32,40\): worst is 14,16,20.
- Small/medium finite instances are far from the asymptotic extremal regime driving the \(n/3\)-scale question.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite search here combines exact worst-case checks on small families with heuristic constructions at larger sizes. In the tested range the guaranteed sum-free subset size stays much closer to \(n/2\) than to the asymptotic \(n/3\)-scale frontier, which shows these instances are not yet extremal. The asymptotic behavior of \(f(n)\) remains open.
