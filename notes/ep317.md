# EP-317

## Problem Statement
Is there some constant $c>0$ such that for every $n\geq 1$ there exists some $\delta_k\in \{-1,0,1\}$ for $1\leq k\leq n$ with\[0< \left\lvert \sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\frac{\delta_k}{k}\right\rvert < \frac{c}{2^n}?\]Is it true that for sufficiently large $n$, for any $\delta_k\in \{-1,0,1\}$,\[\left\lvert \sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\frac{\delta_k}{k}\right\rvert > \frac{1}{[1,\ldots,n]}\]whenever the left-hand side is not zero?

## Site Remarks
Inequality is obvious for the second claim, the problem is strict inequality. This fails for small $n$, for example\[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4}=-\frac{1}{12}.\]Arguments of Kovac and van Doorn in the comment section prove a weak version of the first question, with an upper bound of\[2^{-n\frac{(\log\log\log n)^{1+o(1)}}{\log n}},\]and van Doorn gives a heuristic that suggests this may be the true order of magnitude.

  Define $Q_n$ to be the set of rationals that can be written as $\sum_{a \in A} \frac{1}{a}$ for some subset $A \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$. The first question asks whether the inequality $$\min_{\substack{q, q' \in Q_n \\ q \neq q'}} |q - q'| < \frac{c}{2^n}\hspace{20pt}(1)$$holds for some absolute constant $c$.
  
  By the results in #320 (https://www.erdosproblems.com/320) we have $|Q_n| = \exp[\frac{n f(n)}{\log n}]$ for some $f(n)$ that goes to infinity with $n$ at a rate of $(\log \log \log n)^{1 + o(1)}$. As we have $q \in [0, \log n + 1]$ for all $q \in Q_n$, by the pigeonhole principle we obtain $$\min_{\substack{q, q' \in Q_n \\ q \neq q'}} |q - q'| \le \frac{\log n + 1}{\exp[\frac{n f(n)}{\log n}]} = \frac{1}{2^{\frac{n (\log \log \log n)^{1 + o(1)}}{\log n}}},\hspace{20pt}(2)$$a slight improvement over the bound from Vjeko's comment. Perhaps known concentration inequalities on random harmonic sums can improve upon $(2)$ further.
  
  On the other hand, if $Q_n$ would simply be a set of $|Q_n|$ uniformly random numbers in the interval $[0, \log n + 1]$, then one would expect their minimum difference to be on the order of $\frac{1}{|Q_n|^{2 + o(1)}}$. This would in fact give a lower bound of the same shape as the upper bound in inequality $(2)$, perhaps casting some doubt on the validity of $(1)$.

## Local Computation
Exact meet-in-the-middle data for \(3\le n\le 26\) shows:
- equality \(|\sum|=1/[1,\dots,n]\) occurs at \(n=3,4\);
- for every \(5\le n\le 26\), the minimum nonzero value is strictly larger than \(1/[1,\dots,n]\).
Deeper exact runs extended this finite profile to \(n=30\) and \(n=32\), and a further deep rerun again confirmed strict inequality from \(n=5\) onward in the tested range.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background records a weak positive form of (1) with upper bound
2^{-n * (logloglog n)^{1+o(1)} / log n}.
For (2), the non-strict lcm lower bound is immediate, and strict inequality is
known to fail for small n.

Sharp order for (1) and eventual strict inequality behavior for (2) remain open
in the record.

Target:
Determine sharp asymptotics of the minimum nonzero signed harmonic sum magnitude,
and eventual strictness versus \(1/[1..n]\).
Step A: Exact integer-lattice reduction
Scale by \(L_n=[1,\dots,n]\) to an integer subset-sum minimum problem.
Step B: Lower-bound amplification
Prove anti-concentration for signed sums near \(0\) in that lattice.
Step C: Strictness and asymptotic law
Show equality with \(1/L_n\) is finite-exceptional, and derive decay order.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need an arithmetic anti-concentration theorem for coefficients \(L_n/k\) near
zero that beats trivial gcd-based bounds.

Lemma 1 (Scaled minimum identity):
Let
\[
\mu_n:=\min\Bigl\{\Bigl|\sum_{k=1}^n \varepsilon_k\frac1k\Bigr|:\varepsilon_k\in\{-1,0,1\},\ \text{not all }0\Bigr\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mu_n=\frac{1}{L_n}\min\Bigl\{\Bigl|\sum_{k=1}^n \varepsilon_k\frac{L_n}{k}\Bigr|:\varepsilon_k\in\{-1,0,1\},\ \text{not all }0\Bigr\}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Multiply each candidate sum by \(L_n\), an exact bijective scaling of values.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Baseline lower bound):
\[
\mu_n\ge \frac1{L_n}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By Lemma 1 the scaled minimum is a nonzero integer in absolute value, hence at
least \(1\), then divide by \(L_n\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Strictness criterion):
Equality \(\mu_n=1/L_n\) holds iff the scaled signed-sum set contains \(\pm1\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
From Lemma 1 and Lemma 2, minimum equals \(1/L_n\) exactly when integer minimum is
\(1\).
\(\square\)
