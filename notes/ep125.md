# EP-125

## Problem Statement
Let $A = \{ \sum\epsilon_k3^k : \epsilon_k\in \{0,1\}\}$ be the set of integers which have only the digits $0,1$ when written base $3$, and $B=\{ \sum\epsilon_k4^k : \epsilon_k\in \{0,1\}\}$ be the set of integers which have only the digits $0,1$ when written base $4$.

Does $A+B$ have positive lower density?

## Site Remarks
A problem of Burr, Erd\H{o}s, Graham, and Li \cite{BEGL96}. In \cite{BEGL96} they ask whether this set has 'positive density? positive upper density?'. The formulation in \cite{Er97} is equivalent to asking about positive lower density only. It seems likely, and in keeping with the spirit of other similar questions and references, that Erd\H{o}s intended to ask about positive lower density: in other words, does there exist a constant $c>0$ such that $\lvert (A+B)\cap[1,x]\rvert\geq cx$ for all sufficiently large $x$?

More generally, if $n_1<\cdots<n_k$ have\[\sum_{i=1}^k\log_{n_k}(2)>1\]and $A_i$ is the set of integers with only the digits $0,1$ in base $n_i$ then does $A_1+\cdots+A_k$ have positive density? Melfi \cite{Me01} noted this is false as written, with a counterexample given by $\{3,9,81\}$, but suggests it is true if we further insist that the $n_k$ are pairwise coprime.

If $C=A+B$ then Melfi \cite{Me01} showed $\lvert C\cap[1,x]\rvert \gg x^{0.965}$ and Hasler and Melfi \cite{HaMe24} improved this to $\lvert C\cap [1,x]\rvert \gg x^{0.9777}$. Hasler and Melfi also show that the lower density of $C$ is at most\[\frac{1015}{1458}\approx 0.69616.\]An argument found by DeepMind (see the comment section) shows that the upper density is at least $6/5$ times the lower density; in particular $A+B$ cannot have positive density. DeepMind later improved this argument (https://github.com/mo271/formal-conjectures/blob/c27415379b5dbe34105d1fdd707994540c4c6fc7/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/125.lean#L468) to prove that the lower density has to be $0$: for any $\epsilon>0$ there are infinitely many $x$ such that $\lvert (A+B)\cap [1,x]\rvert <\epsilon x$.

See also [124].

- 17:59 on 25 Feb 2026 - TerenceTao (reply depth 3):
  Here is my understanding of the Lean argument, simplified somewhat using the Kronecker approximation theorem:
  
  The set $A$ contains large gaps of the form $[3^k/2, 3^k)$; and $B$ similarly contains large gaps of the form $[4^m/3, 4^m)$.
  
  Because of this, $A+B$ contains gaps of the form $[4^m/3+3^k/2, \min(3^k,4^m))$.
  
  By the irrationality of $\log 4 / \log 3$ and the Kronecker approximation theorem, there will be infinitely many such gaps of length comparable to their scale; in fact one gets intervals of the form $[(\frac{5}{6}+o(1)) N, N)$ for arbitrarily large $N$. This forces the upper density to be at least $\frac{6}{5}$ times the lower density, which is incompatible with having a positive density. (It also gives an upper bound of $\frac{5}{6} = 0.833\dots$ for the lower density, which is weaker than the current record of $0.696\dots$ of Hasler and Melfi.)
  
  So this does resolve the first formulation of the Erdos problem, but not the second.
  
  EDIT: to put it another way, a priori there are four possibilities regarding the set $A+B$:
  
  1. $A+B$ has zero upper and lower density (and hence also zero density).
  2. $A+B$ has zero lower density, but positive upper density (and hence no density)
  3. $A+B$ has positive upper and lower density that are equal (and hence positive density).
  4. $A+B$ has positive upper and lower density that are unequal (and hence no density).
  
  This argument eliminates Scenario 3, but makes no progress on determining which of the remaining Scenarios 1, 2, 4 is the truth.

- 14:50 on 30 Mar 2026 - GTsoukalas:
  The DeepMind prover agent has now autonomously identified a proof in Lean that the lower density of A + B is zero, which rules out Scenario 4 as discussed here, and, if we understand correctly, fully solves the problem under Erdos’s intended interpretation. It uses a similar construction in the proof that eliminated Scenario 3 (A + B does not have positive density), as classified in this discussion here. We provide the proof at this link (https://github.com/mo271/formal-conjectures/blob/c27415379b5dbe34105d1fdd707994540c4c6fc7/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/125.lean#L468). Here is a summary of the new argument:
  
  Our previous proof showed that since there are numbers of the form $3^k \approx 4^m \approx M$ where there is a big gap, $A+B$ cannot have a positive limit density. What this proof does is then look at the density of $A+B$ up to $M \cdot N_0$ for some number $N_0$. It aims to show that the density of $A+B$ up to $M \cdot N_0$ is at most 99% of the density of the set up to $N_0$. By repeating this step many times, it finds a sequence of scales where the density drops exponentially, implying that the lower density has to be zero. This 99% bound comes from a counting argument: any number below $M \cdot N_0$ can be split into its "top" digits and "bottom" digits, so one can map the elements of $A+B$ below $M \cdot N_0$ into a grid of $(y,c)$ coordinates. The number of choices for $y$ is given by the number of elements in $A+B$ below $N_0$, and the number of choices for $c$ is bounded by $5M/6$ because of the gap argument from our first proof.
  
  Note that in the Lean proof (and in the agent’s work) there are references to results due to Pach and Pintz. We weren’t able to identify the literature the agent referenced, so we are unsure whether it exists or is a “hallucination”.

- 15:47 on 30 Mar 2026 - Thomas Bloom (reply depth 1):
  Very nice, congratulations! I was a little confused at first by your summary, so for those curious here is an alternative explanation. We want to go from density $<\delta$ at scale $N$ to density $<c\delta$ at some bigger scale $MN$, where $c<1$ is some fixed constant. Suppose we actually had $3^k=4^m=M$. If $n=a+b\leq MN$ then let $a_0$ be the $k$ 'bottom' bits of $a$ and $a_1$ the top bits, so $Ma_1=a-a_0$, and similarly for $b$, so that\[n=a+b\approx M(a_1+b_1)+a_0+b_0.\](The key point is that e.g. both $a_0$ and $a_1$ are also in $A$, since truncating to either the top or bottom digits preserves the property of being in $A$). Now there are $<\delta N$ choices for $a_1+b_1$ by assumption. But also\[a_0\leq 1+\cdots+3^{k-1}=\frac{3^k-1}{2}\approx M/2\]and similarly $b_0\leq M/3$, so $a_0+b_0$ is an integer at most $\frac{5}{6}M$, and so the number of choices is $\delta N\frac{5}{6}M$ as required.
  
  (And the rest of the argument is some Diophantine technical fiddling about to get around the fact that we can't literally have $3^k=4^m$).
  
  EDIT: This is quite similar to the arguments for [124] in the comment section that discuss whether all large integers can be written as the distinct sum of integers with only $0,1$ digits in base $d_i$, and in particular Tao's reconstruction of Pomerance's argument that $\sum \frac{1}{d_i-1}<1$ is a necessary condition. I believe this argument can be generalised to prove the stronger fact that if $\sum \frac{1}{d_i-1}<1$ then not only is not the case that all large integers are expressible in this way, but the lower density of such sums is actually $0$.

  Here's my version of the argument as I understand it. Credit to GPT-5.4 Thinking for the explanation (https://chatgpt.com/share/69caa68a-d52c-839d-82ba-0b4962e09633).
  
  Let $D(N) = |(A+B)\cap [0,N)|/N$. We need to make $D(N)$ arbitrarily small.
  
  Let $3^k$ and $4^m$ be close to each other. By Dirichlet’s approximation theorem, given $\varepsilon > 0$, with $L = \min(3^k, 4^m)$ we can make $3^k , 4^m < (1+\varepsilon)L$.
  
  Fix $N$. Consider $D(LN)$. For $x < LN$, take $x = a+b$, $a \in A$, $b \in B$. Then
  $$ x = Ly + c, $$
  where
  $$ \begin{align*}
  y &= \lfloor a/3^k \rfloor + \lfloor b/4^m \rfloor, \\
  c &= |3^k - 4^m| (\lfloor a/3^k \rfloor \text{ or } \lfloor b/4^m \rfloor) + a \bmod 3^k + b \bmod 4^m.
  \end{align*} $$
  Note $y < N$ and $y \in A+B$. Note $a \bmod 3^k \le 3^k/2$ and $b \bmod 4^m \le 4^k/3$ due to $a \in A$ and $b \in B$. And
  $$ c \le (\varepsilon L)y + 3^k/2 + 4^m/3 \le ((5/6) (1+\varepsilon) +\varepsilon N)L. $$
  
  Let $\delta > 0$. For fixed $N$, we can choose $\varepsilon > 0$ and hence $L := L(N)$, so that $c \le (5/6+\delta)L$. The injection $x \mapsto (y, c)$ then shows $D(LN) \le (5/6+\delta) D(N)$. By iterating, we can make $D(N)$ arbitrarily small.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine whether the sumset \(A+B\) (with digit-restricted base sets in the
problem) has positive asymptotic density, and if possible compute the density.
Step A: Automaton/expansion encoding
Model admissible expansions and carry propagation by a finite-state transducer.
Step B: Ergodic frequency analysis
Analyze long-prefix frequencies of accepted expansions to derive lower/upper
density bounds.
Step C: Gap-distribution control
Show maximal forbidden gap growth is sublinear enough to imply positive lower
density (or construct sparse counter-patterns).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a carry-sensitive mixing lemma for the transducer so local digit freedoms
produce global density uniformly.

Proposition AM1 (Density from block frequencies):
If for some block length \(L\), every residue class modulo \(q^L\) appears in
\(A+B\) with frequency at least \(\eta>0\) in long intervals, then
\[
\underline d(A+B)\ge \eta.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Partition long intervals into \(q^L\)-blocks and average class counts.
\(\square\)
Proposition AM2 (Reduction to transducer mixing):
If the finite-state expansion transducer for \(A+B\) is strongly connected and
aperiodic with stationary mass bounded below on accepting states, then the
hypothesis of AM1 holds for some \(\eta>0\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Markov-chain mixing gives uniform asymptotic visitation frequencies of accepting
states, which translate to accepted residue densities.
\(\square\)
