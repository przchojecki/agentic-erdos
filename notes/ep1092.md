# EP-1092

## Problem Statement
Let $f_r(n)$ be maximal such that, if a graph $G$ has the property that every subgraph $H$ on $m$ vertices is the union of a graph with chromatic number $r$ and a graph with $\leq f_r(m)$ edges, then $G$ has chromatic number $\leq r+1$.

Is it true that $f_2(n) \gg n$? More generally, is $f_r(n)\gg_r n$?

- 18:38 on 03 Nov 2025 - Quanyu Tang:
  It seems that the result of Rödl [Rö82, Theorem 1.5] implies that $f_{2}(n)\not\gg n$, which therefore gives a negative answer to the first problem.
  
  Specifically, Rödl gave an explicit construction $K^{*}(n,k)$ and proved in [Rö82, Theorem 1.5] that for any $\varepsilon>0$ and any positive integer $k$, when $n$ is sufficiently large,\[
  \chi\!\left(K^{*}(n,k)\right)=k+2,
  \quad\text{and every subgraph }H\text{ becomes bipartite after deleting }
  \le \varepsilon|V(H)|\text{ edges.}
  \]Now we argue by contradiction. Assume that there exists a constant $c>0$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$, $f_{2}(n)\ge c n$. Let $\varepsilon=c/2$ and $k \geq 2$. By [Rö82, Theorem 1.5], there exists a graph $G=K^{*}(n,k)$ (for large $n$) satisfying that every subgraph $H$ can be made bipartite by deleting at most $\varepsilon|V(H)|$ edges, while $\chi(G)=k+2\ge4$. This means that $G$ satisfies the assumption of the definition of $f_{2}$, since every subgraph $H$ is the union of a bipartite graph and a graph with at most $\varepsilon|V(H)|$ edges, and $f_{2}(m)\ge c m\ge\varepsilon m$. However, the conclusion $\chi(G)\le3$ fails (because $\chi(G)=k+2$). This contradiction shows that no such linear lower bound $c$ can exist.
  
  [Rö82] Rödl, Vojtěch. "Nearly bipartite graphs with large chromatic number." Combinatorica 2.4 (1982): 377-383.

## Local Computation
- deep finite proxy computation based on
\[
b(H)=|E(H)|-\operatorname{MaxCut}(H),
\]
the exact minimum number of edge deletions needed to make $H$ bipartite.
- Evaluated induced subgraphs sampled from high-chromatic Mycielski graphs:
 - Mycielski($C_5$), $n=11$, $\chi\ge 4$,
 - second Mycielski lift, $n=23$, $\chi\ge 5$.
- For each $m$, sampled 1200 induced subgraphs and computed exact max-cut (thus exact $b(H)$) per sample.

Deep run (DEPTH=4) over
\[
m\in\{6,7,8,9,10,11,12\}
\]
showed finite sampled worst-case values up to:
- on $n=11$ graph: best found $b(H)=4$ (at $m=11$),
- on $n=23$ graph: best found $b(H)=4$ (at $m=11,12$).
Corresponding sampled ratios $b(H)/m$ are around $0.14$ to $0.36$ in this range.
These are finite sampled envelopes only; they do not determine asymptotics for $f_2(m)$.

## Local Proof Attempts
Closed Substatement:
For \(r=2\), the claim \(f_2(n)\gg n\) is false by Rödl's construction (as
recorded in background).
The general \(r\)-parameter behavior of \(f_r(n)\) is unresolved here.
- Need a sharp local-to-global decomposition threshold for "\(r\)-partite plus
 few edges" that separates bounded and linear error regimes as \(r\) grows.

Target:
Understand asymptotic growth of \(f_r(n)\) for fixed \(r\ge3\): linear,
sublinear, or bounded-scale regimes.
Step A: Critical Witness Construction
Produce high-\(\chi\) graphs with explicit lower bounds on required edge edits to
\(r\)-partite structure in all \(m\)-vertex induced subgraphs.
Step B: Local-to-Global Transfer
Show any graph with uniformly small local edit budget must satisfy global
\((r+1)\)-colorability bound.
Step C: Threshold Identification
Match Step A lower examples with Step B upper theorem to pin the order of
\(f_r(n)\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need an induced-subgraph stability theorem: if every \(m\)-vertex induced
subgraph is within \(g(m)\) edges of \(r\)-partite, then global chromatic number
is \(\le r+1\) whenever \(g(m)\) is below the true threshold order.
