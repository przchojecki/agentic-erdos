# EP-82

## Problem Statement
Let $F(n)$ be maximal such that every graph on $n$ vertices contains a regular induced subgraph on at least $F(n)$ vertices. Prove that $F(n)/\log n\to \infty$.

## Site Remarks
Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s, Fajtlowicz, and Stanton. It is known that $F(5)=3$ and $F(7)=4$.

Ramsey's theorem implies that $F(n)\gg \log n$. Bollob\'{a}s observed that $F(n)\ll n^{1/2+o(1)}$. Alon, Krivelevich, and Sudakov \cite{AKS07} have improved this to $n^{1/2}(\log n)^{O(1)}$. Dyson and McKay \cite{DyMc26} have improved this further to $F(n) \ll n^{1/2}$.

In \cite{Er93} Erd\H{o}s asks whether, if $t(n)$ is the largest trivial (either empty or complete) subgraph which a graph on $n$ vertices must contain (so that $t(n) \gg \log n$ by Ramsey's theorem), then is it true that\[F(n)-t(n)\to \infty?\]Equivalently, and in analogue with the definition of Ramsey numbers, one can define $G(n)$ to be the minimal $m$ such that every graph on $m$ vertices contains a regular induced subgraph on at least $n$ vertices. This problem can be rephrased as asking whether $G(n) \leq 2^{o(n)}$.

Fajtlowicz, McColgan, Reid, and Staton \cite{FMRS95} showed that $G(1)=1$, $G(2)=2$, $G(3)=5$, $G(4)=7$, and $G(5)\geq 12$. Boris Alexeev and Brendan McKay (see the comments and this site (https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/ramsey.html)) have computed $G(5)=17$, $G(6)\geq 21$, and $G(7)\geq 29$. Dyson and McKay \cite{DyMc26} have proved $G(7)\geq 30$, and $G(k)\geq \frac{9}{163}k^2$ for all large $k$.

See also [1031] for another question regarding induced regular subgraphs.

  Define $G(k)=\min\{ n : F(n)\ge k \}$, that is $G(k)$ is the smallest order on which every graph has a regular induced subgraph of order $k$ or greater. This is the same way Ramsey numbers are defined, and I find it easier on the brain. $G$ determines $F$ due to monotonicity.
  
  In 1997, I computed G(2)=2, G(3)=5, G(4)=7, G(5)=17 but didn't publish it. I just repeated that computation now with fresh code and got the same result. The lexicographic product $P_4[P_4]$ is a short proof that $G(5)\ge 17$ but I don't know of a non-computational proof that $G(5)=17$.

- 14:07 on 19 Sep 2025 - zach hunter:
  here are some amusing weaker problems where I still do not a know how to get a $\omega(\log(n))$ lower bound.
  
  1) let $\alpha(n)$ be the largest $k$ so that every $n$-vertex graph $G$ contains an induced subgraph $G'$ on at least $k$ vertices, so that all the degrees of $G'$ are equal mod 3 (the analogous problem mod 2 has a linear lower bound via a very slick old argument of Gallai, see also the semi-recent work of Ferber and Krivelevich (link (https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.05495)) which shows that if $G$ has minimum degree then you can in fact find an induced subgraph of linear size with all degrees odd);
  2) let $\beta(n)$ be the largest $k$ so that every $n$-vertex graph $G$ contains an induced subgraph $G'$, and a set $S\subset V(G')$ of size $k$, all having the same degree in $G'$.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - Random lower-envelope search found worst max regular induced sizes $4,5,6,6$ for $n=10,12,14,16$.

 - Small-$n$ data is consistent with $F(n)$ above pure logarithmic constants, but far from asymptotic proof of $F(n)/\log n\to\infty$.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
For every \(n\)-vertex graph, force an induced regular subgraph of order
\(F(n)\) with \(F(n)/\log n\to\infty\).
Step A: Degree-energy discretization
Partition vertices into degree bands and show one band carries enough induced
edge energy to support many near-regular candidates.
Step B: Local lemma cleanup
Apply random thinning plus Lovasz Local Lemma to eliminate degree outliers inside
a selected candidate set.
Step C: Exact regularization
Patch residual parity/degree defects via short alternating-path switches to get a
fully regular induced subgraph.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a high-probability discrepancy lemma for induced degrees after thinning that
is strong enough at \(|S| \asymp \log n \cdot \omega(1)\).

Proposition N1 (Near-regular implies regular after bounded edits):
Let \(H\) be a graph on \(m\) vertices with all degrees in
\([d-\Delta,d+\Delta]\). If \(\Delta=o(m)\) and suitable parity conditions hold,
then by changing \(o(m^2)\) adjacency incidences inside \(H\) one can obtain a
\(d'\)-regular graph \(H'\) on the same vertex set.
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Balance surplus/deficit vertices using edge-switch operations along short
alternating paths in the complement/graph pair; each switch reduces total
imbalance by \(2\). Terminate when all deficits vanish.
\(\square\)
Proposition N2 (Reduction for EP-82):
If Step B yields an induced \(m\)-vertex near-regular subgraph with
\(m/\log n\to\infty\), then combining with Proposition N1 proves the same growth
for exact induced regular subgraphs.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply N1 to regularize with negligible size loss.
\(\square\)
