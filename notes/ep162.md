# EP-162

## Problem Statement
Let $\alpha>0$ and $n\geq 1$. Let $F(n,\alpha)$ be the largest $k$ such that there exists some 2-colouring of the edges of $K_n$ in which any induced subgraph $H$ on at least $k$ vertices contains more than $\alpha\binom{\lvert H\rvert}{2}$ many edges of each colour.

Prove that for every fixed $0\leq \alpha \leq 1/2$, as $n\to\infty$,\[F(n,\alpha)\sim c_\alpha \log n\]for some constant $c_\alpha$.

  I think there may still be a typo in the definition of $F(n,\alpha)$: should "largest $k$" perhaps be "smallest $k$"?
  
  As currently written, for any $k>n$ there are no induced subgraphs of $K_n$ on at least $k$ vertices, so the stated property holds vacuously. Hence every $k>n$ is admissible, and the set of admissible $k$'s has no largest element.
  
  Even if one implicitly restricts $k\le n$, the word "largest" still seems inconsistent with the stated logarithmic behaviour: for fixed $0\le \alpha<1/2$, a nearly balanced colouring of $K_n$ makes $k=n$ admissible for all sufficiently large $n$, so the largest admissible $k\le n$ would be $n$, not $\Theta(\log n)$.
  
  Also, the endpoint $\alpha=1/2$ seems incompatible with the strict "more than" condition, since the two colour counts in $H$ sum to $\binom{|H|}{2}$.
  
  So perhaps the intended formulation is with "smallest $k$" and $0\le \alpha<1/2$?

## Local Computation
- full induced-subgraph verification of the defining condition behind F(n,\alpha) for each sampled coloring.
- In tested finite ranges, sampled best colorings reached high F values, illustrating that random colorings can satisfy the density condition only at large subset thresholds.
- Obstacle: asymptotic constant extraction F(n,\alpha)\sim c_\alpha\log n needs significantly larger-n theory and sharper optimization than this finite exact search.

## Local Proof Attempts
Reworked the problem as a 2-color discrepancy-on-subsets threshold:
find largest k such that some edge-coloring of K_n keeps both color densities
above alpha on every induced subgraph of size >=k.

Hard point:
Background already gives matching-order logarithmic bounds
c_1(alpha) log n < F(n,alpha) < c_2(alpha) log n.
The unresolved part is sharp asymptotics F(n,alpha) ~ c_alpha log n.
No new argument was found to identify c_alpha or tighten constants.

Target:
Determine the constant \(c_\alpha\) in
\[
F(n,\alpha)\sim c_\alpha \log n
\]
for fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\).
Step A: Sharp upper via discrepancy concentration
Use concentration and union bounds over all subsets to obtain explicit
\(\log n\)-upper constant.
Step B: Sharp lower via random construction
Show random (or pseudorandom) colorings satisfy the condition up to
\((c_\alpha-o(1))\log n\).
Step C: Constant matching
Align upper and lower constants to identify \(c_\alpha\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a large-deviation estimate uniform over all subset sizes above threshold
with optimal exponent in \(\alpha\).

Proposition BJ1 (First-moment upper template):
If for each subset size \(m\) the failure probability of a random coloring on a
fixed \(m\)-set is at most \(p_m\), then
\[
\mathbb P(\exists\text{ bad }m\text{-set})\le \binom{n}{m}p_m.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Union bound over all \(m\)-subsets.
\(\square\)
Proposition BJ2 (Constant extraction reduction):
If one proves matching thresholds
\[
\binom{n}{m}p_m \to 0 \quad\text{for } m\ge (c_\alpha+\varepsilon)\log n,
\]
and corresponding lower witness for
\[
m\le (c_\alpha-\varepsilon)\log n,
\]
then \(F(n,\alpha)\sim c_\alpha\log n\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Upper and lower asymptotic inequalities squeeze \(F(n,\alpha)/\log n\) to
\(c_\alpha\).
\(\square\)
