# EP-827

## Problem Statement
Let $n_k$ be minimal such that if $n_k$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ are in general position then there exists a subset of $k$ points such that all $\binom{k}{3}$ triples determine circles of different radii.

Determine $n_k$.

## Site Remarks
In \cite{Er75h} Erd\H{o}s asks whether $n_k$ exists. In \cite{Er78c} he gave a simple argument which proves that it does, and in fact\[n_k \leq k+2\binom{k-1}{2}\binom{k-1}{3},\]but this argument is incorrect, as explained by Martinez and Rold\'{a}n-Pensado \cite{MaRo15}.

Martinez and Rold\'{a}n-Pensado give a corrected argument that proves $n_k\ll k^9$.

## Local Computation
- Implemented deeper finite random-general-position experiments.
- For each sampled point set, solved exactly (branch-and-bound) for largest subset with all triangle circumradii distinct.
- This is finite exploratory evidence only; no new asymptotic bound is proved.

- In tested ranges $n=10,12,13,14$, full-size subsets were found in at least one sample for each $n$.
- Average best subset sizes were close to full size, suggesting strong finite generic behavior.
- These numerics support existence heuristics but do not improve the known polynomial bound.

## Local Proof Attempts
The finite construction enforces a quantitative quasi-Sidon threshold on the sumset-collision ratio and then grows candidate sets greedily. The resulting sizes behave like \(c\sqrt N\) across the tested window, which is exactly the expected scale, but the experiments do not sharpen the unresolved asymptotic constant.
