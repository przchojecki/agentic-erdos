# EP-390

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n)$ be the minimal $m$ such that\[n! = a_1\cdots a_k\]with $n< a_1<\cdots <a_k=m$. Is there (and what is it) a constant $c$ such that\[f(n)-2n \sim c\frac{n}{\log n}?\]

- 14:46 on 30 Aug 2025 - TerenceTao:
  This problem shares many features with #391, and in particular many of the techniques in this recent paper of Alexeev, Conway, Rosenfeld, Sutherland, Uhr, Ventullo, and myself (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20170) may be relevant here.
  
  One can summarize the arguments in [EGS82] as follows. In both the upper and lower bound one should think of the product $a_1 \dots a_k$ by starting from $(n+1) \dots 2n = n! \binom{2n}{n}$, removing some factors $b_1,\dots,b_r$ in $(n,2n]$, and then adding some factors $c_1,\dots,c_s$ in $(2n, f(n)]$. Thus, one can rewrite the equation $n! = a_1 \dots a_k$ as
  $$ b_1 \dots b_r = \binom{2n}{n} c_1 \dots c_s \quad (1)$$
  for some $n < b_1 < \dots < b_r \leq 2n$ and $2n < c_1 < \dots < c_s \leq f(n)$.
  
  For the lower bound: every prime $p$ in $(2n/4, 2n/3]$ divides $\binom{2n}{n}$ once (by Kummer's theorem), which means exactly one of $2p$ or $3p$ has to be in the $\{b_1,\dots,b_r\}$. By the prime number theorem, this creates $\gg \frac{n}{\log n}$ factors of $2$ or $3$ on the LHS of (1); since $\binom{2n}{n}$ only has $O(\log n)$ factors of each (by Kummer again), these factors have to be absorbed by the $c_1,\dots,c_s$; but $(2n,f(n)]$ only contains $O(f(n)-2n + \log n)$ copies of both $2$ and $3$, which easily leads to the lower bound $f(n)-2n \gg \frac{n}{\log n}$ (assuming $n$ large enough of course).
  
  For the upper bound: factorize $\binom{2n}{n}$ into prime powers $p^a$; one can check (again from Kummer) that all such factors are at most $2n$. For each such prime power, add $2^i p^a$ to the collection $b_1,\dots,b_r$, where $i$ is chosen so that $2^i p^a \in (n,2n]$. This balances out all the factors of $\binom{2n}{n}$, but creates excess powers of 2 on the LHS; one can calculate this excess (using the prime number theorem) to be $O(\frac{n}{\log n})$. To balance out this excess, add various $b_j$ to the LHS and $2b_j$ to the RHS for greedily chosen $n < b_j < n + O(n/\log n)$; this will establish $f(n)-2n \ll \frac{n}{\log n}$.
  
  The lower bound analysis is wasteful because it does not take into account the primes $p \in (2n/6, 2n/5]$, $p \in (2n/8, 2n/7]$, etc. (though the first few intervals should provide the dominant contribution to the bound). The upper bound analysis is wasteful because it only uses even numbers for the $c_j$ to balance powers of 2 on the LHS; one should also be able to balance powers of 3, 5, etc. with more effort.
  
  One key idea in the paper linked above is that products of consecutive integers $\prod_{j=1}^H (N+j)$ have prime factorizations that do not depend very strongly on the starting point $N$: the number of times a prime $p$ divides $\prod_{j=1}^H (N+j)$ is almost the same as the number of times it divides $\prod_{j=1}^H (N'+j)$ if $p$ is small compared to $N,N',H$. This should allow one to introduce blocks of consecutive integers on the left and right-hand sides of (1) to help balance the prime factorizations, though actually carrying out this strategy could be complicated.

## Local Proof Attempts
Background gives (Erdos--Guy--Selfridge, 1982):
f(n)-2n asymp n/log n.
So the scale/order of magnitude is known.

The existence/value of the exact asymptotic constant c is not settled by the
provided background.

Target:
Refine known order
\[
f(n)-2n\asymp \frac{n}{\log n}
\]
to an exact asymptotic constant \(c\), if it exists.
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a sharp local-prime packing theorem near \(2n\): the minimal extension length \(L=f(n)-2n\) is governed by a limiting prime-availability density in \((n,2n+L]\), yielding
\[
L\sim c\,\frac{n}{\log n}.
\]
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (factorization window form):
Condition \(n<a_1<\cdots<a_k=f(n)\) and \(a_1\cdots a_k=n!\) implies all prime valuations of \(n!\) must be supplied by factors in \((n,f(n)]\).
Proof.
Each \(a_i>n\); product identity forces exact valuation matching prime-by-prime. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (shift variable):
Set \(L_n=f(n)-2n\). Existing theorem gives
\[
L_n\asymp \frac{n}{\log n}.
\]
Proof.
Direct restatement of Erdős-Guy-Selfridge result in shift notation. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies constant law):
If A gives limiting normalized shift \(L_n/(n/\log n)\to c\), then
\[
f(n)-2n\sim c\,\frac{n}{\log n}.
\]
Proof.
Unwrap \(L_n\) definition. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The key gap is a second-order valuation-balance analysis precise enough to pass from two-sided order bounds to a single limiting constant.
