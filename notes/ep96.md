# EP-96

## Problem Statement
If $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ form a convex polygon then there are $O(n)$ many pairs which are distance $1$ apart.

## Site Remarks
Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s and Moser. In \cite{Er92e} Erd\H{o}s credits the conjecture that the true upper bound is $2n$ to himself and Fishburn. F\"{u}redi \cite{Fu90} proved an upper bound of $O(n\log n)$. A short proof of this bound was given by Brass and Pach \cite{BrPa01}. The best known upper bound is\[\leq n\log_2n+4n,\]due to Aggarwal \cite{Ag15}.

Edelsbrunner and Hajnal \cite{EdHa91} have constructed $n$ such points with $2n-7$ pairs distance $1$ apart. (This disproved an early stronger conjecture of Erd\H{o}s and Moser, that the true answer was $\frac{5}{3}n+O(1)$.)

A positive answer would follow from [97]. See also [90].

In \cite{Er92e} Erd\H{o}s makes the stronger conjecture that, if $g(x)$ counts the largest number of points equidistant from $x$ in $A$, then\[\sum_{x\in A}g(x)< 4n.\]He notes that the example of Edelsbrunner and Hajnal shows that $\sum_{x\in A}g(x)>4n-O(1)$ is possible.

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - In regular $n$-gons (after optimal scaling), the maximum number of unit pairs is exactly n in our profile.
 - Random convex-on-circle samples had very low max repeated-distance multiplicity compared to linear scale.

 - Structured examples remain linear-scale; random convex polygons do not approach the current upper bound regime.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Improve the upper bound for the maximum number of unit distances among vertices
of a convex \(n\)-gon, ideally toward linear.
Step A: Convex-order encoding
Represent unit chords by index differences and crossing constraints in cyclic
order.
Step B: Forbidden pattern elimination
Show dense unit-chord patterns force local geometric contradictions (angle/length
monotonicity) in convex position.
Step C: Global counting
Convert forbidden patterns into a linear (or near-linear) extremal bound on
number of unit chords.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a sharp combinatorial-geometric lemma controlling coexistence of many unit
chords across multiple index gaps; current arguments leave an \(n\log n\) slack.

Proposition V1 (Crossing monotonicity constraint):
In a strictly convex polygon, if unit chords are indexed by endpoints
\((i,j)\), then for fixed \(i\), the set of \(j\) with \(|v_i-v_j|=1\) forms at
most two contiguous intervals along cyclic order.
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Distance from \(v_i\) to vertices along each side of the polygon is unimodal by
strict convexity; level set \(|v_i-v_j|=1\) intersects each monotone branch in at
most one interval.
\(\square\)
Proposition V2 (Interval model reduction):
If each vertex contributes at most \(g(n)\) admissible index-interval endpoints
for unit chords, then total number \(U(n)\) of unit chords satisfies
\[
U(n)\le \frac12 n\,g(n).
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Sum per-vertex unit incidences and divide by two.
\(\square\)
