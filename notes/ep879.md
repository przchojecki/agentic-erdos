# EP-879

## Problem Statement
Call a set $S\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}$ admissible if $(a,b)=1$ for all $a\neq b\in S$. Let\[G(n) = \max_{S\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}} \sum_{a\in S}a\]and\[H(n)=\sum_{p<n}p+ n\pi(n^{1/2}).\]Is it true that\[G(n) >H(n)-n^{1+o(1)}?\]Is it true that, for every $k\geq 2$, if $n$ is sufficiently large then the admissible set which maximises $G(n)$ contains at least one integer with at least $k$ prime factors?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s and Van Lint proved that\[H(n)-n^{3/2-o(1)}<G(n)<H(n)\]and\[\frac{H(n)-G(n)}{n}\to \infty.\]They proved that $G(n)>H(n)-n^{1+o(1)}$ assuming 'plausible (but hopeless) assumptions about the distribution of primes'. They also prove the second claim when $k=2$.

See also [878].

## Local Computation
- Built an exact finite proxy optimization:
 - maximize sum of pairwise-coprime subsets of $[1..n]$ for moderate $n$ exactly,
 - compare against a simple greedy lower construction.

- Exact optimizer and greedy lower are often close, but not always equal.
- In tested $n=16,18,20,22,24$, exact maxima are modestly above greedy in several cases.
- This supports that local optimization landscape is nontrivial even at small scales.

## Local Proof Attempts
The exact small-\(n\) proxy shows that local optimization over pairwise-coprime admissible sets is already nontrivial: the naive greedy construction is close to optimal but not always exact. That is useful finite evidence about structure, but it does not sharpen the asymptotic gap between \(G(n)\) and the benchmark quantity \(H(n)\).
