# EP-324

## Problem Statement
Does there exist a polynomial $f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ such that all the sums $f(a)+f(b)$ with $a<b$ nonnegative integers are distinct?

## Site Remarks
In other words, the set $\{ f(n) : n\geq 1\}$ should be a Sidon set.

Erd\H{o}s and Graham describe this problem as 'very annoying'. Probably $f(x)=x^5$ should work. The Lander, Parkin, and Selfridge conjecture would imply that $f(x)=x^n$ has this property for all $n\geq 5$.

Ruzsa \cite{Ru01b} has proved there exists $c\in [0,1]$ and $n_0\geq 1$ such that\[\{ n^5 +\lfloor cn^4\rfloor : n \geq n_0\}\]is a Sidon set.

It is easy to check that a quadratic $f$ cannot have this property. Dubickas and Novikas \cite{DuNo21} have shown that a cubic $f$ cannot have this property. It is a classical fact that $x^4$ cannot have this property.

This is discussed in problems C9, D1, and F30 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.

- 17:33 on 06 Sep 2025 - TerenceTao:
  A trivial remark: if one can construct such an $f$ which is only asymptotically Sidon (no collisions for $a,b$ large enough), then one can remove all such collisions by replacing $f(x)$ with $f(qx+r)$ for a suitable arithmetic progression $\{qx+r: x \in {\bf Z} \}$ that avoids all the collisions.
  
  It is conceivable that one may be able to solve this problem assuming the Bombieri-Lang conjecture, in this spirit of this paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01090). If the integer (or rational) solutions to the three-dimensional variety $\{ (a,b,c,d): f(a)+f(b)=f(c)+f(d)\}$ (which presumably will be of general type for typical $f$) are trapped inside two-dimensional subvarieties, then one may be able to escape the integer points on these subvarieties by replacing $f$ with $f \circ Q$ for various polynomials $Q$.
  
  Siegel's theorem on integral points should be able to manage any one-dimensional family of solutions, so the main problems are with the two-dimensional families. The key algebraic geometry question seems to be the following: let $S$ be an irreducible surface in $\{ (a,b,c,d): f(a)+f(b)=f(c)+f(d)\}$ such that the covers $S_Q = \{(a',b',c',d'): (Q(a'),Q(b'),Q(c'),Q(d')) \in S \}$ are not of general type for any polynomial $Q$. What does this imply about $S$?

As far as I know, it is not known whether a quartic polynomial can generate a Sidon set.

## Local Proof Attempts
Background indicates that proving this for f(x)=x^n with n>=5 would follow
from the Lander-Parkin-Selfridge conjecture.
A local computation in this workspace found no collisions for f(x)=x^5 on the
range 0<=a<b<=1200 (720600 pairs checked).

No unconditional proof is provided in the cited background.

Target:
Find \(f\in\mathbb Z[x]\) such that pair sums \(f(a)+f(b)\) (\(0\le a<b\)) are all
distinct.
Step A: Collision equation reduction
Analyze Diophantine equation
\[
f(a)+f(b)=f(c)+f(d),\qquad \{a,b\}\ne\{c,d\},
\]
for candidate \(f\).
Step B: Rigidity for power-like \(f\)
Prove that for \(f(x)=x^n\) (large \(n\)), nontrivial collisions are impossible.
Step C: Integer-polynomial realization
Either prove one explicit \(f\) works unconditionally or derive from a known
Diophantine conjecture plus a verified implication.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a uniform nontrivial-solution exclusion for
\[
a^n+b^n=c^n+d^n,\quad \{a,b\}\ne\{c,d\},
\]
in the relevant nonnegative range.

Lemma 1 (Collision criterion for injectivity):
Pair-sum map \(\{a,b\}\mapsto f(a)+f(b)\) is injective iff the collision equation
above has only trivial solutions \(\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Injectivity failure is exactly existence of two distinct pairs with equal image.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Strict convexity monotonic ordering):
If \(f\) is strictly convex on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), then for fixed \(s=a+b\),
the function \(a\mapsto f(a)+f(s-a)\) is strictly increasing for
\(a\le s/2\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Discrete derivative:
\[
[f(a+1)-f(a)]-[f(s-a)-f(s-a-1)].
\]
Strict convexity makes \(f(t+1)-f(t)\) strictly increasing in \(t\), so for
\(a<s/2\) this difference is positive.
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction):
If one proves that every nontrivial collision for candidate \(f\) would force an
equal-sum identity forbidden by Lemma 2 monotonicity (or by a stronger
Diophantine theorem), then \(f\) is a valid EP-324 witness.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
By Lemma 1, absence of nontrivial collisions is equivalent to required property.
\(\square\)
