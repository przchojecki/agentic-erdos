# EP-60

## Problem Statement
Does every graph on $n$ vertices with $>\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)$ edges contain $\gg n^{1/2}$ many copies of $C_4$?

## Site Remarks
Conjectured by Erd\H{o}s and Simonovits, who could not even prove that at least $2$ copies of $C_4$ are guaranteed.

The behaviour of $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)$ is the subject of [765].

He, Ma, and Yang \cite{HeMaYa21} have proved this conjecture when $n=q^2+q+1$ for some even integer $q$.

## Site References

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Prove global supersaturation lower bound in EP-60 form (beyond special infinite
subsequences).
Step A: Local supersaturation inequality
For \(e=\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)+1\), prove
\[
\#C_4(G)\ge \psi(n)
\]
for every \(n\)-vertex graph \(G\) with \(e\) edges.
Step B: Optimize \(\psi(n)\)
Refine to target \(\psi(n)\gg \sqrt n\) (or exact conjectured order).
Step C: Uniformity in \(n\)
Eliminate special-form assumptions and prove for all large \(n\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a stability theorem near the \(C_4\)-extremal boundary: graphs with
\(\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)+1\) edges must be edit-close to extremal constructions, and
this closeness forces many \(C_4\)'s.

Proposition O1 (Supersaturation transfer):
If for all large \(n\) and all \(G\) with \(e=\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)+1\),
\[
\#C_4(G)\ge \psi(n),
\]
then EP-60 lower bound follows with function \(\psi\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Direct by definition of the extremal-over-threshold regime in the statement.
\(\square\)
Proposition O2 (Asymptotic order consequence):
If \(\psi(n)\ge c\sqrt n\) for large \(n\), then
\[
\#C_4(G)\gg \sqrt n
\]
uniformly at threshold \(e=\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)+1\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate from O1 and lower bound on \(\psi\).
\(\square\)
