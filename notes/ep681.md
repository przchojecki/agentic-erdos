# EP-681

## Problem Statement
Is it true that for all large $n$ there exists $k$ such that $n+k$ is composite and\[p(n+k)>k^2,\]where $p(m)$ is the least prime factor of $m$?

- 12:47 on 03 Mar 2026 - Przemek Chojecki:
  This should be accessible with sieve methods (Maynard/Chen-style). I did some back and forth with GPT-5.2 and here's where's the difficulty lies. First of all note that if $n+1$ is composite then one may take $k=1$, since $p(n+1)\geq 2>1=k^2$. Thus the only potentially difficult values are $n=p-1$ with $p$ prime. Any admissible witness must satisfy $k^4<n+k$, hence $k\leq n^{1/4}+O(1)$, and it is natural to search on a dyadic block $K<k\leq 2K$ with $K=n^\alpha$ and $\alpha<1/4$, so that $k^2\leq 4K^2$.
  
  Fix $\alpha\in(0,1/4)$, put $K=n^\alpha$ and $y=4K^2$, and consider the short interval $I=(n+K,n+2K]$. A sufficient condition is the existence of a composite $m\in I$ with $p(m)>y$ (then $m=n+k$ for some $k\in(K,2K]$ and $p(m)>y\geq k^2$). The sieve heuristic for $y$-rough numbers predicts $\#\{m\in I:p(m)>y\}\approx K\,\omega(u)/\log y$ with $u=\log n/\log y$ and Buchstab function $\omega$, while $\#\{m\in I:m\ \textrm{prime}\}\approx K/\log n$; since $u\omega(u)>1$ for $u>2$ (equivalently $\alpha<1/4$), the heuristic main term already suggests that $I$ should contain composite $y$-rough values. However, existing pointwise sieve methods do not currently yield such a uniform lower bound in a single length-$K$ interval when $y\asymp K^2$, because the lower linear sieve requires sieve level $R>y^2$ whereas one only has trivial remainder control for divisibility in a fixed short interval.
  
  Unconditionally, an averaging-over-$p$ substitute appears accessible: one may sieve the primes $p\in[X,2X]$ with $K=X^\alpha$ and $y=4K^2$ using Rosser--Iwaniec linear sieve weights and the Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem (level $1/2$) to obtain a positive lower bound for the mean of $R(p)=\#\{K<k\leq 2K:(p-1+k,P(y))=1\}$ provided $s=(\log R)/(\log y)>2$. Taking $R=X^{1/4-\varepsilon}$ and $y=X^{2\alpha}$ gives $s\approx 1/(8\alpha)$, so $s>2$ holds for any fixed $\alpha<1/16$, and a standard second-moment argument (with singular series averaging as in Gallagher) then yields $R(p)\gg K/\log X$ for all but $o(\pi(X))$ primes $p\in[X,2X]$. Since primes in $(p+K,p+2K]$ contribute only $\asymp K/\log X$, one obtains for almost all such primes $p$ a shift $k\in(K,2K]$ with $p-1+k$ composite and $p(p-1+k)>k^2$. References for the sieve ingredients include J. Friedlander and H. Iwaniec, Opera de Cribro (2010), and D. R. Heath-Brown, Lectures on sieves (2002); improving the exponent toward $\alpha=1/4$ would require new distribution input beyond Bombieri--Vinogradov.
