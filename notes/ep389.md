# EP-389

## Problem Statement
Is it true that for every $n\geq 1$ there is a $k$ such that\[n(n+1)\cdots(n+k-1)\mid (n+k)\cdots (n+2k-1)?\]

## Site Remarks
Asked by Erd\H{o}s and Straus.
For example when $n=2$ we have $k=5$:\[2\times 3 \times 4 \times 5\times 6 \mid 7 \times 8 \times 9\times 10\times 11.\]and when $n=3$ we have $k=4$:\[3\times 4\times 5\times 6 \mid 7\times 8\times 9\times 10.\]Bhavik Mehta has computed the minimal such $k$ for $1\leq n\leq 18$ (now available as A375071 (https://oeis.org/A375071) on the OEIS).

  Computational note for [389]. For $n=25$, I searched for a $k\ge 1$ such that\[
  25\cdot 26\cdots (25+k-1)\ \mid\ (25+k)(25+k+1)\cdots (25+2k-1).
  \]I checked all $1\le k\le 12{,}000{,}000$ and found no solution in this range, hence\[
  k_{\min}(25) > 12{,}000{,}000.
  \](Of course this does \emph{not} prove nonexistence of such a $k$ for $n=25$; it is only a lower bound.)
  
  Method: track prime-adic valuation differences\[
  d_p(k)=v_p\!\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(n+k+j)\Big)-v_p\!\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(n+j)\Big),
  \]and update incrementally via\[
  d_p(k+1)=d_p(k)+v_p(n+2k)+v_p(n+2k+1)-2v_p(n+k),
  \]factoring each integer using a precomputed smallest-prime-factor table up to $n+2k_{\max}+1$.
  As a sanity check, the implementation reproduces the sample solutions $n=2\Rightarrow k=5$ and
  $n=3\Rightarrow k=4$, and agrees with the known value $n=4\Rightarrow k=207$ (consistent with
  OEIS A375071 (https://oeis.org/A375071), noting the index shift).

## Local Proof Attempts
Attempt route:
Checked prime-valuation divisibility condition with bounded search over k.

Obstacle:
Bounded search cannot rule out larger k.

Target:
For each \(n\ge1\), decide existence of \(k\) such that
\[
\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n+i)\ \bigm|\ \prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n+k+i).
\]
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a valuation-dominance criterion: for each prime \(p\),
\[
v_p\!\left(\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n+k+i)\right)-v_p\!\left(\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n+i)\right)\ge0
\]
once \(k\) lies in an explicit progression depending on \(n\), simultaneously for all \(p\).
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (factorial ratio rewrite):
\[
\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n+i)=\frac{(n+k-1)!}{(n-1)!},\quad
\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n+k+i)=\frac{(n+2k-1)!}{(n+k-1)!}.
\]
Proof.
Apply consecutive-product factorial identities. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (divisibility as valuation inequalities):
Desired divisibility is equivalent to
\[
v_p((n+2k-1)!)+v_p((n-1)!)\ge 2v_p((n+k-1)!)
\]
for every prime \(p\).
Proof.
Substitute B1 and compare \(p\)-adic valuations. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies existence of witness \(k\)):
If A provides a \(k\) making all inequalities in B2 hold, divisibility follows.
Proof.
B2 is equivalent characterization. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is achieving simultaneous valuation dominance across all primes without requiring prohibitively large \(k\).
