# EP-148

## Problem Statement
Let $F(k)$ be the number of solutions to\[ 1= \frac{1}{n_1}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n_k},\]where $1\leq n_1<\cdots<n_k$ are distinct integers. Find good estimates for $F(k)$.

## Site Remarks
The current best bounds known are\[2^{c^{\frac{k}{\log k}}}\leq F(k) \leq c_0^{(\frac{1}{5}+o(1))2^k},\]where $c>0$ is some absolute constant and $c_0=1.26408\cdots$ is the 'Vardi constant'. The lower bound is due to Konyagin \cite{Ko14} and the upper bound to Elsholtz and Planitzer \cite{ElPl21}.

  I have carefully read [Ko14] and found that equation (4) in [Ko14] seems incorrect, because the equality right above it, namely\[
  \frac{1}{2^{3^k} - 2^{3^{k-1}}}
  = \frac{1}{2^{3^k}} + \frac{1}{2^{3^k} + 2^{3^{k-m}}} + \frac{1}{2^{3^{k+m}} + 2^{3^k}},
  \]is false, regardless of whether \(m\) appears in the exponent of 3 or not. I could not find a way to fix this step.
  
  However, the result of Elsholtz (arXiv:1606.02117v1 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02117v1)) actually covers the bound stated in [Ko14], so the lower bound’s order of magnitude remains correct. Therefore, I suggest that the statement “The lower bound is due to Konyagin [Ko14]” should also cite Elsholtz’s result.

- 10:30 on 29 Dec 2025 - Woett (reply depth 1):
  Based on this comment I have sent an e-mail to Sergei Konyagin, and he kindly responded to me. He agreed that the equation you referred to was indeed a mistake. The corrected equation is
  \begin{align*}
  \frac{1}{2^{3^k} - 2^{3^{k-1}}} &= \frac{1}{2^{3^k}} + \frac{1}{(2^{2 \times 3^{k-1}}-1)2^{3^k}} \\
  &= \frac{1}{2^{3^k}} + \frac{1}{(2^{2 \times 3^{k-1}}-1)(2^{3^k}+2^{3^k-m})} + \frac{1}{(2^{2 \times 3^{k-1}}-1)(2^{3^k}+2^{3^k+m})}. \hspace{10pt} (1)
  \end{align*}He further explained that he made a few more small mistakes (e.g. the statement of Lemma $1$ in his paper should contain the assumption that $m$ is odd), but the main result still holds. In any case, the point of equation $(1)$ is that the final two denominators are divisible by $2^m + 1$, which by his Lemma $1$ has many divisors, eventually leading to the claimed lower bound on $F(k)$.

\textit{Proof.}
\]
Iterate the inequalities on \(\log\)-scale and compare growth rates.
