# EP-997

## Problem Statement
Call $x_1,x_2,\ldots \in (0,1)$ well-distributed if, for every $\epsilon>0$, if $k$ is sufficiently large then, for all $n>0$ and intervals $I\subseteq [0,1]$,\[\lvert \# \{ n<m\leq n+k : x_m\in I\} - \lvert I\rvert k\rvert < \epsilon k.\]Is it true that, for every $\alpha$, the sequence $\{ \alpha p_n\}$ is not well-distributed, if $p_n$ is the sequence of primes?

## Site Remarks
The notion of a well-distributed sequence was introduced by Hlawka and Petersen \cite{Hl55}.

Erd\H{o}s proved that, if $n_k$ is a lacunary sequence, then the sequence $\{ \alpha n_k\}$ is not well-distributed for almost all $\alpha$.

He also claimed in \cite{Er64b} to have proved that there exists an irrational $\alpha$ for which $\{\alpha p_n\}$ is not well-distributed. He later retracted this claim in \cite{Er85e}, saying "The theorem is no doubt correct and perhaps will not be difficult to prove but I never was able to reconstruct my 'proof' which perhaps never existed ."

The existence of such an $\alpha$ was established by Champagne, Le, Liu, and Wooley \cite{CLLW24}.

An internal model at OpenAI \cite{APSSV26} has proved that the sequence $\{\alpha p_n\}$ is not well-distributed for any $\alpha$, using the work of Banks, Freiberg, and Turnage-Butterbaugh \cite{BFT15} on consecutive primes with small gaps (which in turn builds on the work of Maynard and Tao on bounded gaps between primes).

  The problem is solved now, but on looking at the proof I see that my former student, Jacques Benatar, managed to essentially prove a partial result on this problem in 2013 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0348), again using the same Maynard sieve machinery. Namely, from Theorem 1.3 of that paper it follows that for any Bohr set $\{ n: \{ \alpha n \} \leq \delta \}$ with $\alpha$ Diophantine, and any $m$, there exist $m$ primes in that set of diameter $O_{\delta,\varepsilon}(e^{(4+\varepsilon) m})$. The theorem does not explicitly state that these primes are in fact consecutive (no intervening primes outside of the Bohr set), but this can be accomplished by a standard additional upper bound Maynard sieve estimate. From this, one would be able to obtain the Diophantine case of this problem. The proof in [APSSV26] gets around the need to assume Diophantine conditions by approximating $\alpha$ by a rational and finding consecutive primes of small diameter in a suitable congruence class instead, which is a simpler way to proceed.

## Local Computation
- Formalized the discrepancy and exponential-sum equivalence in the note.
- Added a deeper standalone finite proxy computation:
 - first $120{,}000$ primes,
 - window lengths $500,1000,2000,5000,10000$,
 - $30$ sampled $\alpha$ values (named constants + random irrationals),
 - $70$ test intervals,
 - maximal sliding-window relative deviation extraction.

- Across tested $\alpha$, max relative deviation decreases as window size grows (roughly from $\sim 0.07$-$0.09$ at $k=500$ down to $\sim 0.011$-$0.015$ at $k=10{,}000$ for named constants).
- Finite data is consistent with gradual averaging, but does not prove asymptotic well-distribution for every irrational $\alpha$.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Establish equidistribution/well-distribution of \(\{\alpha p_n\}\) for irrational \(\alpha\).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a uniform exponential-sum bound for every nonzero integer \(h\):
\[
\sum_{n\le N} e(h\alpha p_n)=o(N)
\]
for irrational \(\alpha\), with quantitative decay enough for discrepancy bounds.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (Weyl criterion):
Equidistribution of \(\{\alpha p_n\}\) is equivalent to B1-type cancellation for all \(h\neq0\).
Proof.
Direct Weyl criterion for sequences on \(\mathbb T\). \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (Vinogradov transfer to primes):
Exponential sums over primes can be controlled by weighted sums over integers via \(\Lambda\) and partial summation.
Proof.
Use identity \(\sum_{p\le x} a_p = \sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\log n}a_n + \text{error}\), then Abel summation. \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies discrepancy decay):
If A holds with power/log savings, Erdős-Turán gives \(D_N=o(N)\) (or quantitative rates).
Proof.
Plug the exponential-sum bound into Erdős-Turán and optimize truncation. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The difficult step is obtaining strong cancellation in \(\sum e(h\alpha p)\) uniformly in \(h\) for general irrational \(\alpha\), beyond existing average-type estimates.
