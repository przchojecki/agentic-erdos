# EP-81

## Problem Statement
Let $G$ be a chordal graph on $n$ vertices - that is, $G$ has no induced cycles of length greater than $3$. Can the edges of $G$ be partitioned into $n^2/6+O(n)$ many cliques?

## Site Remarks
Asked by Erd\H{o}s, Ordman, and Zalcstein \cite{EOZ93}, who proved an upper bound of $(1/4-\epsilon)n^2$ many cliques (for some very small $\epsilon>0$). The example of all edges between a complete graph on $n/3$ vertices and an empty graph on $2n/3$ vertices show that $n^2/6+O(n)$ is sometimes necessary.

A split graph is one where the vertices can be split into a clique and an independent set. Every split graph is chordal. Chen, Erd\H{o}s, and Ordman \cite{CEO94} have shown that any split graph can be partitioned into $\frac{3}{16}n^2+O(n)$ many cliques.

See also [1017].

## Local Computation
- exact branch-and-bound computation of edge-clique partition number on chordal split-graph families.
- In tested split-graph samples, computed partition counts were well below n^2/6 (negative gap), consistent with the lower-bound example not being typical.
- Obstacle: the conjectural upper bound for all chordal graphs is extremal and requires worst-case structural constructions; random split-graph sampling cannot capture the sharp extremal mechanism alone.

## Local Proof Attempts
Route:
Recast the problem as edge clique cover in chordal graphs and compared with known extremal split-graph constructions.

Hard point:
Closing the factor gap between about 1/4 and 1/6 requires sharper chordal-structure decomposition than currently available.

Target:
Sharpen the universal upper bound for edge clique partition number of chordal
graphs, ideally down to the conjectural scale near \(n^2/6\).
Step A: Clique-tree charging scheme
Represent a chordal graph by a clique tree and assign each edge to one bag while
controlling overlaps.
Step B: Degree-constrained optimization
Translate maximal overlap constraints into a quadratic program on bag sizes and
separator sizes.
Step C: Extremal template identification
Show split-like templates maximize the objective, yielding the sharp constant.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a global inequality linking sum of separator multiplicities to bag-size
squares with the right constant; existing bounds lose too much.

Proposition M1 (Tree decomposition counting identity):
Let \(G\) be chordal with clique tree \(\mathcal T\), maximal cliques
\(\{K_i\}\), and separators \(S_e\) on edges \(e\in E(\mathcal T)\). Then
\[
|E(G)|=\sum_i \binom{|K_i|}{2}-\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal T)}\binom{|S_e|}{2}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Every graph edge appears in all bags along a connected subtree of
\(\mathcal T\). Inclusion-exclusion on tree edges subtracts repeated counts
exactly once per separator incidence, giving the formula.
\(\square\)
Proposition M2 (Optimization reduction):
If one proves a sharp universal upper bound for
\[
\sum_i \binom{|K_i|}{2}-\sum_{e}\binom{|S_e|}{2}
\]
under chordal feasibility constraints, then the corresponding edge-clique
partition upper bound follows.
\[
\textit{Proof sketch.}
\]
Use M1 to convert structural constraints to a finite optimization problem; then
translate each extremal configuration back to a chordal graph template.
\(\square\)
