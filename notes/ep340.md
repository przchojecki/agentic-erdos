# EP-340

## Problem Statement
Let $A=\{1,2,4,8,13,21,31,45,66,81,97,\ldots\}$ be the greedy Sidon sequence: we begin with $1$ and iteratively include the next smallest integer that preserves the Sidon property (i.e. there are no non-trivial solutions to $a+b=c+d$). What is the order of growth of $A$? Is it true that\[\lvert A\cap \{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert \gg N^{1/2-\epsilon}\]for all $\epsilon>0$ and large $N$?

## Site Remarks
This sequence is sometimes called the Mian-Chowla sequence. It is trivial that this sequence grows at least like $\gg N^{1/3}$.

Erd\H{o}s and Graham \cite{ErGr80} also asked about the difference set $A-A$, whether this has positive density, and whether this contains $22$. It does contain $22$, since $a_{15}-a_{14}=204-182=22$. The smallest integer which is unknown to be in $A-A$ is $33$ (see A080200 (https://oeis.org/A080200)). It may be true that all or almost all integers are in $A-A$.

This sequence is at OEIS A005282 (https://oeis.org/A005282).

See also [156].

## Local Proof Attempts
Hard point:
Finite-generation evidence does not settle asymptotic lower bounds near N^{1/2}.

Target:
Establish lower bounds for greedy Sidon counting function \(A(N):=|A\cap[1,N]|\),
ideally \(A(N)\gg N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\).
Step A: Increment-control inequality
Bound growth of next term \(a_{m+1}\) in terms of \(m\) by counting forbidden
differences/sums.
Step B: Differential inequality for inverse counting
Translate \(a_m\) bounds into lower bounds on \(A(N)\) via inversion.
Step C: Exponent extraction
Optimize Step A constants to approach exponent \(1/2\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a sharp forbidden-value count at greedy step \(m\) showing
\(a_{m+1}\le m^{2+o(1)}\).

Lemma 1 (Sidon difference constraint):
For first \(m\) terms \(a_1<\cdots<a_m\), there are \(\binom m2\) distinct positive
differences.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Sidon property implies all pairwise differences are distinct.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Quadratic inversion principle):
If \(a_m\le C m^\alpha\) for all large \(m\), then
\[
A(N)\ge c N^{1/\alpha}
\]
for some \(c>0\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
For given \(N\), let \(m=A(N)\), so \(a_m\le N\). Then
\[
N\ge a_m\ge C^{-1}m^\alpha
\]
up to constants, hence \(m\ll N^{1/\alpha}\) inverted gives lower bound.
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction):
To prove \(A(N)\gg N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), it suffices to prove for every
\(\varepsilon>0\):
\[
a_m\ll m^{2+\varepsilon}.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply Lemma 2 with \(\alpha=2+\varepsilon\).
\(\square\)
