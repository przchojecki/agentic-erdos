# EP-80

## Problem Statement
Let $c>0$ and let $f_c(n)$ be the maximal $m$ such that every graph $G$ with $n$ vertices and at least $cn^2$ edges, where each edge is contained in at least one triangle, must contain a book of size $m$, that is, an edge shared by at least $m$ different triangles.

Estimate $f_c(n)$. In particular, is it true that $f_c(n)>n^{\epsilon}$ for some $\epsilon>0$? Or $f_c(n)\gg \log n$?

## Site Remarks
A problem of Erd\H{o}s and Rothschild. Alon and Trotter showed that, provided $c<1/4$, $f_c(n)\ll_c n^{1/2}$. Szemer\'{e}di observed that his regularity lemma implies that $f_c(n)\to \infty$.

Edwards (unpublished) and Khadzhiivanov and Nikiforov \cite{KhNi79} proved independently that $f_c(n) \geq n/6$ when $c>1/4$ (see [905]).

Fox and Loh \cite{FoLo12} proved that\[f_c(n) \leq n^{O(1/\log\log n)}\]for all $c<1/4$, disproving the first conjecture of Erd\H{o}s.

The best known lower bounds for $f_c(n)$ are those from Szemer\'{e}di's regularity lemma, and as such remain very poor.

See also [600] and the entry in the graphs problem collection (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/CoveredInTriangles.html).

## Local Computation
Finite compute signal:
 - In complete-multipartite models ($n=300$), edge density from $0.333$ to $0.458$ coincides with large books of size about $(0.333..0.833)n$.

 - Structured dense examples naturally force linear-size books in these regimes, but this does not settle universal lower growth for all admissible graphs.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
For graphs with edge density threshold in the EP-80 regime, force a linear-size
book \(B_t\) (many triangles sharing one edge) with explicit \(t\ge c n\).
Step A: Stability decomposition
Show any near-extremal graph is close to a complete multipartite template plus a
small error set.
Step B: Heavy-edge extraction
Prove that unless the error set is too large, some edge \(uv\) has
\[
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\ge c n,
\]
giving a linear book.
Step C: Contradiction in the exceptional case
If all common-neighborhood sizes are \(o(n)\), derive a global triangle deficit
that contradicts the assumed density regime.
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a quantitative triangle-count-to-book-size transfer at the exact threshold
window; known supersaturation bounds are too coarse there.

Proposition L1 (Edge-common-neighborhood criterion):
If a graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices has an edge \(uv\) with
\[
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\ge t,
\]
then \(G\) contains a book of size at least \(t\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
For each \(w\in N(u)\cap N(v)\), triangle \(uvw\) exists. All these triangles
share edge \(uv\), yielding a book with \(t\) pages.
\(\square\)
Proposition L2 (Reduction to a neighborhood lower bound):
To prove a linear book lower bound \(t\ge c n\), it is enough to show that every
graph in the target density class has some edge \(uv\) with
\[
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\ge c n.
\]
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate from Proposition L1.
\(\square\)
