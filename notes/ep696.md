# EP-696

## Problem Statement
Let $h(n)$ be the largest $\ell$ such that there is a sequence of primes $p_1<\cdots < p_\ell$ all dividing $n$ with $p_{i+1}\equiv 1\pmod{p_i}$. Let $H(n)$ be the largest $u$ such that there is a sequence of integers $d_1<\cdots < d_u$ all dividing $n$ with $d_{i+1}\equiv 1\pmod{d_i}$.

Estimate $h(n)$ and $H(n)$. Is it true that $H(n)/h(n)\to \infty$ for almost all $n$?

Erd\H{o}s writes it is 'easy to see' that $h(n)\to \infty$ for almost all $n$ (which is proved in the comments by van Doorn), and believed he could show that the normal order of $h(n)$ is $\log_*(n)$ (the iterated logarithm (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterated_logarithm)).

  In terms of methods, both the lower bounds and the upper bounds stem from a common scaffold: a tower-rank descent over the iterated tower $T_m = \exp T_{m-1}$, controlled by what counts as a "bad" short step.
  
  For upper bounds, a chain step is *bad* if it is too short. For $H$, a divisor pair $(d, e)$ with $e \equiv 1 \pmod d$ is bad when $e < \exp \sqrt{d}$; since $(d, e) = 1$ the count of $n \leq x$ with $d, e \mid n$ is $\leq x/(de)$, and the tail $\sum_{d \geq Y} d^{-3/2} = O(Y^{-1/2})$ (where $Y = Y(x)$ is a slowly-growing small-divisor cutoff, e.g. $Y = \lfloor (\log_* x)^{1/2} \rfloor$) shows almost all $n$ admit no large bad step, forcing one full tower level per step. For $h$, primes give Brun-Titchmarsh slack and the bad threshold pushes up to $Q(p) = \exp \exp(p/(\log p)^2)$, with convergent tail $\sum_p \big( \log p/p^2 + 1/(p (\log p)^2) \big) < \infty$. The stronger threshold forces two tower levels per step, which is the source of the $1/2$ constant.
  
  Both lower bounds stem from a greedy construction in the independent-prime model (each prime selected with probability $1/p$, independently) and transfer to integers by the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) once the primorial cutoff is $o(x)$. For $h$, each stage finds a successor prime $q \equiv 1 \pmod{p_j}$ in the window $\exp \exp y_j < q \leq \exp \exp((B/2) y_j)$ via a Siegel-Walfisz reciprocal-mass lemma in the safe modulus range $p_j \leq y_j \ll \log(\exp \exp y_j)$. (Here $y_j = T_{m_0+2(j-1)}/B$ is the stage-$j$ tower scale, and $B = (\log_* x)^2$ is a growth-rate constant.)
  
  For $H$, the new - I believe new - technical input from GPT-5.5-Pro is a "subset-product successor lemma": for $g_1, \ldots, g_K$ independent uniform on a finite abelian group $G$ of order $N$, $\Pr(\text{no nonempty subproduct equals } 1_G) \leq N/(2^K - 1)$ (proof roughly goes like: pairwise independence of distinct subset-products yields $\mathrm{Var}\, Z \leq \mathbb{E} Z$, then Chebyshev). The number-theoretic application splits the prime window into Mertens-sized blocks $(\alpha_k, \alpha_{k+1}]$ with $\log \log \alpha_k = \log y + k - 1$ (here $y$ is the Siegel-Walfisz scale parameter; the modulus $d$ satisfies $d \leq y$, and primes live in $(\exp y, \exp(y^{A-1})]$ with $A = 20$), conditions on the $\sim \gamma \log y$ blocks each containing exactly one selected prime ($\gamma = 15$ in the write-up), and reduces those primes mod $d$. Siegel-Walfisz makes the residues $O(y^{-1/2})$-close to uniform on $(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^\times$, then the abstract lemma produces a squarefree composite successor $e \equiv 1 \pmod d$.

Excluding short steps forces large divisor-chain steps to grow at least like exp(sqrt d), which gives H(n) << log_* x.

  Y_1=\exp(\exp(\sqrt{\log_* x})),\qquad
  Y_{j+1}=\exp(\exp(Y_j^2)).
  \]The transfer from this finite prime-divisibility model back to integers n <= x is done modulo the primorial M over the primes up to Y_k. Each event depending only on which primes up to Y_k divide n is a union of residue classes modulo M, and the Chinese remainder theorem identifies the uniform residue model exactly with independent divisibility events, with probability 1/p for each prime p. The tower estimate gives M=o(x), so this product-model calculation transfers to natural density up to o(1).

  For the claim that $h(n)$ goes to infinity for almost all $n$, one can use the fact (also mentioned in that very same 1979 paper) that almost all $n$ are divisible by a prime $p \in P$ for some set of primes $P$ if, and only if, the sum $\sum_{p \in P} \frac{1}{p}$ diverges. More precisely, let $c \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\epsilon > 0$ both be arbitrary but fixed. Let $C$ be such that for every set of primes $P$ with $\sum_{p \in P} \frac{1}{p} > C$ we have that the density of $n$ with a prime divisor $p \in P$ is larger than $1 - \frac{\epsilon}{c}$.
  
  In step $1$ we let $P_1$ be any set of primes with $\sum_{p \in P_1} \frac{1}{p} > C$. Now, for $1 \le k < c$, in step $k+1$ we choose, for every prime $p \in P_k$, a set of primes $Q_p$ (disjoint from all primes chosen so far) with $q \equiv 1 \pmod p$ for all $q \in Q_p$, and $\sum_{q \in Q_p} \frac{1}{q} > C$. That this is possible follows from the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions. We then set $P_{k+1} = \bigcup_{p \in P_k} Q_p$.
  
  The density of $n$ with a prime divisor $p_1 \in P_1$ is larger than $1 - \frac{\epsilon}{c}$. And for every $1 \le k < c$, the density of $n$ with a prime divisor $p_{k+1} \in Q_{p_k}$ is also larger than $1 - \frac{\epsilon}{c}$. By the union bound the density of $n$ with primes $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_c$ dividing $n$ with $p_{k+1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p_k}$ is larger than $1 - \epsilon$. Since both $\epsilon > 0$ and $c \in \mathbb{N}$ were arbitrary, this proves the claim that $h(n)$ goes to infinity for almost all $n$.

## Local Computation
- Computed exact \(h(n)\) and \(H(n)\) for all \(2\le n\le N\) by explicit DAG longest-path calculations:
 - prime-divisor DAG for \(h(n)\),
 - full-divisor DAG for \(H(n)\).
- Used this to measure empirical mean growth and frequency of strict separation \(H(n)>h(n)\).

- Deep run to \(N=120000\).
- At \(n\le 120000\):
 - mean \(h\approx 1.8174\), mean \(H\approx 2.2183\),
 - fraction with \(H(n)>h(n)\) about \(0.2699\).
- Best observed gap in this range: \(n=27720\) with \((h,H)=(3,12)\), ratio \(H/h=4\).

## Local Proof Attempts
I reduced the finite problem to exact longest-path computations in divisor DAGs: one DAG restricted to prime divisors for \(h(n)\), and one over all divisors for \(H(n)\). This makes the separation \(H(n)>h(n)\) completely explicit on the tested range and shows that the ratio can already be noticeably large at finite scale, but it gives no normal-order theorem and does not prove that \(H(n)/h(n)\to\infty\) for almost all \(n\).
