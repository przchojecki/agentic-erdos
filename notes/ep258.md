# EP-258

## Problem Statement
Let $a_1,a_2,\ldots$ be a sequence of positive integers with $a_n\to \infty$. Is\[\sum_{n} \frac{\tau(n)}{a_1\cdots a_n}\]irrational, where $\tau(n)$ is the number of divisors of $n$?

## Site Remarks
Erd\H{o}s and Straus \cite{ErSt71} proved this is true if $a_n$ is monotone, i.e. $a_{n-1}\leq a_n$ for all $n$. Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er48} proved that $\sum_n \frac{\tau(n)}{t^n}$ is irrational for any integer $t\geq 2$.

Chojeckl and GPT-5.4 Pro have answered this question (https://www.ulam.ai/research/erdos258.pdf) in the affirmative, using the work of Tao and Ter\"{a}v\"{a}inen \cite{TaTe25} (see [69] and [248]).

Erd\H{o}s and Straus further conjectured that if $a_{n-1}\leq a_n$ for all $n$ then\[\sum_{n} \frac{\phi(n)}{a_1\cdots a_n}\]and\[\sum_{n} \frac{\sigma(n)}{a_1\cdots a_n}\]are both irrational.

## Local Computation
- rational-approximation scans of finite truncations of \sum \tau(n)/(a_1\cdots a_n).
- Across tested models, no exceptionally strong rational approximations emerged in the scanned denominator range.
- Obstacle: finite approximation scans are heuristic and cannot settle irrationality for the full infinite series in the general non-monotone case.

## Local Proof Attempts
The background says that Erdos and Straus proved irrationality when \((a_n)\) is monotone nondecreasing.

A natural extension attempt is to pass from monotone sequences to broader non-monotone classes by block or effective-monotone decompositions of the denominator products. The main obstruction is that after truncation or shifting, the resulting tails involve \(\tau(n+\text{offset})\) in the numerator, so the monotone theorem does not transfer in a black-box way.

Target:
Extend irrationality from monotone \(a_n\) to all positive integer sequences
\(a_n\to\infty\) in
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\tau(n)}{a_1\cdots a_n}.
\]
Step A: Product-growth normalization
Analyze denominator products \(Q_n=a_1\cdots a_n\) and extract subsequences with
controlled growth ratios.
Step B: Shifted-divisor handling
Control the effect of non-monotone blocks that introduce \(\tau(n+\Delta)\)-type
tails in reduction attempts.
Step C: Irrationality criterion
Prove no eventual periodic or rational-tail structure can persist under
\(a_n\to\infty\).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a robust irrationality criterion that depends only on \(Q_{n+1}/Q_n\to\infty\)
along subsequences, without monotonicity of \(a_n\).

Proposition DE1 (Tail control):
Let \(S=\sum_{n\ge1}\tau(n)/Q_n\), \(Q_n=a_1\cdots a_n\). Then
\[
0<S-S_N\le \sum_{n>N}\frac{n^\varepsilon}{Q_n}
\]
for any fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and large \(n\), using \(\tau(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^\varepsilon\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply the standard divisor-function bound termwise.
\(\square\)
Proposition DE2 (Reduction):
If one proves infinitely many \(N\) with denominator-growth windows where no
rational with denominator \(\le Q_N^\theta\) approximates \(S\) within the DE1
tail scale, then \(S\) is irrational.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
A rational \(S=p/q\) would force compatible approximations from partial sums at
all large \(N\), contradicting the windowed exclusion.
\(\square\)
