# EP-170

## Problem Statement
Let $F(N)$ be the smallest possible size of $A\subset \{0,1,\ldots,N\}$ such that $\{0,1,\ldots,N\}\subset A-A$. Find the value of\[\lim_{N\to \infty}\frac{F(N)}{N^{1/2}}.\]

## Site Remarks
The Sparse Ruler (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_ruler) problem. R\'{e}dei asked whether this limit exists, which was proved by Erd\H{o}s and G\'{a}l \cite{ErGa48}. Bounds on the limit were improved by Leech \cite{Le56}. The limit is known to be in the interval $[1.56,\sqrt{3}]$. The lower bound is due to Leech \cite{Le56}, the upper bound is due to Wichmann \cite{Wi63}. Computational evidence by Pegg \cite{Pe20} suggests that the upper bound is the truth. A similar question can be asked without the restriction $A\subset \{0,1,\ldots,N\}$.

- 09:13 on 16 Sep 2025 - Sean Eberhard:
  Leech also showed that $\lim F(N) / N^{1/2} = \inf (F(N)+2)/(N+1)^{1/2}$. This is rather striking because if the limit is $\sqrt 3$ (as mildly suggested by computational evidence) then in fact from the infimum formula and Wichman's result it follows that $F(N) = \sqrt{3N} + O(1)$.

Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a second-order lower bound capturing overlap inefficiency in difference
coverage beyond the trivial \(\binom{m}{2}\ge N\).

Proposition BN1 (Classical counting lower bound):
If \(m\) marks cover all differences \(1,\dots,N\), then
\[
\binom{m}{2}\ge N,
\]
hence \(m\ge \sqrt{2N}+O(1)\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Each unordered pair contributes one difference; at most \(\binom{m}{2}\)
distinct positive differences are available.
\(\square\)
Proposition BN2 (Constant-extraction reduction):
If one proves constructions with \(m\le (c_++o(1))\sqrt N\) and universal lower
bound \(m\ge (c_- -o(1))\sqrt N\), then asymptotic constant lies in
\([c_-,c_+]\); matching \(c_-=c_+\) resolves EP-170.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Divide bounds by \(\sqrt N\) and pass to limit superior/inferior.
\(\square\)
