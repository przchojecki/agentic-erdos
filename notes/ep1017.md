# EP-1017

## Problem Statement
Let $f(n,k)$ be such that every graph on $n$ vertices and $k$ edges can be partitioned into at most $f(n,k)$ edge-disjoint complete graphs. Estimate $f(n,k)$ for $k>n^2/4$.

## Site Remarks
The function $f(n,k)$ is sometimes called the clique partition number.

Erd\H{o}s, Goodman, and P\'{o}sa \cite{EGP66} proved that $f(n,k)\leq n^2/4$ for all $k$ (and in fact the complete graphs can be taken to be edges and triangles), which is best possible in general, as witnessed for example by a complete bipartite graph. In \cite{Er71} Erd\H{o} asks vaguely whether this result can be 'sharpened' for $k>n^2/4$.

Lov\'{a}sz \cite{Lo68} proved that every graph on $n$ vertices and $k$ edges is the union of $\binom{n}{2}-k+t$ complete graphs, where $t$ is maximal such that $t^2-t\leq \binom{n}{2}-k$, but without the assumption that the complete graphs are edge disjoint. Lov\'{a}sz's result is sharp in many cases.

If $k>n^2/4$ and the graph contains no $K_4$ then this is equivalent to finding the minimum number of edge disjoint triangles. This special case was also asked about by Erd\H{o}s. A complete answer was provided by Gy\"{o}ri and Keszegh \cite{GyKe17}, who proved that every $K_4$-free graph with $n$ vertices and $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+m$ edges contins $m$ pairwise edge disjoint triangles.

See also [184] for an analogous problem decomposing into edges and cycles, and [583] for decomposing into paths. The clique partition problem for chordal graphs is the subject of [81].

## Local Computation
- Kept explicit deterministic construction:
 - start from balanced $K_{a,b}$,
 - add $q$ matching edges inside one side,
 - build $q$ pairwise edge-disjoint triangles explicitly.
- deterministic verification over a large finite grid:
 - even $n\in[20,200]$,
 - many $q$ values per $n$,
 - exact structural checks recorded for each instance.

- All tested constructive instances satisfy the certified target
\[
\nu_3(G)\ge q.
\]
- The construction simultaneously keeps $K_4$-freeness and exact edge excess $q$ over $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ by design.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Quantify optimal lower bounds for edge-disjoint triangle packing \(\nu_3(G)\) in \(K_4\)-free graphs with edge excess \(q\) above \(\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor\).
A. Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Prove a stability theorem: every near-extremal \(K_4\)-free graph with excess \(q\) is close to bipartite plus sparse internal edges, and each internal edge can be assigned to an edge-disjoint triangle except for \(o(q)\) losses.
B. Reduction Lemmas (with proofs):
Lemma B1 (constructive lower family):
In the balanced bipartite-plus-matching construction, one gets \(\nu_3(G)\ge q\).
Proof.
Each added internal matching edge \(uu'\) forms a triangle with a distinct vertex on opposite side; matching ensures disjointness across triangles. \(\square\)
Lemma B2 (edge-excess accounting):
\[
e(G)=\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor+q
\]
means exactly \(q\) edges beyond Turán bipartite baseline.
Proof.
Immediate by definition of excess parameter \(q\). \(\square\)
Lemma B3 (A implies asymptotic sharpness):
If A yields \(\nu_3(G)\ge q-o(q)\) universally and construction gives \(\nu_3(G)\le q\) examples up to lower-order terms, then optimal guarantee is \(q(1-o(1))\).
Proof.
Combine universal lower with matching family upper behavior. \(\square\)
C. Main unresolved point:
The bottleneck is handling adversarial internal-edge geometry where triangles compete for shared cross edges, beyond clean matching constructions.
