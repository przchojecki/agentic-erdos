# EP-322

## Problem Statement
Let $k\geq 3$ and $A\subset \mathbb{N}$ be the set of $k$th powers. What is the order of growth of $1_A^{(k)}(n)$, i.e. the number of representations of $n$ as the sum of $k$ many $k$th powers? Does there exist some $c>0$ and infinitely many $n$ such that\[1_A^{(k)}(n) >n^c?\]

## Site Remarks
Connected to Waring's problem. The famous Hypothesis $K$ of Hardy and Littlewood was that $1_A^{(k)}(n)\leq n^{o(1)}$, but this was disproved by Mahler \cite{Ma36} for $k=3$, who constructed infinitely many $n$ such that\[1_A^{(3)}(n)\gg n^{1/12}\](where $A$ is the set of cubes). Erd\H{o}s believed Hypothesis $K$ fails for all $k\geq 4$, but this is unknown. Hardy and Littlewood made the weaker Hypothesis $K^*$ that for all $N$ and $\epsilon>0$\[\sum_{n\leq N}1_A^{(k)}(n)^2 \ll_\epsilon N^{1+\epsilon}.\]Erd\H{o}s and Graham remark: 'This is probably true but no doubt very deep. However, it would suffice for most applications.'

Independently Erd\H{o}s \cite{Er36} and Chowla proved that for all $k\geq 3$ and infinitely many $n$\[1_A^{(k)}(n) \gg n^{c/\log\log n}\]for some constant $c>0$ (depending on $k$). In \cite{Er65b} Erd\H{o}s claims an unpublished proof that, if $B$ is the set of $k$th powers of any set of positive density, then\[\limsup 1_B^{(k)}(n)=\infty.\]This is discussed in problem D4 of Guy's collection \cite{Gu04}.

## Local Proof Attempts
Target:
Determine growth of maximal representation multiplicity
\[
r_{k,3}(n):=\#\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^3:\ a^k+b^k+c^k=n\}
\]
along infinitely many \(n\), especially polynomial lower growth in \(n\).
Step A: Parametric-family production
Construct algebraic families of equal-sum \(k\)-th-power triples generating many
collisions at the same \(n\).
Step B: Height counting on parameter space
Count distinct parameter values up to size \(T\) and transfer to \(n\)-scale.
Step C: Multiplicity lower bound
Infer \(r_{k,3}(n)\ge n^\alpha\) infinitely often (for some \(\alpha>0\)).
Concrete Blocking Lemma (Most Critical):
Need a nondegeneracy lemma: parameterized collision family produces
pairwise-distinct triples after removing a thin exceptional set.

Lemma 1 (Collision-to-multiplicity transfer):
If there are \(M\) distinct triples \((a_i,b_i,c_i)\) with the same value
\(a_i^k+b_i^k+c_i^k=n\), then \(r_{k,3}(n)\ge M\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Definition of \(r_{k,3}(n)\) is exactly the number of such triples.
\(\square\)
Lemma 2 (Infinite-often principle):
Suppose for infinitely many parameters \(T\) one can produce \(M(T)\) distinct
triples with common sum \(n(T)\), and \(M(T)\ge n(T)^\alpha\). Then
\[
r_{k,3}(n)\ge n^\alpha
\]
for infinitely many integers \(n\).
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Apply Lemma 1 at \(n=n(T)\) for infinitely many \(T\).
\(\square\)
Lemma 3 (Reduction):
To prove a polynomial lower bound infinitely often, it suffices to build one
parameterized family satisfying Lemma 2 after deduplication.
\[
\textit{Proof.}
\]
Immediate from Lemma 2.
\(\square\)
