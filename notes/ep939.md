# EP-939

## Problem Statement
Let $r\geq 2$. An $r$-powerful number $n$ is one such that if $p\mid n$ then $p^r\mid n$.

If $r\geq 4$ then can the sum of $r-2$ coprime $r$-powerful numbers ever be itself $r$-powerful? Are there at most finitely many such solutions?

Are there infinitely many triples of coprime $3$-powerful numbers $a,b,c$ such that $a+b=c$?

## Site Remarks
The answer to the third question is yes: Nitaj \cite{Ni95} has proved that there are infinitely many triples of coprime $3$-powerful numbers $a,b,c$ such that $a+b=c$, such as\[2^3\cdot 3^5\cdot 73^3+271^3 = 919^3.\]In Nitaj's construction at least two of $a,b,c$ are perfect cubes. Cohn \cite{Co98} constructed infinitely many such triples, none of which are perfect cubes. An alternative construction was given by Walsh \cite{Wa24}.

Euler had conjectured that the sum of $k-1$ many $k$th powers is never a $k$th power, but this is false for $k=5$, as Lander and Parkin \cite{LaPa67} found\[27^5+84^5+110^5+133^5=144^5.\]Cambie has found several examples of the sum of $r-2$ coprime $r$-powerful numbers being itself $r$-powerful. For example when $r=5$\[3^761^5=2^83^{10}5^7+2^{12}23^6+11^513^5.\]Cambie has also found solutions when $r=7$ or $r=8$ (the latter even with the sum of $5$ $8$-powerful numbers being $8$-powerful).

## Local Computation
- Method: exact SPF scan up to \(B\), classify by minimum prime-adic exponent, then brute-force additive tests with pairwise-coprime filters.
- Run: \(B=5\cdot 10^7\).
- Counts of \(r\)-powerful numbers up to \(B\):
 - \(r=3\): 1281
 - \(r=4\): 380
 - \(r=5\): 177
- Found patterns:
 - coprime \(r=3\), \(a+b=c\): 0
 - coprime \(r=4\), \(a+b=c\): 0
 - coprime \(r=5\), \(a+b+c=d\): 0

No small-height examples appear even with a much larger bound, which is consistent with rarity/structured constructions rather than random density.

## Local Proof Attempts
The larger search found no pairwise-coprime additive patterns in any of the three regimes. That is consistent with strong local obstructions, but a proof would need a structural argument showing that the high prime-adic divisibility requirements and pairwise-coprimality cannot coexist with these additive identities.
