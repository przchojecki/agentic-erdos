# EP-706

## Problem Statement
Let $L(r)$ be such that if $G$ is a graph formed by taking a finite set of points $P$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and some set $A\subset (0,\infty)$ of size $r$, where the vertex set is $P$ and there is an edge between two points if and only if their distance is a member of $A$, then $\chi(G)\leq L(r)$.

Estimate $L(r)$. In particular, is it true that $L(r)\leq r^{O(1)}$?

## Forum Comments
- 17:12 on 03 May 2026 - Quanyu Tang:
  I would like to add a few references and partial results which seem directly relevant to this problem.
  
  Let \(\overline{\chi}(\mathbb R^2;m)=\max_{|A|=m}\chi(\mathbb R^2,A)\), where two points of \(\mathbb R^2\) are adjacent if their Euclidean distance belongs to \(A\). This is the corresponding infinite-graph formulation of the quantity \(L(m)\) in this problem; equivalently, by the de Bruijn--Erdős compactness theorem for graph colourings [dBE51 (https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1951-01.pdf), Theorem 1], it gives the same finite-subgraph supremum.
  
  First, Eric Naslund discussed this problem in an answer to a MathOverflow question of Matthew Kahle; see [MO20] (https://mathoverflow.net/questions/375219/updated-bounds-or-references-for-an-old-erd%C5%91s-problem-coloring-the-plane-with). The usual lattice construction gives a lower bound \(\overline{\chi}(\mathbb R^2;m) \gg m\sqrt{\log m}\). On the other hand, the elementary product-colouring argument gives the general upper bound \(\overline{\chi}(\mathbb R^2;m)\le 7^m\). A related recent paper is Naslund's [Na23] (https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.12197) (also available on arXiv: [Na23-arXiv] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12312)).
  
  For small \(m\), there is a concrete improvement when \(m=2\). Exoo and Ismailescu [EI19] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13177) proved that \(\chi(\mathbb R^2,\{1,2\})\ge 6\). Equivalently, in the notation of this problem, \(L(2)\ge 6\). Parts later gave a smaller and simpler 6-chromatic two-distance construction for a suitable pair of forbidden distances, based on a graph with only 16 vertices; see [Pa20] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12656).

  [Na23] (https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.12197) Eric Naslund, ''The chromatic number of \(\mathbb R^n\) with multiple forbidden distances'', Mathematika 69 (2023), 692--718.
  [EI19] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13177) Geoffrey Exoo and Dan Ismailescu, ''A 6-chromatic two-distance graph in the plane'', arXiv:1909.13177, 2019.

## Local Computation
- Built standalone finite proxy experiments:
 - sample planar point sets,
 - choose \(r\) target distances,
 - connect near those distances,
 - compute greedy coloring upper bound and clique-based lower bound.
- This is heuristic finite evidence, not a theorem for \(L(r)\).

- Deep run: \(r=1\) to \(6\), 40 trials each, 160 points per trial.
- Mean greedy upper bounds increased with \(r\) (about 4.4, 5.7, 7.5, 9.0, 10.7, 12.2).
- Max observed upper bounds reached 15 at \(r=6\), suggesting clear growth in these finite models.

## Local Proof Attempts
The current approach is purely finite-model: sample planar point sets, impose \(r\) allowed distances approximately, and compare coloring upper bounds with clique lower bounds. Those experiments show growth with \(r\) in the proxy model, but they do not translate into rigorous bounds for the exact extremal quantity \(L(r)\). The polynomial-growth question remains open.
